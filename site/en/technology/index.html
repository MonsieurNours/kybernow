Measured on 19 October 2026 with the Kyber 0.29 engine and its own command-line client, from a PC with an RTX 3080 Ti (4K display, H.264) to a MacBook Pro M4 Max over a local network: 3,263 frames, displayed at 59 per second. Each step is a median; the total is the median of the whole journey, and 95% of frames took less than 17.4 ms. The same day, with KyberNow's virtual display at 3840 × 2160 and 160 Hz, the median was 8.3 ms (stream received at 152 frames per second).

Transport

QUIC keeps video, sound and input apart.

Kyber sends video, sound and your input (keyboard, mouse, gamepad) over QUIC (RFC 9000), the IETF-standardised protocol that HTTP/3 also runs on. Each has its own channel, so if a video packet goes missing, sound and input keep arriving. If the connection drops, the app on your device reconnects by itself, with ten attempts one second apart, and the PC holds your session for 20 seconds.

Encryption

QUIC has TLS 1.3 built in, the encryption used by HTTPS websites, so video, sound and your keystrokes are always encrypted, even at home. In Safari, on iPhone and in Firefox, the web version uses a WebSocket connection instead, which is encrypted too.

Separate channels

On a single connection such as TCP, one lost packet holds up everything behind it until it is sent again, which is known as head-of-line blocking. With QUIC, a loss only delays its own channel.

Repairing losses

In the Game and Max profiles, video is sent with spare data that rebuilds lost packets on arrival (forward error correction, or FEC). Sound is always sent this way. In the Desktop profile, lost video packets are sent again. After a loss, the native apps immediately ask for a complete frame; the web version does not.

Two ports

The installer picks two ports at random between 20000 and 44999, and you can change them: one for the stream (TCP and UDP), the other for KyberNow Admin, the settings page (TCP). Discovery on the local network uses Bonjour (UDP 5353). Away from home, use a VPN such as Tailscale or WireGuard.

The engine

KyberNow is built on the Kyber engine.

Kyber is a free, open-source streaming engine (AGPL-3.0 licence), created by VLC developers, written in Rust and built on FFmpeg and QUIC. KyberNow uses it to capture, compress and carry video and sound, and adds the installation, the settings and the apps for your devices.

What Kyber does

  • Capturing video and sound, and compressing the video on an NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics card, or on the processor if needed
  • Encrypted transport over QUIC, with error correction (FEC)
  • Sending keyboard, mouse and gamepad input
  • A development kit (SDK) for writing client apps

What KyberNow adds

  • KyberNow Station: the installer (engine, virtual display, gamepad driver, firewall), settings in the browser with KyberNow Admin, and updates
  • A virtual display created for each session, with sound switched to Steam's virtual speakers when Steam is installed
  • KyberNow Link: native apps for Mac, Windows, Apple TV, iPhone and iPad, Android and Android TV, LG TVs, Linux, Steam Deck and Raspberry Pi (with KyberNow Link OS), which decode and display the video themselves, plus a web version served by the Station
  • Engine fixes, published and documented

A community, non-commercial project

KyberNow is free and open source, and all of its code is public. It needs no online account or subscription, shows no ads and sends no telemetry. It is developed by Les Lignes Claires. KyberNow is not affiliated with Kyber: it is neither published nor endorsed by the Kyber team.

Features

What each feature does.

A display that matches your device

For each session, the Station creates a virtual display on the PC at your device's resolution and refresh rate, turns off the PC's own screens, then turns them back on at the end. There is nothing to set up. The virtual display accepts up to 7680 × 4320 and 500 Hz; the highest mode verified so far is 3840 × 2160 at 160 Hz.

1920 × 10802560 × 14403840 × 2160

Stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 sound

The format depends on the device: 5.1 at most on an LG TV, stereo in the browser. Sound is compressed with Opus (up to 768 kbit/s in 7.1), in 10 ms packets, with a buffer of about twenty milliseconds. If Steam is installed, sound moves from the PC's speakers to Steam's virtual speakers; otherwise it also plays on the PC.

Variable refresh rate (VRR)

Each frame is shown as soon as it is decoded, and a variable refresh rate screen refreshes as each one arrives. This works with the Mac, Windows, Linux and Android phone apps (the Steam Deck has not been tried yet). The TV apps have no access to it.

Adding a device

The PC appears on your local network by itself. Otherwise, the QR code or link shown in Admin gives its address and its certificate fingerprint, which the app checks. A six-digit code, accepted in Admin, gives each device its own access, which you can revoke.

In a browser

The Station also serves a web version of Link. Open its address in Chrome or Edge on a computer, tablet or phone, and use a trackpad or your finger. In Safari, on iPhone and in Firefox, it goes through WebSocket, which adds a little latency. Sound is in stereo.

Updates

The Station updates in one click from Admin. The Mac, Windows and Android apps update themselves; the Linux, Raspberry Pi and LG TV apps tell you when a new version is out. On Apple TV, iPhone and iPad, you reinstall the app from a Mac. Every package is verified before it is installed.

Three profiles

Lowest latency

The picture follows your screen's refresh rate, in 4:2:0 colour, which is quicker to compress and decompress. Error correction rebuilds lost packets, and a damaged frame is skipped rather than waited for.

  • Screen refresh rate
  • 4:2:0
  • Error correction

In 4:4:4, every pixel keeps its own colour and text stays sharp; in 4:2:0, four pixels share their colour, which lightens the stream. Profiles adapt to what your device can decode: if it cannot decode 4:4:4, they switch to 4:2:0 with a higher bitrate, and without HEVC, to H.264.

Getting started

Install KyberNow in three steps.

Install KyberNow Station on the PC you want to share, connect your device with the KyberNow Link app, then start your first session. The guide covers each step, screen by screen. KyberNow is free and needs no online account.
