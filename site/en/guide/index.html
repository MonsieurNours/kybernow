Guide and FAQ

Installing KyberNow, step by step.

You install KyberNow Station on the Windows PC you want to share, then the KyberNow Link app on each of your devices. Allow three minutes for the PC, and between one minute and a quarter of an hour per device, depending on the model.

  1. 1Install KyberNow Station
  2. 2Connect a device
  3. 3Play or work

Before you start

The PC you share

  • Windows 10 or 11, and an account with administrator rights
  • An NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics card, external ones (eGPU) included. Without one, the processor compresses the picture, and the installer warns you
  • Microsoft Edge WebView2, already on Windows 11 and most Windows 10 PCs. If it is missing, the installer tells you and opens Microsoft's download page
  • Steam, so that the sound goes to your devices. Without Steam, the sound also plays through the PC's speakers

Your devices

  • A Mac with Apple silicon, on macOS 14 or later
  • A PC on Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11, x64 or ARM64 (ARM64 PCs, such as those with Snapdragon chips, need Windows 11)
  • An Apple TV 4K from 2021 or later, on tvOS 18 or later, and a Mac with Xcode to install the app
  • An iPhone or iPad on iOS 18 or iPadOS 18 or later, and a Mac with Xcode to install the app
  • A phone, tablet or TV on Android 11 or later (Android TV, Google TV, NVIDIA Shield)
  • An LG TV on webOS 5 or later (2020 models onwards), and a computer to install the app in developer mode
  • A Linux PC with Flatpak (x86_64 or ARM), a Steam Deck, or a Steam Machine or PC running SteamOS
  • A Raspberry Pi 3, 4 or 5, with a microSD card of at least 8 GB for KyberNow Link OS
  • Otherwise, a recent browser on any device: Chrome or Edge ideally, Safari or Firefox also work
  • To add it, the device must be on the same network as the PC, or linked to it by a VPN. Away from home, it can then go through KyberNow's relay

An Ethernet cable between the PC and your router avoids Wi-Fi micro-dropouts. For the device, use a cable too when you can, or else 5 GHz or 6 GHz Wi-Fi close to the router.

Step 1

Install KyberNow Station

On the PC you want to share, download KyberNow-Station-Setup.exe and open it. Windows asks for administrator permission, because the installer adds drivers and services. That prompt says “Unknown publisher”: KyberNow's Windows files do not yet carry an Authenticode signature, which tells Windows who published a program. If Windows SmartScreen blocks the file, click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. Nothing changes on the PC until you click “Install”.

  1. Welcome

    The installer looks over the PC: graphics card and driver, local network, network ports, main display, power mode and remote sound. An orange dot flags a recommendation; it does not stop the installation.

    It picks two free network ports at random, between 20000 and 44999, steering clear of those used by Windows, Steam and Sunshine. A port is a numbered entrance on the PC: your devices use one for the stream (picture, sound and controls) and the other for KyberNow Admin. “Change” lets you pick others. If the PC's network is set to “Public” in Windows, the installer switches it to “Private”, otherwise your devices would not see the PC.

  2. Your account

    Choose a username and a password of at least 10 characters. This account stays on the PC: there is no online account to create. You use it to open Admin and to connect your devices; you can create more accounts later in Admin, under Users. The password is not stored as such, only a hash that is used to check it.

    If the Station is already installed, this screen starts with a question. “Keep my settings” replaces only the programs and drivers, and your devices stay connected. “Start from scratch” creates a new account, and every device will need connecting again.

  3. Options

    The options are already chosen for you, and you can change everything later in Admin. Virtual display: the PC creates a display at each device's resolution and refresh rate. Gamepads: the ViGEmBus driver makes your device's controller appear to the PC as a real one, an Xbox 360 controller by default. Remote sound: Steam's virtual speakers send the sound to the device, up to 7.1. Start with the computer: the Station is ready even before you sign in to Windows. Maximum performance: the PC does not slow down during a session, which keeps latency steady. Then click “Install”.

    The virtual display driver (SudoVDA, open-source software) is not signed by Microsoft, so the installer adds its certificate to Windows' trusted authorities, and removes it if you uninstall the Station.

  4. Installation

    It takes less than a minute. The installer also opens Windows Firewall, for your private network only: the two ports, plus the one that lets your devices find the PC on their own (5353). The final screen shows the PC's address, a QR code to open Admin on your phone, and the address to open in a browser to use the PC without an app. If the controllers need it, “Restart now” restarts the PC; they will only work after that.

The first time you open Admin, your browser shows a security warning. That is expected: the Station encrypts the connection with a certificate it created itself, which the browser does not know. Accept it to carry on. The KyberNow Link apps recognise this certificate by its fingerprint.

A KyberNow Station icon appears next to the Windows clock. Its dot is green while the Station is running and purple during a session. A click opens a panel with the Station's status and a way into Admin; a right-click opens a menu with the same actions. Admin, the Station's control panel in your browser, has five sections: Overview, Picture and sound, Link devices, Users and System.

Step 2

Connect a device

On each device, you install the KyberNow Link app, then add your PC to it. On the same network, the app finds the PC by itself; otherwise, you enter its address. To give it access, you either accept in Admin the six-digit code the app shows, or enter the account created during installation, depending on the app. Pick your device:

With the Mac app

  1. Download KyberNow-Link-macOS.zip, open it and drag KyberNow Link into your Applications folder. The app is signed and notarised by Apple.

  2. Open it, then choose “Add a computer”:

    • On this network: your PC shows up on its own.
    • By address: the PC's IP address or name, and the Admin port, shown at the end of the installation and in Admin. This is the one to use over a VPN.
    • QR code or link: in Admin, under Link devices, click “Add a device”, then hold the QR code up to the Mac's camera or paste the link. It contains the PC's address and its certificate fingerprint, never a password.

  3. The app asks “Is this really your computer?” If the PC was found on the network or through the link, the app has already checked its fingerprint, a string of characters that identifies it. If you added it by address, compare the first four groups with the ones Admin shows (Link devices › Add a device).

  4. Enter the username and password you chose during installation; the Mac keeps them in its Keychain. Then click “Connect” and the session opens.

With the Windows app

The Windows app is mainly for a laptop or a more modest PC, to reach your main PC. The graphics card decodes the picture when it can, otherwise the processor does. With this app, we measured about 10 ms of latency on an x64 PC.

  1. Download KyberNow-Link-Setup.exe and open it. The same file covers x64 and ARM64 PCs and installs the right version by itself. The app installs for your Windows account only, with no administrator rights.

  2. Open KyberNow Link, then choose “Add a computer”:

    • On the network: your PC shows up on its own.
    • With the pairing link: in Admin, under Link devices, “Add a device” shows a kybernow:// link. Click it on this PC or paste it into the app: the app opens on the add screen, with the PC and its fingerprint already checked.
    • By address: the PC's IP address or name, and the Admin port. This is the one to use over a VPN. Then compare the first four groups of the fingerprint with the ones Admin shows.

  3. Give it access. With a code: the app shows a six-digit code, which you accept in Admin, under Link devices. Nothing to type: this PC gets its own access, which you can revoke later. With an account: the username and password you chose during installation, which Windows keeps in Credential Manager. Then click “Connect” and the session opens.

During the beta, the installer and the app carry no Authenticode signature: on first launch, Windows SmartScreen may show a warning. Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”.

Your computers also appear when you right-click the KyberNow Link icon in the taskbar: one click starts the session.

On Linux

The Linux app runs on Linux PCs (GNOME, KDE or another desktop) with an x86_64 or ARM processor. You use it with a mouse and keyboard, with a session bar at the top of the screen. It decodes the picture with the graphics card when it can (VA-API), otherwise with the processor. It supports variable refresh rate on screens that have it, stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 sound, and four controllers with rumble. It comes as a Flatpak package, an app format that works across most distributions and installs for you alone, without administrator rights. So far, we have only tried it in a virtual machine (Ubuntu with GNOME). For the Steam Deck, see On the Steam Deck and SteamOS.

  1. Download the package for your machine: KyberNow-Link-Linux-x86_64.flatpak for most PCs, KyberNow-Link-Linux-aarch64.flatpak for an ARM machine.

  2. Double-click the file: GNOME Software or KDE Discover installs it. You can also use a terminal, opened in the file's folder:

    flatpak install --user KyberNow-Link-Linux-x86_64.flatpak

    The app needs the shared base that Flatpak apps run on, the org.freedesktop.Platform 25.08 runtime. If it is missing, Flatpak offers to download it from Flathub: accept.

  3. Open KyberNow Link from the applications menu, then add your PC:

    • On the network: your PC shows up on its own. The app shows a six-digit code: accept it in Admin, under Link devices. Nothing to type.
    • By address: the PC's IP address or name, and the Admin port. This is the one to use over a VPN. Then compare the first four groups of the fingerprint with the ones Admin shows.

Flatpak is already installed on most distributions (otherwise: flatpak.org/setup). If it does not know the Flathub repository yet, add it and install the runtime by hand:

flatpak remote-add --user --if-not-exists flathub https://dl.flathub.org/repo/flathub.flatpakrepo
flatpak install --user flathub org.freedesktop.Platform//25.08

Recommended settings: for crisp text on the desktop, turn on Computer settings › Picture › 4:4:4 colour, which keeps colour at full resolution. It is off by default and only works in HEVC; it needs more bitrate, and the processor takes over decoding if the graphics card cannot. Leave Display on Auto: with a variable refresh rate screen (FreeSync, VRR), each frame appears as soon as it is decoded, and the screen follows the stream's pace.

If something goes wrong, the app's log is in ~/.var/app/fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow/config/kybernow/kybernow.log. Full procedure: installing KyberNow Link on Linux and the Steam Deck.

On Apple TV

The app is not on the App Store yet. It installs from a Mac with Xcode, Apple's free tool for building apps, and is signed with your own Apple account. Allow ten minutes the first time, one minute after that. You need an Apple TV 4K from 2021 or later, up to date, and a Mac on the same network with Xcode (from the Mac App Store; open it once so that it finishes installing).

  1. On the Mac, open Terminal. Install XcodeGen with Homebrew, then download the KyberNow code:

    brew install xcodegen
git clone https://github.com/MonsieurNours/kybernow.git
cd kybernow

  2. In Xcode › Settings › Accounts, add your Apple account. Note its team ID, ten characters such as ABCDE12345: Xcode shows it when you choose the team that signs an app. With a free account, it is your “(Personal Team)”.

  3. On the Apple TV, open Settings › Remotes and Devices › Remote App and Devices and leave that screen open. On the Mac, in Xcode, open Window › Devices and Simulators, choose the Apple TV, click Pair and type the code shown on the TV. The first time, Xcode takes a few minutes to prepare the Apple TV.

  4. Wake the Apple TV, then run the installation from the kybernow folder. If it is asleep, it accepts the app but refuses to open it.

    apps/tvos/scripts/sideload.sh --team ABCDE12345

    With a paid account (Apple Developer Program, €99 a year), add --full: you get the row of your computers at the top of the Apple TV home screen (Top Shelf), a profile for each member of the household, and computers added from your Mac through iCloud.

  5. Open KyberNow on the Apple TV, allow local network access, then add your PC:

    • From your Mac: with --full only. It lists the computers of a Mac app built with your own team, not those of the Mac app downloaded from this site.
    • On the network: your PC shows up on its own; type the account, using your iPhone's keyboard if you like.
    • On-screen code: the Apple TV shows a six-digit code; accept it in Admin, under Link devices. Nothing to type. This code only works on the local network: over a VPN, choose Address.
    • Address: the PC's IP address or name and the Admin port, then the account.

With a free Apple account, the app stops opening after 7 days. Run the same command again to renew it: your computers and settings stay. With a paid account, it lasts a year.

Full procedure and troubleshooting: installing KyberNow Link on your Apple TV.

On iPhone and iPad

As with the Apple TV, the app is not on the App Store or TestFlight yet: it installs from a Mac with Xcode, signed with your own Apple account. You need an iPhone or iPad on iOS 18 or iPadOS 18 or later, and a cable to connect it to the Mac the first time. Without a Mac, you can use KyberNow Link in Safari: see In a browser.

  1. On the Mac, open Terminal. Install XcodeGen with Homebrew, then download the KyberNow code:

    brew install xcodegen
git clone https://github.com/MonsieurNours/kybernow.git
cd kybernow

  2. In Xcode › Settings › Accounts, add your Apple account and note its team ID (ten characters such as ABCDE12345; with a free account, your “(Personal Team)”).

  3. Connect the iPhone or iPad to the Mac, unlock it and tap Trust This Computer. Then turn on Developer Mode: Settings › Privacy & Security › Developer Mode (the device restarts). Without it, the device refuses apps that do not come from the App Store.

  4. Run the installation from the kybernow folder:

    apps/ios/scripts/sideload.sh --team ABCDE12345

    With a paid account (Apple Developer Program), add --full to add computers from your Mac through iCloud, with the shared Keychain.

  5. With a free account, first allow the app: Settings › General › VPN & Device Management, tap your account under “Developer App”, then Trust. Open KyberNow, allow local network access, then add your PC:

    • From your Mac: with --full only, for the computers of a Mac app built with your own team, as on the Apple TV.
    • On the network: your PC shows up on its own; type its account.
    • Code or QR code: the app shows a six-digit code to accept in Admin, under Link devices. It can also use its camera to read the QR code Admin shows in the same place, under “Add a device”.
    • Address: the PC's IP address or name and the Admin port. This is the one to use over a VPN.

With a free Apple account, the app stops opening after 7 days: run the same command again to renew it. A free account also allows at most three apps installed this way.

Full procedure and troubleshooting: installing KyberNow Link on your iPhone or iPad.

On Android

The same app works on phones, tablets and TVs (Android TV, Google TV, NVIDIA Shield), from Android 11. On a TV, it goes up to 4K at 60 Hz, with 7.1 sound through an HDMI receiver, Bluetooth controllers and the remote. It is not on the Play Store yet: you install it from its file, KyberNow-Link-Android.apk.

On a phone or tablet

  1. On the device, open the KyberNow-Link-Android.apk link, then the downloaded file.

  2. Android asks you to allow your browser to install apps: allow it, go back, then tap Install.

On a TV: the simplest way is through a computer on the same network, with adb, Android's developer tool. Allow five minutes the first time.

  1. On the TV, turn on developer options: Settings › Device Preferences › About (on Google TV: Settings › System › About), then click Build seven times. In Developer options, turn on Network debugging (or ADB debugging) and note the TV's address.

  2. On the computer, download KyberNow-Link-Android.apk and install adb: on a Mac with Homebrew, brew install android-platform-tools; elsewhere, Google's SDK Platform-Tools.

  3. Connect the computer to the TV, then install the app, from the folder of the downloaded file:

    adb connect 192.168.1.30
adb install KyberNow-Link-Android.apk

    Replace the address with the TV's. On the first adb connect, the TV asks you to allow debugging: tick “Always allow” and confirm. You can turn debugging off afterwards.

Then, on every device: open KyberNow and add your PC.

  • On the network: your PC shows up on its own.
  • On-screen code: the app shows a six-digit code; accept it in Admin, under Link devices. Nothing to type.
  • QR code, on a phone or tablet: the one Admin shows under Link devices, in “Add a device”.
  • Address: the PC's IP address or name and the Admin port. This is the one to use over a VPN.

On an LG TV

The app runs on LG TVs on webOS 5 or later (2020 models onwards). It goes up to 4K at 120 Hz, with four Bluetooth controllers and their rumble, the Magic Remote as a mouse, and 5.1 Dolby sound to a receiver. It is not in the LG Content Store: it installs in developer mode, which LG opens to anyone with a free account, without modifying the TV. Allow a quarter of an hour the first time, from a computer on the same network.

  1. On the computer, create a free account at webostv.developer.lge.com (Sign In › Create Account), then confirm your email address.

  2. On the TV, install the Developer Mode app from the LG Content Store, sign in with that account and turn on Dev Mode Status: the TV restarts. Open Developer Mode again, turn on Key Server, then note the TV's IP address and its Passphrase.

  3. On the computer, install webOS Dev Manager (Windows, Mac or Linux). Add the TV (Add device › Developer mode, with the address and passphrase), then, in the Apps tab, install KyberNow-Link-webOS.ipk.

  4. Open KyberNow Link from the TV's home screen. Your PC shows up on its own if it is on the same network: select it, then accept the six-digit code it displays in Admin, under Link devices. Otherwise, choose Add › With address and port, type the PC's address and the Admin port, then compare the fingerprint with the one Admin shows.

From the command line, with LG's tools (npm install -g @webos-tools/cli):

ares-setup-device --add tv -i "host=192.168.1.50" -i "port=9922" -i "username=prisoner"
ares-novacom --device tv --getkey
ares-install --device tv KyberNow-Link-webOS.ipk

Developer mode has a time limit, shown in the Developer Mode app. When it reaches zero, the TV leaves developer mode and apps installed this way disappear. The Extend Session button resets it to the maximum: remember to press it now and then.

Recommended settings: during the session, choose the Game Optimizer picture mode (Magic Remote settings button › Picture › Mode); without it, the TV adds its own picture processing, and latency with it. Pair your controllers over Bluetooth in the TV's settings. For 5.1 sound to an eARC receiver: in the app, computer settings › Sound › 5.1 Dolby, and on the TV, Sound › Digital Sound Output › Pass-Through.

On the Steam Deck and SteamOS

On the Steam Deck, you use the Linux app, launched from Gaming Mode. It sets itself up for the controller: on the Deck's screen, a handheld interface with a button legend; on a TV, the living room interface, readable from across the room, with its “Ready to play” column (controllers, sound, display, decoders). Allow ten minutes the first time.

  1. Switch to Desktop Mode: Steam button › Power › Switch to Desktop.

  2. With Firefox or another browser, download KyberNow-Link-Linux-x86_64.flatpak.

  3. In your Downloads folder, double-click the file: Discover installs it. You can also open Konsole and type:

    flatpak install --user ~/Downloads/KyberNow-Link-Linux-x86_64.flatpak

  4. Add the app to Steam: open Steam in Desktop Mode, Games › Add a Non-Steam Game, tick KyberNow Link, then Add Selected Programs.

  5. Go back to Gaming Mode (the “Return to Gaming Mode” icon on the desktop). KyberNow Link is in your library, under the Non-Steam tab.

  6. On KyberNow Link's page, controller icon › Layout, pick the Gamepad with Mouse Trackpad template: the sticks and buttons go to the PC's controller, the right trackpad moves the mouse and its clicks click.

  7. Launch KyberNow Link and add your PC: it shows up on its own if it is on the same network, and you accept the six-digit code it displays in Admin, under Link devices. To enter an address, use the on-screen keypad or Steam's keyboard.

We have not yet been able to try the app on a real Steam Deck. Tell us what works and what does not on GitHub or Discord.

In the living room: a Steam Machine, a PC running SteamOS or Bazzite plugged into the TV, or the Deck on its dock. Installation is the same. The app switches to the living room interface as soon as the screen is larger than 1280 × 800 in Gaming Mode, and App settings › Interface lets you force Desktop, Handheld or Living room. If the TV supports variable refresh rate (VRR), the app shows each frame as soon as it is decoded and the TV follows the stream's pace. The Steam Deck's own screen does not support it.

Recommended settings: the defaults, that is the Game profile, the screen set to “Like this screen” (1280 × 800 on the Deck, at its refresh rate) and Display on Auto. Lighter on battery, when turned on, limits the unplugged Deck to 60 frames per second and 20 Mbit/s. For the network, 5 GHz or 6 GHz Wi-Fi close to the router, or an Ethernet cable on the dock.

On a Raspberry Pi

You have two options. KyberNow Link OS is a complete system to copy onto the microSD card: the Pi does nothing else and starts up like a console, on a TV with a controller, or at a desk with a keyboard and mouse. The app alone, as a .deb package, installs on the 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS you already use. Both suit the Pi 3, 4 and 5 (Pi 400, Pi 500 and Compute Module included). The Pi 4 and 5 decode the picture with their own chip, in HEVC, up to 4K at 60 Hz; the Pi 3 does so in H.264. Over HDMI, expect 4K at 60 Hz at most, or 1080p at 120 Hz if the TV allows it, without variable refresh rate. Neither the app nor Link OS has run on a real board yet.

KyberNow Link OS, on the microSD card

  1. Download KyberNow-Link-OS.img.xz. With Raspberry Pi Imager, the official tool for preparing cards, copy it onto a microSD card of at least 8 GB (or an SSD): Choose OS › Use custom, then the file. Alternatively, paste https://www.kybernow.app/updates/rpi-imager.json into Imager › Settings › Custom repository, and “KyberNow Link OS” appears in the list. Do not customise anything in Imager: everything is set at first start-up.

  2. Connect the Pi to the TV over HDMI (on a Pi 4 or 5, the HDMI 0 socket, the one nearest the power input), to your router with an Ethernet cable if you can, then to the power supply. The KyberNow mark appears, then lights up with a sound.

  3. At first start-up, put your controller into pairing mode (Xbox: the pairing button near the USB port; DualSense: Create + PS; Switch Pro: SYNC): the Pi accepts it without a code. A USB controller or a keyboard works too, as does the TV remote if the TV supports HDMI-CEC (the feature that lets a TV remote operate devices connected over HDMI). Then choose the language, the network (Ethernet is detected automatically; otherwise pick your Wi-Fi and type its password), the use (Living room or Desktop), then your PC: accept the six-digit code it displays in Admin, under Link devices.

  4. From then on, ☰ on the home screen opens the Device settings (network, controllers, screen and HDMI-CEC, sound, update, language, advanced settings). Start held for three seconds switches between Living room and Desktop. Guide (or PS) puts the Pi on standby, and any button wakes it.

Updating the OS: Device settings › Update. The new version downloads, as soon as it is out if “Automatic” is ticked. The card has two slots for the system: “Restart on X” starts the new version in the free one. If it does not get as far as the home screen, the Pi goes back to the previous version by itself. Your computers and settings are kept separately and stay. This kind of update needs a Pi 4 or 5; on a Pi 3, reinstall the card with the new image.

The app alone, on Raspberry Pi OS

  1. Download KyberNow-Link-RaspberryPi-arm64.deb (for 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS, Debian 13).

  2. Install it with a double-click in the file manager, or in a terminal:

    sudo apt install ./KyberNow-Link-RaspberryPi-arm64.deb

    KyberNow Link appears in the menu, under Games. When a new version comes out, the app tells you: install the new file the same way.

  3. On a TV, with a controller: App settings › Interface › Living room gives you the interface readable from across the room (A confirms, B goes back, Y opens settings, X adds a computer; L1 + R1 + Start + Select open the session menu).

First version: the app and the image are built and tested in a virtual machine, with the image booted under emulation, but nothing has run on a real board yet. If you try it, tell us what works and what does not on GitHub or Discord.

In a browser

On a device on the same network, or linked to the PC by a VPN, open Admin's address followed by /link in the browser, for example https://192.168.1.20:31415/link. The exact address is shown at the end of the installation, and in Admin (Overview › Open in the browser). Accept the certificate warning, then sign in with the account created during installation. Chrome and Edge give the lowest latency; in Safari, on iPhone and in Firefox, the picture goes through Admin, with a little more latency.

The browser works well as a stopgap, for instance to work from a tablet or a borrowed computer. For gaming, use an app: the browser only has stereo sound, and it does not ask for the picture again when part of it is lost on the way.

Step 3

During a session

When you connect, the PC creates a virtual display at your device's resolution and refresh rate, turns off its own displays and sends the sound to the device (if Steam is installed on the PC). At the end, everything goes back to how it was: displays and sound output. Windows is not locked.

The profiles

GameFor playing: the lowest latency. Spare data travels with the picture and fills in small losses, without waiting for anything to be resent.
DesktopFor working: sharp text, thanks to full-resolution colour (4:4:4). Lost data is sent again, so nothing is missing from the picture.
MaxLow latency, sharp text and a high bitrate, with the Game profile's spare data. For a wired network.

Changing a setting switches the profile to “Custom”. By default, the mouse is in Auto mode: it behaves as on a desktop, and switches to game mode when a game fills the PC's screen. It then sends raw movements, the way games expect them.

Share with someone

KyberNow Share lets up to four guests onto your PC: they see the screen and hear the sound, and you decide what they can do. It needs KyberNow Station and the KyberNow Link app on version 0.8.0 or later; otherwise the app tells you.

Open a share

  1. From your session: in the session bar, choose “Share”. The share shows your session's main display, with the sound.

  2. From the PC itself, without a session: in the KyberNow Station icon's menu next to the clock, or on Admin's home page, choose “Share a screen…”, then the screen to show. You keep using the PC: no virtual display is created and your screens stay on.

  3. A panel shows the six-digit code, a link (“Copy link”), a QR code and the list of guests. The code holds as long as the share is open; “New code” changes it, and guests already in stay. “Stop sharing” makes everyone leave, and closing your session also stops the share.

Join a share

  1. In the KyberNow Link app, on the home screen, next to “Add a computer”, choose “Join with a code” and type the six digits. You can also open the link, or scan the QR code with the camera (iPhone, iPad, Android).

  2. In a browser, open the link: it opens as a guest, without Admin's password.

  3. Give your first name, choose the picture quality, then “Join”. A guest is not added to the device's list of computers: nothing is kept after the share, except their first name.

What a guest can do

SpectatorA guest arrives as one: they see the screen and hear the sound, and send nothing to your PC.
Give controllersTheir controllers become Windows' next controller, a virtual controller of their own. “Take back controllers” removes them.
Give keyboard and mouseThey can type and move the pointer. “Take back keyboard and mouse” removes them. The clipboard is never shared with a guest.
RemoveThey leave the share. They can come back with the same code, as long as you have not asked for a “New code”.

The two rights are independent: you can give the controllers without the keyboard or the mouse, and the other way round. The Station enforces these rights: even if a guest's device tried to send something else, nothing reaches the PC. Four guests at most: beyond that, the code is refused.

Each guest's picture

Each guest picks the “Picture quality” on the “Join” screen and in their bar: Auto (suggested, from their device's display and what its decoder can play), Same as host, 4K · 60 Hz, 1080p · 60 Hz or 720p · 30 Hz. The host always comes first: a guest's picture is never above yours, and nothing they do changes your screen or your picture. A guest who follows your picture shares it; the others get a picture prepared for them by the PC, two at most beside yours (only one with an Intel card). When there is no room left, the guest gets the closest open picture, a lighter one, and a message says so. A guest who falls behind drops to a lighter picture on their own, without you seeing anything. Your panel shows each guest's quality.

In a share started from the PC, the first guest sets the format and bitrate of the shared picture.

When the guest is away from your home

The same code, the same link and the same QR code work from anywhere: the guest goes through KyberNow's relay (see Away from home). You accept each guest who comes from the internet: their request shows in the panel's “Requests” section, with “Accept” and “Decline”. On the same network, there is nothing to accept. Keep the relay set in Admin (Link devices › “Away from home” › “Relay”, KyberNow's public relay by default): when it does not answer, the panel says “Not reachable away from home yet.”.

Away from home

At home, the picture travels straight from your PC to your device, without going through a server. Away from home, at a hotel or a friend's, your devices reach the PC through KyberNow's relay, or through a VPN. Either way, the PC must stay switched on.

Through KyberNow's relay

A relay is a server on the internet that passes the connection between your device and your PC. KyberNow's public relay, share.kybernow.app, is open to everyone; you can also install your own. Nothing to open on your router: the Station itself opens the connection to the relay, and only while a share is open or access away from home is switched on.

  1. First add the device to your PC, at home (step 2): adding a new device only works on the home network.

  2. In Admin, under Link devices, in the “Away from home” section, switch on “Access away from home”. It is off by default. “Relay reached” shows once the Station has reached the relay.

  3. Away from home, open the KyberNow Link app as usual: your PC is marked “Away from home” instead of “Online”, and the session carries the “Via the relay” mention.

Through the relay, the picture takes a detour: expect a little more latency than a direct connection, depending on your distance from the relay. If the network you are on does not let UDP through (some hotels, company networks), the connection falls back to TCP and the app shows “Via the relay (TCP)”: fine for watching, less good for playing. Away from home, an app can neither restart nor update the Station: that is done on site or in Admin. After too many failed attempts, access through the relay is blocked for ten minutes. Access away from home is for the KyberNow Link apps; in a browser, the relay only serves to join a share.

What goes through the relay

In the KyberNow Link apps, the picture, the sound and your keystrokes stay encrypted end to end: they cross the relay without it being able to read them, and the app checks it is really talking to your PC thanks to the fingerprint of its certificate. The relay sees IP addresses, bitrates and share codes. KyberNow's, run by Les Lignes Claires in France, keeps IP addresses in its logs for a few days, never the codes; its privacy page gives the details.

The browser is the exception: the relay decrypts what passes to forward it, and could therefore read it. If that matters to you, use an app, or your own relay.

Through a VPN

A VPN is a private, encrypted network between your devices, such as Tailscale or WireGuard. It works without a relay, and the picture then travels straight from your PC to your device.

  1. Install the same VPN on the PC and on the device, signed in to the same account or joined to the same network.

  2. Over a VPN, the PC does not show up on its own in the app. Add it “By address”, using its VPN address (for example 100.x.y.z with Tailscale) and the Admin port, then give it access as you would at home. On Apple TV, iPhone and iPad, the on-screen code does not travel over the VPN: enter your account instead.

Either way, choose the Game or Desktop profile: the Max profile's bitrate is meant for a local network. If the picture stutters, lower the bitrate in the computer settings, in the app: it must stay below your connection's upload speed, which is what your router can send. On mobile data, allow about 9 GB per hour of session at 20 Mbit/s.

Do not open KyberNow's ports to the Internet on your router (“port forwarding”): neither the relay nor a VPN needs it, and your PC stays invisible to the rest of the Internet.

Install your own relay

KyberNow's public relay works for everyone, with nothing to install. Install your own if you would rather have shares and access to your PCs go through no server run by someone else, for a team, a club or on principle. This part is for someone comfortable with a Linux server.

What you need

  • A Linux machine that is always on, with Docker and docker compose
  • A domain name, for example relay.example.org, whose DNS points to your public address with two records: the name itself and its wildcard name *.relay.example.org, which the browser uses for each computer
  • An access token at the host of that DNS (Gandi by default), limited to your domain: Let's Encrypt uses it to check that the domain is yours and to issue the relay's certificate
  • A port open from outside to the machine, over TCP and UDP, 443 preferably. UDP carries the picture; without it, the relay falls back to TCP, fine for watching and less good for playing

The steps, in short

  1. At your DNS host, create the two records pointing to your public address, and the access token. Note its expiry date: the certificate no longer renews after it.

  2. On the machine, get the server/relay folder of the KyberNow repository, write the token in a file (secrets/gandi_pat), then copy .env.example to .env and fill it in: your domain name, an email address for Let's Encrypt, the ports.

  3. On your router, forward the public port (443, over TCP and UDP) to the machine's listening port, 8443 by default.

  4. Read Let's Encrypt's terms and accept them in .env, then run docker compose up -d --build. The relay starts with a test certificate; once it is obtained, you switch to the production certificate (the README gives the commands). The relay then reloads its certificate by itself.

  5. On each Station, in Admin, under Link devices, in the “Away from home” section, the “Relay” field: your relay's address, with its port if it is not 443 (relay.example.org, or relay.example.org:9443), then “Save”. “Relay reached” confirms that the Station found it.

A code typed in an app goes through the public relay and through the relays of the computers the app already knows. For a guest who does not know your relay, give them the share's link or QR code.

The full step by step (the three ports and the router, the settings, updating, logs) is in the relay's README on GitHub.

Updating

The Station: Admin checks for a new version every 6 hours. In System › Updates, “Install now” downloads it, checks that it really comes from KyberNow and installs it in about a minute; a session in progress is cut off. If the new version does not start properly, the Station goes back to the previous one by itself, and “Go back to the previous version” does the same on request. The icon next to the clock offers the update too.

The Mac app: it checks for a new version every day, then downloads and installs it by itself. To get betas too, tick “Get beta versions” in its settings (KyberNow Link › Settings…). To check right away: KyberNow Link › Check for updates…

The Windows app: it checks for a new version at launch, then every 6 hours, and offers it to you: “Install and relaunch” installs it and reopens the app, never during a session. Its settings (the gear in the title bar) offer betas too.

The Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps: they are reinstalled from the Mac. In the kybernow folder, run git pull, delete the libraries the script downloaded (rm -rf apps/tvos/Vendor for the Apple TV, rm -rf apps/ios/Vendor for the iPhone and iPad), then run the installation command again. With a free Apple account, do it before the 7 days are up.

The Android app: when a version comes out, “Version X” appears on the home screen; a button downloads and installs it, in App settings › Updates, where you can also choose betas. The first time, Android asks you to allow KyberNow to install apps.

The LG TV app: it shows “Version X available” on the home screen when a version comes out; its settings (Settings › This TV › Updates) offer betas too. In developer mode it cannot install itself: install the new KyberNow-Link-webOS.ipk from the computer, as the first time. Your computers are kept.

The Linux app and the Raspberry Pi package: the app shows “Version X available” on the home screen; its settings (App settings › Offered versions) offer betas too. Installed from a file, it does not update itself: download the new package and install it as the first time (double-click, flatpak install --user or sudo apt install). Your computers and settings stay. KyberNow Link OS updates itself from its Device settings: see On a Raspberry Pi.

For troubleshooting, if Admin no longer responds, you can also update the Station from PowerShell run as administrator:

irm https://github.com/MonsieurNours/kybernow/releases/latest/download/update.ps1 | iex

Uninstalling

The Station: Windows Settings › Apps › Installed apps › KyberNow Station › Uninstall. The uninstaller asks whether to keep your settings, which helps if you reinstall later. It then removes the services, Admin, the virtual display and its certificate, and the firewall rules. It also restores your previous power mode, and sets your network back to “Public” if it had switched it to “Private”. The ViGEmBus controller driver stays installed, because other software may use it.

The Mac: drag KyberNow Link from the Applications folder to the Bin.

The Windows PC: Windows Settings › Apps › Installed apps › KyberNow Link › Uninstall. The app offers to erase your computers, settings and passwords as well.

The Apple TV: on the Home Screen, press and hold the touch surface on the KyberNow icon, then choose to delete the app. The iPhone and iPad: touch and hold the KyberNow icon, tap “Remove App”, then “Delete App”.

Android: Settings › Apps, KyberNow, then Uninstall.

The LG TV: in webOS Dev Manager, Apps tab, or ares-install --device tv --remove fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow. Leaving developer mode removes it too.

Linux and the Steam Deck: in GNOME Software or Discover, or in a terminal; the second line also erases your computers, your settings and the log. On the Deck, also remove KyberNow Link from your Steam library.

flatpak uninstall --user fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow
rm -rf ~/.var/app/fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow

The Raspberry Pi: sudo apt remove kybernow-link removes the app; your computers, settings and log stay in ~/.config/kybernow/.

Frequently asked questions

Is KyberNow really free?

Yes. KyberNow is free, open-source software under the AGPL-3.0 licence, developed with no commercial aim. There is no subscription and no advertising, and no online account to create. The code is on GitHub.

Why is KyberNow in beta?

KyberNow has not reached version 1.0 yet. We already use it every day, but some bugs remain, and settings or features may still change from one version to the next. Some apps have not yet run on real hardware: the Steam Deck and Raspberry Pi ones, and the Linux app, tried only in a virtual machine. Sharing and the relay, both new, have not been tried on every device yet. Keep the Station and your apps up to date, and report problems on GitHub or Discord.

Can KyberNow replace Sunshine and Moonlight?

For many uses, yes. Like them, KyberNow puts your PC on your other devices, for gaming or for work, in an app or in a browser. It is built on the Kyber engine rather than the GameStream protocol. The picture is always encrypted, a virtual display at the device's size is included, and most devices connect once you accept their code in Admin, with nothing to copy out. It still lacks HDR. The details are in the comparison.

Is KyberNow a cloud gaming service?

No. Cloud gaming rents you a computer in a data centre, on a subscription. KyberNow uses your own: you get your games and software exactly as you installed them, with no subscription and no queue. At home, the picture goes straight from your PC to your device, without passing through a server; away from home, it can pass through a relay, which cannot read it in the apps. See Away from home.

Can I use KyberNow away from home?

Yes, in two ways. Through KyberNow's relay: add the device at home, then switch on “Access away from home” in Admin; nothing to open on your router. Another way is a VPN, a private, encrypted network between your devices, such as Tailscale or WireGuard: install the same VPN on the PC and on the device, then add the PC by its VPN address. The PC must stay switched on. See Away from home.

Can I share my PC with a friend?

Yes, with KyberNow Share: you open a share, and up to four guests come in with a six-digit code, a link or a QR code, in the KyberNow Link app or in a browser, at home or from afar. A guest arrives as a spectator; you give them, or not, the controllers and the keyboard and mouse. See Share with someone.

Can KyberNow's relay see my screen?

Not in the KyberNow Link apps: the picture, the sound and your keystrokes stay encrypted end to end, and the relay does not read them. It sees IP addresses, bitrates and share codes. In a browser, the relay decrypts the traffic to forward it: it could read it. You can also install your own relay. See Away from home and Install your own relay.

Can I play my PC games on a Mac or an Apple TV?

Yes. The game runs on your Windows PC, on its graphics card, and its picture appears on the Mac or the Apple TV. You play with a controller (Xbox or PlayStation), or a keyboard and mouse, with any game or launcher installed on the PC. The Mac app follows your screen's refresh rate, 120 Hz or more; the Apple TV goes up to 4K at 60 Hz.

Do I need a particular graphics card?

No. The PC compresses the picture with any NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics card, external ones (eGPU) included. Without a graphics card, the processor does the work. Only the AV1 format needs an NVIDIA RTX 40 or 50 series card. You can change the encoder in KyberNow Admin, under System › Advanced.

Which resolutions and refresh rates are supported?

Your screen's: for each session, the PC creates a virtual display at your device's resolution and refresh rate. The highest we have verified is 3840 × 2160 (4K) at 160 Hz. Some devices have their own ceiling: 4K at 60 Hz on Apple TV, the NVIDIA Shield and the Raspberry Pi, 4K at 120 Hz on LG TVs. In H.264, the picture is at most 4096 pixels wide. If the network or the graphics card struggles, the “4K at most” option in the Mac, Windows, iPhone and iPad apps lightens the stream.

How much latency should I expect?

On a local network, we measured 9.4 ms between capturing the picture on the PC and displaying it on the device, with the Kyber engine. That is a median: half of the frames arrive faster. With KyberNow's virtual display at 4K and 160 Hz, 8.3 ms; with the Windows app, about 10 ms. At home, latency depends mostly on the graphics card, the network and the device; the app shows it live during the session.

Does KyberNow support HDR?

Not yet: the Kyber 0.29 engine captures the screen in 8 bits. Keep Windows HDR turned off during sessions.

My screens turn off during a session. Is that normal?

Yes. During the session, the PC shows only the virtual display, at your device's size, and turns off its own screens. They come back on at the end. If the app drops out without warning, the Station waits 20 seconds, then removes the virtual display and turns your screens back on. If needed, Admin has a “Turn my displays back on” button, under Picture and sound.

Why does the sound still come out of the PC's speakers?

To send the sound to your devices, the Station uses Steam's virtual speakers. Install Steam on the PC, then run the KyberNow Station installer again and choose “Keep my settings”: the “Remote sound” option becomes available, and your devices stay connected.

My controller does not respond. What can I do?

First check that it is connected to your device, over Bluetooth or by cable; the Apple TV, Android, LG and Linux apps have a screen for testing controllers. On the PC, controllers go through the ViGEmBus driver, installed with the Station if the Gamepads option was ticked; after the first installation, the PC sometimes needs a restart. The PC accepts four controllers at most: a fifth waits for a free slot. Finally, while the session menu is open, the controller drives the app and the game receives nothing.

Can two devices connect at the same time?

No. The second one takes over: the first is disconnected, and the virtual display is recreated at the new device's size. To let someone else in without cutting your session, open a share: see Share with someone.

The picture stays black or freezes. What can I do?

First update the Station and the app. The apps ask for a full frame again by themselves when part of one is lost, and if nothing arrives at the start, they retry with a lighter stream. If the screen stays black, try a lower resolution or another format (H.264 or HEVC) in the computer settings, in the app. Connect the PC by Ethernet if you can, and check that no other remote play software is running on the PC. Admin's logs (System › Logs) help find the cause.

I forgot my password. What can I do?

On the PC, open the menu of the KyberNow Station icon next to the clock, choose “Reset the Admin password…” and accept the Windows prompt. The icon shows a code: Admin opens and asks for that code along with your new password. Devices already connected stay connected. A new device can connect with its six-digit code; otherwise, change the account's password in Admin, under Users, then enter it again on the devices that use it.

Does the Mac app work on an Intel Mac?

No, it needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). On an Intel Mac, use KyberNow Link in a browser, ideally Chrome or Edge.

Does KyberNow work on iPhone and iPad?

Yes, for iOS 18 and iPadOS 18 or later. It is not on the App Store or TestFlight yet: it installs from a Mac with Xcode, signed with your own Apple account. With a free account, you need to reinstall it every 7 days. See On iPhone and iPad. Without a Mac, KyberNow Link also works in Safari.

Does KyberNow work on Android?

Yes. The same app works on phones, tablets and TVs, from Android 11. It is not on the Play Store yet: you install it from its APK file. See On Android.

Does KyberNow work on an LG TV?

Yes, on LG TVs running webOS 5 or later (2020 models onwards), up to 4K at 120 Hz, with controllers, the Magic Remote and 5.1 Dolby sound to a receiver. The app is not in the LG Content Store: it installs in developer mode from a computer, with a free LG account. That mode expires, so remember to extend it now and then. See On an LG TV.

Does KyberNow work on Linux and the Steam Deck?

Yes. The same app runs on Linux (GNOME, KDE or others, x86_64 or ARM), on the Steam Deck and on SteamOS in the living room, with an interface suited to each: mouse and keyboard on a PC, the controller on the Deck and on a TV. It installs as a Flatpak package, without administrator rights. So far it has only run in a virtual machine, not on a real Steam Deck. See On Linux and On the Steam Deck and SteamOS.

Does KyberNow work on a Raspberry Pi?

Yes, a Pi 3, 4 or 5, in two ways. KyberNow Link OS is a system you copy onto the microSD card: the Pi starts straight into KyberNow Link, like a console plugged into the TV. You can also install the app alone on 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS. On a Pi 4 or 5, the picture goes up to 4K at 60 Hz. Neither has run on a real board yet, only in a virtual machine and under emulation. See On a Raspberry Pi.

Why is the Apple TV app not on the App Store?

Not yet: for now KyberNow is published only on GitHub, and TestFlight followed by the App Store are planned. On Apple TV, an app that is not on the App Store installs from a Mac with Xcode, signed with your own Apple account: see On Apple TV. You need an Apple TV 4K from 2021 or later; older models cannot run the app.

How is KyberNow related to Kyber?

KyberNow uses Kyber, the open-source engine that carries the picture, sound and controls, built by VLC developers. It adds KyberNow Station (the installer, virtual displays and speakers), KyberNow Admin and the KyberNow Link apps. KyberNow is neither published nor endorsed by the Kyber team, and is not affiliated with it. Our fixes to the engine are published.

What data does KyberNow collect?

No usage statistics, and at home nothing is sent to a server of ours: the picture goes straight from your PC to your devices. If you share with someone far away or switch on “Access away from home”, the connection goes through KyberNow's relay, which keeps IP addresses in its logs for a few days, never the share codes. The other outside connections are for checking for updates (on GitHub, and on kybernow.app for the Mac app) and, between your Apple devices, for syncing your computers and passwords through iCloud, which you can turn off.
