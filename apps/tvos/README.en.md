# KyberNow Link for Apple TV

English · [Français](README.md)

KyberNow Link, the KyberNow streaming client for the TV: your computer (KyberNow Station: Kyber 0.29 server + KyberNow Admin) full screen, with the Siri Remote or a gamepad, at up to 4K 60 Hz (the Apple TV's limit), with multichannel sound and up to four gamepads. Brand v2 ([guidelines](../../brand/v2/README.en.md)); mockup followed screen by screen: [Claude Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/RfGbfguTyAP1jjwFASVRyK) (home, adding, Top Shelf, passage, session, gamepads, settings).

**Installing the app without the App Store**: [SIDELOAD.en.md](SIDELOAD.en.md).

Apple TV 4K 2nd generation (A12) and later, tvOS 18 or later. Hardware HEVC and H.264; no AV1 (none of these chips decodes it in hardware).

## What the app does

- **Home** (`HomeView`, screens shared by every app in [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md)): the selected computer fills the screen, its last image slowly drifting in the background. In order: its state (“Online”, “Offline”, “Searching…” until it has answered), its name in large type, its system, an error if there is one (password refused, Station reinstalled), the display the session will ask for (“3840 × 2160 display at 60 Hz, sized for this screen”, or the computer's own displays as they are), “Connect” (when offline: “Wake the computer” when the app knows one of its network cards, learnt from Admin or received from the Mac through iCloud, then “Waking the computer…” until it is back, checked every 2 s for 2 min at most; otherwise “Try again”), the Game, Desktop and Max profiles with the profile's sentence, and “Settings” (the computer's settings). At the top, the controllers line (“2 controllers”, which opens their test) and “App settings”. At the bottom, the computers (four at most, name and state): focusing a card changes the scene; then the first one announced on the network (Bonjour) and not added yet (“On your network”), and “Add a computer”. No graphics card, address or latency on the home screen: they are in Statistics. The help glyphs follow the last controller used (remote, A / B, ✕ / ○).
- **tvOS focus**: the brand's neon standby. The focused element grows (× 1.06), lifts and gets a frost outline and a Spectre halo (`GlassButton`, `ChoiceButton`, `PrimaryButton` in `Theme.swift`).
- **Add a computer** (`AddHostView`), preferably without typing on the remote:
  - **From your Mac**: computers published by the Mac app in iCloud (key-value store, key `hosts`) arrive with their password (iCloud Keychain, service `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow.cloud`); added automatically when "Sync with iCloud" is on (`CloudSync`). The Mac app publishes them on every addition, change or removal (`CloudPublisher`), when it is signed with the iCloud entitlements (see [apps/macos/README.en.md](../macos/README.en.md#signing-and-icloud)).
  - **On the network**: Admin's Bonjour announcement `_kybernow._tcp` (except `kyber=0`: an Admin with no Kyber server behind it), then the account. Kyber's certificate is required at each session as Admin gives it (`kyber_fingerprint`), never remembered: reinstalling the computer does not block the app. tvOS offers typing on the iPhone keyboard and the Keychain. The certificate fingerprint seen must match the announcement.
  - **Code on screen**: the Apple TV draws a 6-digit code and presents it to every Station found (`POST /api/pair/request`, then `GET /api/pair/request/{id}`, in the core: `apps/core/src/console.rs`, through KyberKit's `ConsoleClient`, `ConsoleClientCore.swift`); you accept it in KyberNow Admin (Link devices, or the panel of the tray icon by the clock); the Apple TV receives a Kyber account of its own, revocable from its card under Link devices (see [server/README.en.md](../../server/README.en.md)). The code is valid for five minutes, then another one is drawn. It only goes to the Stations found through Bonjour: over a VPN, use the address. QR code to open Admin on a phone. A Station older than 0.2.2 does not know these calls: the app says so (“update it on the computer”).
  - **Address**: Admin address and port (VPN), then the account, with the first four groups of the fingerprint to compare.
- **Top Shelf** (`TopShelf/` extension): when KyberNow Link is in the top row of the Apple TV home screen, "Resume on a computer" shows each computer with its last image, the most recently opened first; a click or Play opens the session directly (`kybernow-tv://connect/<id>`), or the app on that computer when it is offline (`kybernow-tv://show/<id>`). The app drops the list and images in the `group.fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` container.
- **Choosing the display**: as on the Mac, the app waits at most 6 s, only once, for Kyber to announce only the virtual display, then takes the display at the requested size placed at (0, 0). Outputs that Windows lists as 0 × 0 (off) are left out; if no usable display remains, the session says so.
- **The passage** (`SessionView`, `Passage`): as on the Mac, the home screen splits along the 45° seam, the neon tube lights up, and the connection shows its three steps (“Preparing the display”, “Connecting”, “First frames”) and “Cancel” (the Back button cancels too); then both halves slide apart over the picture, alone: no capsule or figure on arrival. If the picture does not come despite the repairs, the core restarts the Station once and connects again: the screen says “The Station is restarting…” instead of the steps, then picks them up again. A failure before the picture reads “The connection failed”, with the reason.
- **In session**: the image lives in a `GCEventViewController`. With the menu closed, gamepads and the remote only talk to the game (Back does not quit the app). **Menu** with the remote's Back button or L1 + R1 + Start + Select (as on the Mac) held together for at least 250 ms, each can be turned off (under 250 ms, the combo is not formed: the game received the four buttons like any others and nothing is released for it; once formed, the menu opens and the held buttons are released for the game): the image recedes (× 0.94, dimmed), the stream's “Statistics” at the top (latency, frames per second, bitrate, format, losses: the session's only figures, on demand), and Resume, Gamepads, Sound (output and its delay, AirPods / HomePod / TV picker), Touch surface as a mouse (the remote), Gamepad as a mouse, Type text (tvOS or iPhone keyboard, sent character by character), Disconnect. While the menu is open, gamepads drive KyberNow Link and the game receives nothing: when it opens, everything is reset on the computer (held buttons released, sticks and triggers at rest, and the clicks and keys of the gamepad as mouse); when it closes, a stick still held is sent again as it is, but not the button that just closed the menu. The session controller is what takes the gamepad when the menu really opens, not `GamepadBridge`, which only reports the combination: outside a session (connecting, reconnecting), the combination does nothing and the gamepad stays with the game; when the picture restarts (profile change from the menu), the menu keeps the gamepad.
- **Sound**: Opus decoded by the Rust core's sound engine, pulled by the RemoteIO output (KyberKit's `AudioPlayerCore`); Auto follows the output (stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 LPCM on an HDMI receiver); spatial audio in AirPods (multichannel content); or sound stays on the computer. Warning when the output adds delay (AirPods, HomePod, AirPlay: over 40 ms), with the measured delay.
- **Gamepads** (`GamepadBridge`, `GamepadsView`): GameController instead of SDL. Xbox, DualSense, DualShock and MFi; each gamepad is a player on the computer (P1 to P4), in power-on order or at a fixed slot. Four at most: XInput (ViGEm on the computer) only exposes four; a fifth one waits, not plugged into the computer (message in the Gamepads screen, capsule during the session), and takes the slot of the first one that disconnects. The fixed order remembers a gamepad's slot by its vendor and model (key `vendorName|productCategory`), for want of a stable identifier in GameController: two identical gamepads (two DualSense) cannot be told apart, the second one switched on takes the first free slot; player indicator, Sony light bar in the player's colour. Per-computer setting “Gamepad seen by the computer”: Xbox 360 (default), they all become Xbox 360 gamepads on the computer, Sony ones included; or faithful, a Sony gamepad stays a DualShock 4 (the session's `gamepad_force_xbox360`, which overrides Admin's option). They announce themselves ("plugged") when the inputs reach the computer (`INPUT_SERVICE_CONNECTED`), then on each reconnection: sent earlier, the announcement would be lost, and the computer would then reject the buttons of an unknown gamepad. An axis is only sent when it has moved (by 1/512 of the travel), and the axes of a 4 ms slice leave together: GameController reports a stick's X and Y even when only one changed, and a DualSense over Bluetooth produces up to 1,500 packets per second, enough to fill kynput's send queue (64 packets) and lose releases. An axis refused by the send queue waits for the next slice. Packets that cannot catch up on their own (buttons, clicks, returns to rest, plugs) are retried for up to 200 ms and refusals are counted (see “Refused packets” in [apps/macos/README.en.md](../macos/README.en.md)). Game rumble on both motors (Core Haptics, left and right handles, large low motor on the left and small sharp one on the right as in XInput): each motor loops a continuous pattern (`CHHapticAdvancedPatternPlayer`) whose intensity follows each `Rumble` packet (`hapticIntensityControl`): a player recreated for every packet would chop the rumble; the Core Haptics engine stays started during the session (`isAutoShutdownEnabled` off) and the rumble only really stops on (0, 0), at the end of the requested duration (5 s at most without one, as kynput does on Mac) or at the end of the session. A controller whose motors do not play steps down one level: left and right motors, otherwise a single one on the handles (`.handles`), otherwise a single default one (`.default`), when a motor cannot be created or its first start fails; a 30 s pattern that is refused is remade over 4 s, and a refused dynamic intensity makes the pattern be recreated at each 10% step. The log (`KyberLog.app`, “vibrations de …”) gives, for each controller, its localities and motors on connection, every Core Haptics failure, the first start and, once per session and per controller, the arrival of the first `Rumble` packet, then of the first one that vibrates, and whether it found its controller. Test screen: live buttons, sticks, triggers, test rumble. Guide button (“Guide button” setting, the computer's settings › Controls): **Game** (default), the app asks tvOS for the gamepad's Home button during the session, menu closed, and on the test screen (`preferredSystemGestureState = .disabled`; not `.alwaysReceive`, deprecated with tvOS 27, which also lets tvOS go back to the home screen); it then goes to the game as Guide (Steam Big Picture, Game Bar). tvOS guarantees nothing: in “Test controllers” (App settings), Home lights up if it reaches the app, otherwise the Apple TV goes back to its home screen, and **Long press** is the choice: Menu or Options held for 600 ms go out as Guide while held, a shorter press goes out on release, released 50 ms later so that a game reading the gamepad once per frame sees it; no Guide while L1 or R1 are held (the menu combo may be forming) (Start and Select then reach the game on release rather than on press). **System**: the Home button stays with tvOS, no Guide. The other system gestures (screenshot on Options or Share) are turned off so the button goes out without delay.
- **Gamepad as mouse** (session menu tile, on by default with the Desktop profile): left stick = pointer (progressive acceleration, the computer's screen crossed in 1.5 s at full tilt whatever its resolution, LT / L2 for precision, RT / R2 to go fast, speed in the computer's settings › Controls), right stick = wheel (up: scrolls up), A / ✕ left click, X / □ right click, B / ○ middle click, d-pad = arrow keys, L1 / R1 = Alt + Tab (Alt held while you keep going, plus Shift with L1), Start = Windows key, Select = Escape. The menu combo's buttons act on release, unless the combo was formed. The game does not receive the gamepad; turned off, it is a gamepad again, nothing left pressed.
- **Remote as a mouse** ("Touch surface as a mouse" tile): touch surface = pointer, click = left click, Play/Pause = right click; on from the start with the Desktop profile, or with the computer's “Remote as a mouse” setting. The finger's movement follows the gamepad-as-mouse acceleration curve, applied to its speed, and is relative to the size of the computer's screen: slow finger, one pad crossed = 40 % of the screen (for aiming); fast swipe, up to nearly three screens. A lifted finger is read from the touch surface's "touched" state (Siri Remote 2nd generation and later), not from the (0, 0) position, which is also the centre of the pad; a jump (finger put down elsewhere) does not move the pointer. **Bluetooth keyboard and mouse** (`KeyboardMouse`): HID usages converted to scancodes by kynput. Wheel: GameController gives the vertical on the first axis (positive = down) and the horizontal on the second (positive = right), in notches (1.0 = one notch, fractions for a Magic Mouse or a trackpad), as SDL reads it on iOS and tvOS; kynput expects the same unit and signs, at most one notch per packet (a big wheel spin goes out as several). The first values of each session go to the log (“molette Bluetooth”), to check the unit on a real mouse. Buttons 4 and 5 (thumb side, `auxiliaryButtons`): back and forward (X1 and X2 on Windows). “Apple ISO keyboard” setting (the computer's settings › Controls, off by default): on a European Apple keyboard, the key below Escape (`@#` on an AZERTY) and the one next to ⇧ (`<>`) arrive swapped (see “Apple ISO keyboard” in [apps/macos/README.en.md](../macos/README.en.md)); tvOS does not tell the layout, when the setting is on, the swap is applied to keyboards whose vendor is Apple or unknown (a keyboard from another vendor, which names itself, keeps its keys).
- **Background**: when the app goes to the background (TV button, another app, sleep), the releases that follow would never reach it: everything is released on the computer (gamepad buttons and axes, clicks and keys of the gamepad as mouse, then kynput's `flush_keys` for the Bluetooth keyboard and everything else). On return, sticks and triggers are sent again as they are (not the buttons: the one that brought the app back is still pressed; their state in the app is put right).
- **Input queue**: gamepads, remote, Bluetooth keyboard and mouse arrive on a single serial `userInteractive` queue (`GamepadBridge.queue`, the `handlerQueue` of every GameController device), not on the main thread, where an interface animation would delay a button by several milliseconds (peaks above 16 ms). The internal state (slots, menu capture, gamepad as mouse, pending axes, rumble) is only touched on that queue; only the gamepad list, the test screen's live state, the last gamepad used and the callbacks to the interface (menu, drawn pointer) go up to the main thread. Rumble goes from kynput's thread to its own queue (Core Haptics calls, which are synchronous, never delay a button). A Bluetooth mouse in relative mode (full-screen game) sends its raw moves from the queue; in desktop mode, the shared pointer layer (`PointerLayer`, on the same queue) holds the position and sends it as absolute, and clicks and the wheel (mouse, remote, gamepad as mouse) leave from that same queue, so they never overtake the move before them.
- **Pointer** (`StreamView`), like the Mac app in desktop mode: the Apple TV holds the pointer position and sends it as absolute (remote, gamepad and mouse move it). Windows hides its pointer with no mouse plugged in, or at the slightest keystroke, and only shows it again on an absolute position: with relative moves it would stay hidden and the app would lose it while moving. The app draws it at that position, always visible, with the computer's pointer shape (an arrow until then), twice desktop size (readable from three metres). If the computer moves its pointer itself, the app picks up its position once idle. Only the Bluetooth mouse sends raw motion, when Admin sees a full-screen game (`GET /api/stream/foreground`, every second), like the Mac's Auto mode.
- **Settings for a computer** (`SettingsView`): “Settings for {name}”, the profile at the top (Game, Desktop, Max, Custom), and the six sections shared by the KyberNow Link apps. **Picture**: resolution (match this display, 4K, 1440p, 1080p), refresh rate (60, 50 or 30 Hz), virtual display, bitrate (5 to 4,294 Mbit/s, warning above 150), video format (HEVC or H.264), sharp text (4:4:4) if the Apple TV decodes it in hardware, keep the last image, match the TV to the session's refresh rate. **Sound**: Auto, Stereo, 5.1, 7.1 or On the computer; spatial audio in AirPods; late sound alert. **Controls**: remote as a mouse (always on, not adjustable, with the Desktop profile), menu with Back or with the combination (at least one of them), controller seen by the computer (Xbox 360 or as is), rumble, controller-as-mouse speed, Guide button (System, Game or Long press), player order (power-on or fixed), Apple ISO keyboard. **Network**: picture transport (Fast or Reliable), reconnect automatically, Wake on LAN (“Ready” and the MAC address learnt, or how to learn it; tvOS may refuse the broadcast, so the packet also goes directly to the last known address), the Apple TV's link (Ethernet or Wi-Fi). Under each section in the list, a simple summary: “Match this display · 60 Hz”, “Stereo”, “Menu: Back and combination”, “Fast”, “Yesterday”, “Up to date”. **Statistics** (`StatsCard.swift`): the Mac app's “Last session” card at TV size (median in large type, a 0 to 20 ms ruler with the marks of one frame at 160, 120 and 60 Hz, a band from the median to the 95th percentile, “95% of frames under …”, duration and day, what was sent: resolution, refresh rate, format, bitrate), kept at the end of every session of at least 3 s; then “This computer”: system, graphics card, Admin's address and port, fingerprint (first four groups; Station reinstalled: the fingerprint presented, to compare with Admin's before trusting it). **Station**: the version (“Up to date” or “Version … available”), a QR code of Admin to scan with your phone (the browser asks for Admin's password) and its address in plain text, “Restart the Station” and “Update the Station” (when Admin says the version installs from a device; otherwise, the installer on the computer), followed until the Station is back; if a device is in session, “Restart anyway” or “Update anyway”; a Station 0.4.9 or older offers neither. Then the account (“This device's own access” for an account created by code pairing, as Admin says or as recorded when it was added; a new password if it is refused) and “Remove this computer”, with a confirmation. With the remote, right goes from a section to the first row of its panel, wherever the section sits in the list (`defaultFocus` with `.userInitiated` priority on each focus section); left or Back return to the chosen section. Information rows (Statistics, account) take focus, so the panel can scroll. In French, « de » elides before a name starting with a vowel or an h (« Réglages d'Atelier »): two keys per text, picked by `French.elides`. The profile, the picture (except “Match the TV”), sound, transport, reconnection, controller seen by the computer, remote as a mouse and the last image apply to this computer; the rest applies to the whole app on this Apple TV (for the current tvOS user).
- **App settings** (`AppSettingsView`): sync with iCloud, language, “Test controllers”, version, and what this Apple TV can do (display, hardware decoding, 4:4:4, sound output and its measured delay).
- **tvOS profiles**: the app runs as the current tvOS user (`com.apple.developer.user-management`: `runs-as-current-user`): each household member has their own computers, settings and Keychain. Users are switched in the Apple TV's Control Center (tvOS does not let an app do it, nor read the user's name).
- **Picture restart** (profile, fallback, display): the core stops the previous input chain, `SessionEngine` creates a new one and calls `StreamView.attach` again without a `detach` in between; the view first undoes what was tied to the old chain (`KeyboardMouse.attach` removes its previous observers, which would otherwise pile up at every restart), and keeps the pointer, the gamepad as mouse and the open menu.
- **Session** (`SessionController`): same flow as on the Mac (preparation by Admin with the TV's screen capped at 4K60, heartbeat, reconnection, virtual display requested again, fallback when the image does not get through, clean end and last image kept).
- **Metal layer alone on screen**. With the menu closed, nothing stays above the Metal layer: the menu's veil and material are only in the hierarchy during the passage and the menu, the `Passage` leaves it 1.7 s after opening (`passageShown`), and the rounded-corner clipping (`clipsToBounds`) is only set while the menu is open. A transparent layer left on top, even at opacity 0, forces tvOS to composite the image instead of sending it to the screen as is: one more refresh, 16.7 ms at 60 Hz. **Measured presentation**: on the Apple TV, `VideoRenderer` reads the presentation time the screen reports (`addPresentedHandler`); without a callback 100 ms after the GPU finishes, or with a zero time, it estimates the time (next refresh), never treating the frame as discarded nor rebuilding the layer; estimated frames are counted (`PresentStats.estimated`, `KN_PRESENT_STATS` log). The menu's latency is thus measured, except for those frames.
- **Image continuity** (as on the Mac, see [apps/macos/README.en.md](../macos/README.en.md)): a computer mode change moves the input coordinate space to the size of the decoded frames or of Kyber's list (the pointer held by the app keeps its relative place, and the computer learns it right away) and shows in a capsule at the top of the screen; the Game profile lets it be, Desktop has it put back (`mode_policy`), and the menu then offers “Restore 3840 × 2160” at the bottom right. An image pipeline error on the computer or a refused image restarts the image one second later, three times at most, before ending the session (“The picture was restarted”). A shown display that disappears is replaced first by the same display back under another number (same place, same size), and never by a number missing from the latest list. After an image restart (new input service on Kyber's side), on `INPUT_SERVICE_CONNECTED`: gamepads plugged again, Bluetooth mouse raw or absolute depending on full screen, modifiers held on the Bluetooth keyboard sent again, pointer held by the app (remote, gamepad as mouse, mouse) sent back to its place; the gamepad as mouse keeps going by itself. The heartbeat carries the current profile's policy; with `restore`, if Admin still reports another mode, the app asks for the starting one again at most every 30 s.
- **KyberNow Share** (contract: [docs/kybernow-share.en.md](../../docs/kybernow-share.en.md); texts: [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md), “Sharing”; component shared with the Mac, iPhone and iPad: [apps/macos/README.en.md](../macos/README.en.md), “KyberNow Share”). **Guest**: “Join with a code” (home card, next to “Add a computer”; the network card only has room when there are fewer than four computers) opens the code and name screen (`ShareViews.swift`): a **digit pad** for the remote (1 to 9, delete, 0; six boxes grouped by three, focus moves by itself to the name then to “Join” at the sixth digit), the name (the Apple TV's name, remembered; tvOS or iPhone keyboard) and “Join”; “Looking for the share…”, the core's refusals in the app's language. The `kybernow-tv://join?…` link (same shape as `kybernow://join`) opens the same screen with the code filled in. The guest session is an ordinary session (`SessionController(guest:)`), titled “Guest on {name}”, adding nothing to the computer list or to iCloud (no last picture, no statistics); reduced menu: “Resume”, “Controllers” only as a player, “Sound”, “Remote as a mouse”, “Controller as mouse” and “Type text” only with the keyboard and mouse given by the host, “Leave”; the role (“Spectator”, “Player”) above the title; brief capsule on a role change; the end by the share (“{host} stopped sharing.”) on screen with “Back to home”, never “Take back control”. **Host**: the “Share” tile of the session menu (eighth tile; dimmed with the core's reason when the Station is too old or sharing is off) opens the share of the primary display and its panel, in tiles and buttons: QR code, the code, big, guests (role, app, how long; “Let play”, “Give/Take back keyboard and mouse”, “Watch only”, “Remove”), “New code”, “Stop sharing”, “Close”; Back closes the panel. No “Copy link”: the Apple TV has no clipboard.

## Code shared with the Mac

KyberKit is the same module as on the Mac. The project (`project.yml`) takes everything in `apps/macos/KyberKit` that does not depend on AppKit and excludes only the Mac's files (`StreamView`, `MetalSurface`, `RelativeMouse`, `KeymapMac`). The Apple TV rests on the Rust core like the Mac ([docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md)): the session (preparation, connection, display choice, relaunches, reconnections, fallback to 150 Mbit/s then 4:2:0, heartbeat, verified end) through `SessionEngine`, Admin through `ConsoleClient` (`ConsoleClientCore.swift`), sound through the core's engine (`AudioPlayerCore` keeps only the RemoteIO output), latency, and inputs through `InputCore`: kynput chain and retry backlog, gamepad axes filtered and batched, keyboard (scancodes, ISO swap, lock keys), release all and held modifiers, the computer's pointer and the position held by the Apple TV, the L1 + R1 + Start + Select combination (`PadCombo`, on the core, with its own thread for the 250 ms). The core is built for tvOS by `apps/apple/scripts/build-apple-mobile.sh tvos` (`Vendor/KyberNow.xcframework`).

What `KyberKitTV/` provides: capture of GameController's events (`GamepadBridge`: gamepads and remote, menu, gamepad as mouse, long press; `KeyboardMouse`: Bluetooth keyboard and mouse), all on the serial input queue `InputQueue.device`; drawing in `StreamView` (UIKit, always-visible layer, 48-point box) and `MetalSurface` (which creates its Metal layer once in the window and attaches the rendering at that point: on iOS, a layer that presented before being in a window stays off screen, see apps/ios/README.en.md); the “Apple ISO keyboard” setting (Apple or unknown-vendor keyboards, where the Mac reads the layout from the system). No clipboard on tvOS. The model (`Model.swift`: computers, settings, profiles), the texts and Station tracking shared by the Apple apps (`LinkUI.swift`) and discovery (`Discovery.swift`) come from the Mac app.

## Building

```sh
# Kyber SDK for tvOS (once, then after a change to kyber-patches):
rustup toolchain install nightly --profile minimal --component rust-src
scripts/build-kyber-tvos.sh

# The Rust core and its C bridge for tvOS (after every change to apps/core or apps/apple):
../apple/scripts/build-apple-mobile.sh tvos

# Xcode project (XcodeGen):
xcodegen generate
open KyberNowTV.xcodeproj
```

`scripts/build-kyber-tvos.sh` builds Kyber 0.29's libkynput and libkyclient, with the Mac app's patches (`apps/macos/kyber-patches`), for Apple TV and its simulator (tier 3 Rust targets: `-Zbuild-std`, like Kyber's `build-ios.sh`), plus libopus. VLC is never called (the app decodes image and sound): empty functions satisfy the linker. Everything goes into `Vendor/KyberSDK.xcframework` (arm64 only), the headers into `Vendor/include` (`CKyber` and `COpus` modules); the app only takes libkynput, libopus and those empty functions from it. The script empties `Vendor/` before writing, core included: run `build-apple-mobile.sh tvos` again afterwards. `apps/apple/scripts/build-apple-mobile.sh tvos` builds the core (`kybernow-core`) and its C bridge (`kybernow-apple`) as static libraries, device and simulator, gathered in `Vendor/KyberNow.xcframework` with the `kybernow.h` header (`CKyberNow` module, `Vendor/KyberNow/include`).

Other scripts: `scripts/phosphor-icons.mjs` (Phosphor icons in the catalog, from the `assets` folder of the `@phosphor-icons/core` package given as argument), `scripts/app-icon.py` (layered icon for parallax and Top Shelf images, from the `brand/v2` geometry).

Remote tests (`UITests/`, XCUIRemote): reaching the computer's settings from the home screen; from every section, entering its panel at the first row and coming back to the same section (left or Back); keeping focus in the app settings list; keeping a form after the keyboard. `xcodebuild test -scheme KyberNowTV -destination 'platform=tvOS Simulator,name=…'`.

Debug builds: `-demo YES` shows three demo computers (`loadDemo` in `apps/macos/KyberNow/LinkUI.swift`, in their own preferences, with no network, iCloud or Top Shelf); `-demoState online|offline|searching`, `-demoEmpty YES`, `-demoStation current|update|setup|installing|old|restarting|updating`, `-demoProblem password|certificate`, `-stationInSession YES`; `-hostIndex 0…2` picks the computer; `-screen add|app|settings|gamepads|connecting|restarting|passage|menu` opens a screen directly, `-wakeNow YES` wakes the selected computer, `-stationAction restart|update` runs the Station section's action, `-section picture|sound|controls|network|statistics|station` a settings section, `-addMethod 0…3` an add pane (checking without a remote).

## Distribution

Not on the App Store: the app is installed from a Mac with Xcode, signed with your own Apple account ([SIDELOAD.en.md](SIDELOAD.en.md), `scripts/sideload.sh`). Every release attaches `KyberSDK-tvOS.zip` (the contents of `Vendor/`: Kyber SDK and core), which the script downloads and which CI reuses to build the app for the simulator (build skipped, with a warning, when the core or the Kyber patches changed since the latest release).

## Tests

- KyberNow Share: built for the tvOS 27.0 simulator (Apple TV 4K); “Join” screen (digit pad), guest menu (player), host panel with three guests seen in the demo (`-demo YES -screen join|guest|guest-menu|menu`, `-demoRole spectator|player|player_kbm`, `-demoShare YES`, `-demoRoleThen player|player_kbm|end-stopped|end-removed`). **Not verified**: a real share (the KyberNow Share Station could not be tried yet), typing the code with the remote (the simulator has no remote: the pad and the focus hand-over are not tried), the tvOS keyboard for the name, the `kybernow-tv://join` link, a real Apple TV.

- Rumble on a DualSense in “As is” mode and on an Xbox controller: checked on a real Apple TV (25/10/2026).
- Not checked yet on a real Apple TV: that the iCloud Keychain does bring the passwords published by the Mac, and the wheel unit of a Bluetooth mouse (log “molette Bluetooth”).
