# KyberNow Link for Android and Android TV

English · [Français](README.md)

KyberNow Link on Android: a single app for phone, tablet, TV (Android TV, Google TV) and handheld console, bringing back the computer (KyberNow Station: Kyber 0.29 server + KyberNow Admin) full screen, by touch, with the remote, keyboard and mouse, or a controller. Brand v2 ([guidelines](../../brand/v2/README.en.md)); mock-ups: [Claude Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/Ug3n57UPSvsrA3Hd9UMawX) (home, session menu, controllers and rumble, image settings on the TV; home, adding and session on the phone; home on the tablet).

**Test status**: checked on 22/10/2026 on the NVIDIA Shield TV 2019, to a Windows PC over Ethernet: 4K at 60 Hz session in HEVC (61 frames per second, 7.1 sound), discovery and adding, session menu, Xbox controller over Bluetooth, remote and controller as mouse, ending the session; Halo is playable. The Xbox controller's rumble does not reach it on the Shield (see “Rumble”). Also checked: a session on an Android phone. Not tried yet: 1080p at 120 Hz, updating the app from a published release, the on-screen controller and the keyboard that folds away on touch (checked in screenshots and tests, not on a device). KyberNow Share (sharing, joining): written and checked in screenshots and tests (`ShareTest`), native library rebuilt; never tried on a device or with a Station 0.8.0. The decision is in [PROJECT.en.md](../../PROJECT.en.md) (“Android and Android TV”).

## Devices

- **Android 11 or later** (API 30): the NVIDIA Shield goes no further than Android 11.
- **TVs**: NVIDIA Shield first, Google TV Streamer, Chromecast with Google TV, Google TV sets. The Chromecast with Google TV and many TVs run 32-bit Android: the APK carries `arm64-v8a`, `armeabi-v7a` (and `x86_64` for the emulator).
- **Phones and tablets**. The session is a resizable window (Samsung DeX, ChromeOS), with keyboard and mouse. The app does not drive an external display on its own: no image sent by itself to a USB-C display, no desk mode.
- **Handheld consoles** (Retroid, AYN Odin) and **phone controllers** (Razer Kishi, Backbone): the built-in controller is a player like any other; the interface is driven by focus, as on the TV.
- **Test hardware**: Shield TV 2019 “tube” (no USB port: Bluetooth controllers only), Xiaomi Redmi A5; DualSense, Xbox, Shield and Switch Pro controllers. Nothing to try a tablet, a handheld console, DeX or ChromeOS.

## What the app does

- **Home** (`ui/home`): the chosen computer full frame (its last image, otherwise Admin's preview; very slow zoom, desaturated when offline), its state (“Online”, “Offline”, “Searching…”; Admin polled every 8 s; a refused password or a reinstalled Station shown under the name), its name, its system, the display the session will create (“2400 × 1080 display at 120 Hz, sized for this screen”, through the same computation as the session, `model/SessionMode.kt`; “The computer's own displays, as they are” without a virtual display), “Connect”, the Game, Desktop and Max profiles (“Custom” when the settings differ from them) with their sentence, and “Settings”; on phone and tablet in portrait, “Connect” across the width, then the profiles as a segmented control with the gear at the end of the row. Offline: “Wake the computer” when the app knows its network cards (learnt from Admin at every successful poll, or a MAC address typed in), then “Waking the computer…” while it comes back (Admin polled every 2 s, 2 min at most); otherwise “Try again”. The core sends the wake packet: to the subnet broadcast, 255.255.255.255 and directly, ports 9 and 7. on TV, the controllers plugged in (“2 controllers”). Then the other computers, their name and state (long press: their settings), those found on the network (“On your network”) and “Add a computer”. At the top: the app's update (“Version X available”) and the app settings. No graphics card, address, decoder or latency: the screens and their texts, shared by every KyberNow Link app, are described in [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md).
- **Adding** (`ui/add`): on the network (`_kybernow._tcp` through NsdManager, TXT `name`, `fp`, `kyber`; when Android only gives a link-local IPv6 address, the IPv4 address is asked of JmDNS, then through a direct mDNS query, `model/MdnsLookup.kt`), a six-digit code shown to every Station found and accepted in Admin (a Kyber account of the device's own; the default tab on the TV), an address with the fingerprint's first four groups to compare, a camera QR code (phone, tablet; CameraX and zxing, no Google services) and a `kybernow://` link opened from another app. Except with a code, the chosen computer then asks for the Admin account (username, password), or a code instead. Passwords encrypted with an Android Keystore key.
- **Session** (`session/`):
  - The **connection screen**: home split along the 45° seam, the neon; the computer's name, its three steps (Preparing the display, Connecting, First frames), what opens the menu or the bar (from the computer's settings), and Cancel. If the picture does not come, the core restarts the Station once, as a last resort: “The Station is restarting…” in place of the steps, which then resume. On the first frame, the halves slide apart on the picture alone: nothing shows over it. A failure before the first frame: “The connection failed” and the reason, in one sentence.
  - The **image** full frame, at its aspect ratio (ink bars), on a `SurfaceView` that the decoder renders to directly; the interface and pointer views are removed when there is nothing to show.
  - **TV**: menu through the remote's Back button (configurable) or L1 + R1 + Start + Select (in controller-as-mouse mode too): stream health at the top (latency, frames per second, bitrate, mode, losses), then tiles that all fit on screen (five per row): Resume, other profiles, Sound, Remote as mouse, Controller as mouse, Computer display (without a virtual display, when the computer has several: switches to the next one), Type text, Keys, Statistics, and Disconnect in red, last. While the menu is open, controllers drive the app (A selects, B closes, the d-pad moves; other buttons and the combination's release do nothing) and the computer releases everything.
  - **Phone and tablet**: nothing over the picture (except the on-screen controller, when it is shown); the Back gesture opens the bar (live statistics, profiles, trackpad or direct touch, On-screen controller, Auto / desktop / game mouse, Display (without a virtual display, when the computer has several: switches to the next one), keyboard, Keys, Disconnect). L1 + R1 + Start + Select opens the bar driven by the controller (handheld consoles): focus on its first item, A selects, B closes, the d-pad moves, the game receives nothing until it closes; opened by touch, the controller stays with the game; a row of keys above the on-screen keyboard (Esc, Tab, sticky Ctrl, Alt and ⊞, arrows, F1 to F12, Ctrl + Alt + Del), text sent as Unicode; a tap on the picture folds the keyboard away and does not click (sliding, two fingers and holding keep their effect; in direct touch, the click goes as soon as the finger slides or stays down).
  - **States** as capsules, never alerts: reconnecting (with Disconnect), image restarted, computer mode changed (“Restore” in the Desktop profile), fallback, virtual display adapted, controller connected.
  - **End**: “Disconnect” shows “Disconnecting…” at once while the computer gets its display and sound back (a few seconds); the app returns home after 6 s at the latest. The last image is kept (PixelCopy, 0.8 s at most). A session of at least 3 s leaves its latency (median, 95th percentile), duration, time and what was sent (resolution, refresh rate, format, bitrate) to the Statistics section; a last session written by 0.4.x still reads, without what was sent. A session stopped by an error leaves a card (the error, the computer's display or sound not restored) and “Back to home”.
- **KyberNow Share, sharing on your network** ([contract](../../docs/kybernow-share.en.md), texts from [link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md), “Sharing”; the core does all the work, `Native.share*` and `Native.consoleShare*`, `core/Share.kt` sorts its answers):
  - **Joining** (`ui/share/JoinScreen.kt`): “Join with a code” next to “Add a computer” (home row, and the home with no computer). On a phone and a tablet, the code on Android's numeric keyboard (grouped “123 456”); on a TV, a remote-driven pad (1 to 9, ⌫, 0) and the code in large type. “Your name”: the device's name, remembered (`share` preferences) for next time. The code goes out, with `app: android`, to every computer found on the network (the pairing ones; waited for 4 s at most); the core returns the first Station that accepts it and says the refusals itself (no share with this code, full, too many attempts, picture format not playable: “This device cannot play this computer's picture (HEVC).”, checked against what the device decodes before entering), in the app's language. With a camera (phone, tablet), the “Code” / “QR code” choice at the top of the screen: “QR code” aims at the host's and acts like a link. A `kybernow://join?host=…&port=…&fp=…&code=…` link (QR code read by Android's camera, link opened from another app, or QR code scanned in “Add a computer › QR code”) opens the same screen with the Station and the code filled in: its address is shown, nothing is looked up on the network and nothing is sent before “Join” (a QR code read in “Join” fills the screen the same way, sending nothing). If the caller gives up while searching, or the app went to the background (Android would not open the session), the guest leaves right away (`shareLeave`) and its tokens are kept nowhere.
  - **Guest session** (`SessionActivity` with `EXTRA_GUEST`): the Station and its tokens are handed over by `KyberNowApp.handOverGuest`, **in memory only** (never in an Intent or on disk; a session recreated after the process or the activity died does not resume: the activity closes at once, without error, and the core makes the guest leave). No computer is added to the device and nothing is written to the ones it has: no last image, no last session (Statistics), no on-screen controller settings, no password; the Keystore is not touched. The session is an ordinary on-screen session (the `sessionStart` JSON carries `guest`: the answer of “Join” as is and what the device decodes; no mode, no virtual display, no account) titled “Guest on {name}”, with the share's display as the Station gives it; the picture takes its place again when the window changes size (a tablet turned, a window resized: the activity is not recreated). Reduced bar (phone, tablet), like the web client's: what only concerns the device (latency and statistics), “Spectator” or “Player”, “On-screen controller” only as a player, touch, mouse mode, keyboard and “Keys” only as a player who has the keyboard and mouse, “Leave” (also in the reconnecting capsule); no profile, no computer settings, no clipboard; Android has no full screen to offer. On a TV, the menu with the stream's health at the top, Resume, Sound and Statistics, remote or controller as mouse, “Type text” and “Keys” with the keyboard and mouse, and “Leave” in red. The role follows `onShareRole`: a spectator sends nothing (the core does not open its input chain; its controllers do not go out either, and without the combination, which that chain forms, Back, Select and Start on its controller open the menu: a controller alone can leave; the connection help then only mentions Back) and the app offers nothing that would go nowhere; a player sends controllers as usual, never the keyboard or mouse without that right (touches, plugged or captured mouse, physical keyboard and remote ignored, the TV's Back never sent as Escape; remote controls switched off and a finger gesture in progress dropped when the right is taken back); brief notices “You can play.”, “You have the keyboard and mouse.”, “The keyboard and mouse were taken back.”, and “You are watching again.” when the host sends a player back to spectating (“Watch only”). Ending: the end card gives the core's reason (“{host} stopped sharing.”, “{host} removed you from the share.”, as the core writes them: the host's name keeps its case; “The connection failed” and the unplayable format before the first image), never “Take back control” or “The computer gets its screen and sound back” (a guest changes nothing on the computer); “Leave” goes out through `share_leave`, no last image.
  - **Sharing** (`session/SharePanel.kt`, host): in the fixed part of the bar, next to “Disconnect” (visible without scrolling it; icon only on a small phone in landscape, under 700 dp wide), the “Share” tile of the TV menu. Impossible when `Native.shareAvailability` says the Station is too old or sharing is turned off: dimmed but selectable; by touch and with the remote, it opens the panel reduced to the reason (the core's text); with a mouse it is inactive and its tooltip gives the reason (info read again each time the bar or the menu opens; while unknown, active). The panel (solid, so the code and QR code stay readable over the bar): “Share {name}”, “Code” and the code in large type, the apps' link QR code (`link`), “Copy link” (the web client's address, `web`; not on a TV; “Link copied.” or “Could not copy: select the link by hand.” with the address to select), “Guests” (“{name} · Spectator”, “· Player”, “· Player, keyboard and mouse”, the guest's app and “for {duration}” by the Station's clock), “Let play” (spectator); “Watch only” in its place for a player, then “Give” or “Take back keyboard and mouse”; “Remove”, “New code”, “Stop sharing”, “Four guests at most.”. “Share” opens the share of the main display; if one is already open, it shows it. The list follows the session's heartbeat (`onGuests`) and is read again after each action; if the share stops elsewhere (Admin) while the panel is open, the panel closes. An open share shows in the bar: a “2 guests” pill that reopens the panel (“0 guests”, “1 guest”, “2 guests”: Android's plural). Closing the panel leaves the share open; closing the session stops it (the Station).
- **Controllers** (`model/Gamepads.kt`, `session/Inputs.kt`): four players at most in arrival order, a fifth waiting (`PlayerSlots`; the on-screen controller is one more); announced to the computer at every reconnection; buttons, sticks, triggers (analogue or digital) and hat d-pads; a controller's Back and Menu go to the game as Select and Start; Guide button (Game; Long press: Start held 600 ms counts as Guide; System: it stays with Android); controller as mouse (left stick pointer, RT faster, LT finer, right stick wheel, A / X / B clicks, d-pad arrows, Start ⊞, Select Esc, L1 / R1 Alt + Tab; Start, Select, L1 and R1 act on release and only alone, to let the combination through; an Xbox controller's Back and Menu over Bluetooth count as Select and Start). A device is a controller only if it has a stick (the Shield's remotes and virtual devices that claim to be controllers are left out). Test screen (`ui/gamepads`, from the app settings): live buttons, sticks, triggers, each controller's motors and a test rumble per motor.
- **On-screen controller** (phone, tablet; `session/OnScreenPad.kt` for the layout and touches, `OnScreenPadView.kt` for the drawing, `Haptics.kt`): the bar's “On-screen controller” button, remembered for each computer. Two fixed layouts for both thumbs: “Full” (LT, LB, the left stick and the D-pad on the left; RT, RB, A, B, X, Y and the right stick on the right; View, Guide and Menu at the bottom centre) and “Simple” (left stick, D-pad, A, B, X, Y and Menu, in the same places, Menu in the middle); the small button above Guide switches between them (“Simple controller”, “Full controller” for screen readers), the choice remembered for the computer. It sits at the bottom of the screen, clear of the notch and of Android's gesture areas (Back on the sides, home at the bottom, the shade at the top); on a tall screen (tablet, portrait) it moves up a little; it shrinks when it does not fit. Each finger takes the control it touches and keeps it until it lifts, several at once, in areas larger than the drawing: the stick starts from where the finger lands (landing on its edge, it pushes at once), two taps click it (L3, R3, full layout); the triggers are progressive (light at the top, fully pressed at the bottom: you pull it down); the D-pad has eight directions; the finger slides from one button to the next. The computer sees it as an Xbox 360 controller (045E:028E, even in “As is”), in the first free player slot: announced when shown and at every input reconnection, unplugged when removed, with the same packets as a real controller (`PadRouter.sync`); everything is released when it is removed, when the app goes to the background and at the end of the session, and so are the controls that disappear when switching to the simple layout. While it is shown, the screen does not act as a mouse (a connected mouse still does); a tap outside the controls folds the keyboard away. Drawn to the brand: ink glass, a frost line and an ink edge under the letters and signs, legible on a bright picture, the Spectre signal on press; the pressed button lights up and sinks. Haptic feedback (Off, Light, Medium, Strong): Android's composition primitives when the device has them (intensity set at each level), otherwise the system's feedback at Medium and the predefined effects at the other levels; a crisp click when pressing a button, the D-pad or a stick, a light click at the start of a trigger's travel and a firm one at the end, for the sticks a tick when leaving the centre, a very light tick at each change of direction (eight) and a tick at the edge, never a continuous vibration. The game's rumble aimed at its slot vibrates the device, at the game's amplitude when the motor can set it. Without a vibration motor, the setting gives way to a line that says so.
- **Keyboard, mouse, touch**: physical keyboard as scancodes (the Linux code from `KeyEvent.getScanCode()` converted by kynput; a key with no hardware code goes as text), Ctrl + Alt + Shift + M opens the menu or the bar, Ctrl + Alt + Shift + Q quits; mouse by position (the computer's pointer drawn by the app, always visible with remote or controller as mouse: Windows hides its own without a mouse plugged in) or captured as raw motion when a game wants it (Auto mouse); trackpad (one finger = pointer, tap = click, two fingers = right click and wheel, hold = drag) or direct touch; remote as arrows and Enter, or as a mouse.
- **Clipboard**: text both ways (phone, tablet); the device's text is read only when the computer pastes (Android 12 shows a notice on every read).
- **Background**: everything is released on the computer, bitrate capped at 2 Mbit/s, image frozen; with the surface gone, the decoder is rebuilt on a keyframe on return.
- **App updates** (`update/Updates.kt`): one check every 6 hours at most (at launch, when you come back to the app or while it runs, if the previous one is 6 hours old; “Check now” in the app settings); “Version X available” at the top of home; download on request, checked against the signed manifest, then installation through PackageInstaller (see “Updates and publishing”).
- **“Resume on a computer” row** on the Android TV home screen (`tv/TvChannel.kt`): each computer with its last image, lent to the home launcher only; one click opens the session (`kybernow://connect/<id>`). Google TV does not show these rows.
- **Computer settings** (`ui/settings`, “Settings for {name}”): the profile at the top, then six sections; on TV and tablet, the sections in a column on the left (on TV, focus picks one) and their contents on the right; on the phone, one scrolling page. Picking a profile resets the format, bitrate and transport to its own; changing them makes the profile “Custom”.
  - **Picture**: resolution (match this display, 4K, 1440p, 1080p, 720p), refresh rate, virtual display, video format, bitrate, sharp text (4:4:4: for information, the Desktop or Max profile uses it when the device decodes it), frame display (immediate, for information), match the display to the session and TV game mode.
  - **Sound**: Auto, stereo, 5.1, 7.1, on the computer.
  - **Controls**: controller seen by the computer, combination, Back (TV), Guide button, mouse; touch, shared clipboard and on-screen controller (shown, layout, size, opacity, haptic feedback) on phone and tablet.
  - **Network**: picture transport (fast or reliable), reconnect automatically, Wake on LAN (“Ready” and the MAC address learnt from Admin, or “No address yet”; as an advanced setting, a MAC address typed in, for a Station older than 0.5.0, sent as well as Admin's).
  - **Statistics** (`ui/settings/Statistics.kt`): the last session, as on Mac (large median, a 0 to 20 ms ruler with the 160, 120 and 60 Hz marks and the band from the median to the 95th percentile, “95% of frames under …”, duration and time, what was sent), then this computer (system, graphics card, address, fingerprint). When the Station was reinstalled (Admin's certificate changed), the new fingerprint, to compare with Admin's and then pin.
  - **Station**: its version and update; Admin (“Open Admin” in the browser on phone and tablet, QR code and address on TV); “Restart the Station” and “Update the Station” (when the version installs from a device), to confirm when a device is in a session, then followed by polling Admin every 2 s until the Station is back (`AppModel.station`); the account (username and password, or “This device's own access” when it was added with a code: remembered when adding it, or told by Admin for a computer added before 0.5.0); when the Station is slow to come back or the update does not complete, the app says so and points to Admin; “Remove this computer”, to confirm. With a Station 0.4.9 or older: no restart or update, only the invitation to update it from Admin.
- **App settings**: language (Auto, Français, English), this device (name seen by Admin, hardware decoding, display, variable refresh rate, game mode, sound output), controller test, updates (status, notes, stable or beta channel), licences.
- **Languages**: French and English (`res/values-fr`, `res/values`, produced by `scripts/strings.py`); French when Android is in French, English otherwise; per-app choice (also in Android's settings from Android 13).

No layout of its own for foldables. The TV adds its computers itself (network, code, address): it does not receive the phone's.

## Image, refresh rate and game mode

- **Session mode**: “match this display”, at the highest refresh rate it holds; a requested rate the resolution cannot hold lowers the resolution (the Shield, HDMI 2.0: 4K at 60 Hz or 1080p at 120 Hz); brought down to what the decoder accepts (4096 wide at most in H.264). The computer's virtual display is created in this mode.
- **Display**: on the TV, the HDMI output takes the session's mode (`preferredDisplayModeId`, “Match the display” setting); on phones, the same resolution at the session's refresh rate (120 Hz must be requested: Android 15 keeps games at 60 Hz unless asked); the surface announces its frame rate (`Surface.setFrameRate`).
- **TV game mode**: `Window.setPreferMinimalPostProcessing(true)` (ALLM over HDMI) when the display allows it.
- **VRR**: Android gives apps no control over HDMI VRR and the Shield has none. On a variable refresh rate display (120 Hz phones and tablets), each frame goes out as soon as it is decoded, the newest replacing the others.
- **Decoding** (`jni/src/video.rs`): packets from patch 0001 go to MediaCodec (the format's hardware decoder, `low-latency`, real-time priority, the Qualcomm, Exynos and Amlogic vendor keys that turn off frame reordering, retried without them if refused), rendered straight to the surface. The parameter sets are put in front of the keyframe that starts a decoder (no `csd-0`, whatever the format); a loss waits for the next keyframe, requested again by the core; a new surface rebuilds the decoder. Profiles brought down to what the device really decodes (MediaCodecList at launch): the Shield decodes HEVC and H.264, not AV1 nor 4:4:4.
- **HDR**: impossible with Kyber 0.29 (8-bit capture), as in the other apps.

## Sound

Opus decoded by the core's engine (`apps/core/src/audio`), pulled straight by AAudio's real-time callback (`jni/src/audio.rs`), in low-latency mode and exclusive when possible, never through Java. Windows channel order (Android's). Auto follows the output: 5.1 or 7.1 to an HDMI amplifier, stereo elsewhere; an output that refuses multichannel gets stereo mixed down by the engine; an output that changes (headphones, HDMI) is reopened. Sound can stay on the computer. On the Shield over 100 Mbit/s Ethernet, at 4K and 60 Hz, sound loses packets.

## Rumble

The game's rumble reaches the core (`InputPlatform::rumble`) and is played on the player's controller (`Gamepads.rumble`):

- **Android 12 and later**: `InputDevice.getVibratorManager()`, two motors (big low motor on the left, small high one on the right, as in XInput).
- **Android 11 (Shield)**: `InputDevice.getVibrator()`, a single vibrator at the stronger of the two motors' intensities; many Bluetooth controllers do not vibrate there. Checked on the Shield 2019: the Xbox Wireless controller over Bluetooth shows “no rumble” (Android exposes no vibrator), while the computer does send Halo's rumble. Nothing to do on the app side: the Shield “tube” has no USB, and an app cannot talk directly to a controller paired with the system. The DualSense and the Shield controller have not been tried there yet.
- Without a duration, 5 s at most (as kynput on Mac); (0, 0) stops. The test screen says “2 motors”, “1 motor” or “no rumble” for each controller.
- Log (`adb logcat -s KyberNow`, `vibrations :` lines): first rumble received from the computer, no controller at that slot, number of vibrators Android exposes for the controller.

## Architecture

```
apps/android/
├── jni/                 kybernow-android crate → libkybernow.so (JNI bridge, MediaCodec, AAudio)
├── app/                 the app (Kotlin, Jetpack Compose)
│   └── src/main/java/fr/leslignesclaires/kybernow/
│       ├── core/        Native (JNI functions), SessionListener, ConsoleClient (Admin), DeviceIdentity, Share (KyberNow Share)
│       ├── model/       computers and passwords, discovery, controllers, decoders, display, sound, session mode
│       ├── ui/          brand theme, icons, home, adding, joining a share, settings, gamepad test
│       ├── session/     SessionActivity, SessionState, inputs, session interface, share panel, pointer, on-screen controller
│       ├── tv/          Android TV home row
│       └── update/      app updates (check, download, PackageInstaller)
└── scripts/             build-native.sh, release-apk.sh (published APK), strings.py (texts),
                         app-icon.py and brand-drawables.py (brand drawings)
```

- **Interface**: Jetpack Compose (Material 3) in the brand's colours and fonts; focus on the TV is Compose's own (`ui/Theme.kt`, `FocusCard` and its neon), without Compose for TV's components.
- **JNI bridge** (`jni/`, the Kotlin counterpart of `apps/apple`): Kotlin object `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow.core.Native`, core objects as `Long`, Admin replies in JSON (`{"code": 0, "value": …}`), the same session parameters as the Apple apps' `kn_session_start`, plus `combo`. Callbacks to Kotlin (`SessionListener`) go through a global reference; each core thread attaches to the JVM and works inside a local reference frame. The whole hot path (image, sound, input chain) stays in Rust.
- **VLC**: kyvlcplayer references libvlc, never called (image and sound go to the app): `jni/build.rs` compiles an empty function for each `libvlc_*` function of vlc-rs, as on iOS. The script lays down a `libvlc.pc` with no library so vlc-rs finds it.
- **Certificates**: the core always gives the Kyber certificate's fingerprint (the one Admin announces): kyclient checks it itself, without Android's verifier (`rustls-platform-verifier`), which is therefore not initialised.
- **Drawings**: the icon and the Android TV banner are the Apple TV's (the mark and its neon on ink, the Spectre glow and the faint seam), produced by `scripts/app-icon.py` with the drawing code of `apps/tvos/scripts/app-icon.py`; signed logo, mark and the icon's monochrome layer converted from `brand/v2`'s SVGs by `scripts/brand-drawables.py`, untouched. Variable Archivo and Martian Mono fonts from `brand/v2/fonts`.

## Building

```sh
# Once: Rust targets, cargo-c, NDK r27c (Android Studio › SDK Manager › SDK Tools › NDK (Side by side)
# 27.2.12479018, or ANDROID_NDK_HOME), Android SDK (platform 36), JDK 17 or later, cmake and Ninja (libopus).
rustup target add aarch64-linux-android armv7-linux-androideabi x86_64-linux-android
cargo install cargo-c --version 0.10.15 --locked

# Native library (after every change to apps/core or apps/android/jni):
apps/android/scripts/build-native.sh                     # all three ABIs
apps/android/scripts/build-native.sh --target aarch64-linux-android
apps/android/scripts/build-native.sh --check             # cargo check only; --debug: unoptimised

# The app:
cd apps/android
./gradlew assembleDebug        # app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
./gradlew assembleRelease      # signed when KYBERNOW_ANDROID_KEYSTORE (and its passwords) is given
                               # (the published APK: scripts/release-apk.sh, see below)
./gradlew lintDebug
./gradlew recordRoborazziDebug  # screenshots of the screens, no device needed → app/screenshots/
adb install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
```

`scripts/build-native.sh` applies the patches in `apps/macos/kyber-patches`, builds into `third_party/kyber-desktop/rootfs-<target>` libkynput without SDL (`cargo cinstall`) and libopus 1.6.1 (cmake and the NDK toolchain; sources from the Opus repository's `v1.6.1` tag, cloned into `third_party/opus-1.6.1`), lays down `libvlc.pc`, then builds `kybernow-android` with the NDK's clang for API 30 and copies `libkybernow.so`, without debug information, into `app/src/main/jniLibs/<abi>/` (outside git). Kyber 0.29's sources go in `third_party/kyber-desktop` (see [docs/build-notes.en.md](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)). Versions: Android Gradle Plugin 8.13, Gradle 8.14, Kotlin 2.4, Compose (BOM 2026.06); the app's version number is `apps/Cargo.toml`'s. Gradle also declares Compose for TV 1.0 (`androidx.tv:tv-material`), which no screen uses.

Screenshots (`app/src/test/.../Screenshots.kt`): Robolectric with native rendering and Roborazzi, with demonstration data and a fake computer image, without the native library; home (`HomeContent`), the computer's settings (`HostSettingsContent`) and the app's (`AppSettingsContent`), the controller test (`GamepadsContent`) and the session interface (`SessionOverlay`) take their data as parameters for this. Every screen in French and English, on phone, tablet and TV at 1080p, named `<screen>-<fr|en>-<size>.png`; the phone's settings pages are captured whole, on a screen tall enough for them. Endless animations (waiting, current step, background zoom) are switched off there (`KnTheme(motion = false)`): Robolectric would never reach idle and the capture would not finish; Material's progress indicator blocks the same way, hence the settings' own arc. Robolectric downloads an Android image on first run (`android-all-instrumented`, 200 MB, Maven Central).

Logic tests (`app/src/test`, `./gradlew testDebugUnitTest`): player slots (`PlayerSlotsTest`), the on-screen controller (sticks, double tap, triggers, D-pad, several fingers, simple layout, layouts that fit without overlapping: `OnScreenPadTest`), stick ticks (`StickZoneTest`), the tap that folds the keyboard away (`TouchInputTest`, Robolectric), mDNS reply (`MdnsLookupTest`), KyberNow Share (`ShareTest`, Robolectric: the core's answers sorted for the interface, roles, rights-change notices, tokens kept out of logs; `ShareUiTest`, Robolectric and Compose: “Share” unavailable, order of the actions on a guest, durations, a guest's bar and endings; `TouchInputTest`: a dropped gesture).

**Trying it on a device** (Shield or phone connected to Android Studio, USB or network debugging on): `git pull`, `apps/android/scripts/build-native.sh` if `apps/core` or `apps/android/jni` changed, then ▶ Run in Android Studio (project `apps/android`). Without the native library Gradle refuses to build (`libkybernow.so absente…`): an app installed without it would die at launch (`UnsatisfiedLinkError`). The script uses NDK 27.2.12479018; failing that, the newest installed one, with a warning. Log: `adb logcat -s KyberNow` (Rust core and app).

Texts: `scripts/strings.py` (both languages in one file, French typography included) writes `res/values*/strings.xml`. Drawings: `scripts/app-icon.py` (icon, banner; Pillow) and `scripts/brand-drawables.py`.

## Updates and publishing

The app updates itself from published releases (`KyberNow-Link-Android.apk`, checked against the signed manifest): app settings › Updates, stable or beta channel. The published APK is built and signed by `scripts/release-apk.sh`, which `scripts/release.sh` runs (`--check`: key and tools; `--fingerprint`: the certificate's fingerprint, the one in `scripts/release-cert.sha256`, which the APK must carry); signing key and backup: [docs/releases.en.md](../../docs/releases.en.md), “Android app signing key”. An app installed from Android Studio (debug key) cannot receive these updates: uninstall it once, then install the published APK (`adb install KyberNow-Link-Android.apk`).
