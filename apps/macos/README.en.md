# KyberNow Link for macOS

English · [Français](README.md)

KyberNow Link, KyberNow's streaming client: you get your computer (KyberNow Station: Kyber 0.29 server + KyberNow Admin) in full screen on the Mac, with image, sound, keyboard, mouse and gamepads. v2 identity ([guidelines](../../brand/v2/README.en.md)); mock-ups: [Claude Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/YGA7unwqztvuA26CMtdbv8) (home, first contact, transition, session). The video and audio code and the capability detection do not use AppKit: the Apple TV and iPhone/iPad apps ([apps/tvos](../tvos/README.en.md), [apps/ios](../ios/README.en.md)) reuse them as they are, along with the model (`Model.swift`) and Bonjour discovery (`Discovery.swift`). The few differences are behind `#if os(...)`; after a change to KyberKit, build the Apple TV and iPhone/iPad apps too.

## What the app does

- **Home** ([docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md)): the image of the computer shown full frame (very slow zoom, desaturated when offline) and, in this order, its state (“Online”, “Offline”, “Searching…” until Admin has answered once), its name in large, its system, the error that prevents getting in if there is one (password refused, Station reinstalled: a click opens the setting that fixes it), the display the session will ask for (`HomeScreen`, same calculation as the session, taking an unavailable virtual display into account), “Connect” (when offline: “Wake the computer” when the app knows one of the computer's network cards, otherwise “Try again”), the profile and its sentence, “Settings” (the computer's). At the bottom, the other computers (name and state; a click shows them, right-click: connect, settings), those announced on the network (“On your network”, Bonjour, they light up as they appear) and “Add a computer”. At the top: “Version {v} available” when Sparkle has found an update, and “App settings”. Online or not: Admin is polled every 8 s (30 s with the window closed, and right away when the menu bar menu opens); a refused account (401) or a changed certificate leaves the computer “Online” with its error (`HostStatus.problem`).
- **A computer's image** (`HostImagery`), in this order: the last image of its last session, photographed at the end (`SessionEngine.snapshot`, 1600 pixels wide, JPEG in `~/Library/Application Support/KyberNow/Images`), can be turned off per computer (“Keep the last image”, which deletes it); otherwise an up-to-date preview requested from Admin (`GET /api/stream/preview`, at most once a minute, arriving with a 45° sweep); otherwise its **signature**, two glows whose hue and position are drawn from Admin's fingerprint, with the huge symbol as a watermark.
- **Adding a computer**: on the local network (Admin's Bonjour announcement `_kybernow._tcp`; those marked `kyber=0`, an Admin with no Kyber server behind it, are not offered), by Admin's address and port, or by QR code (the Mac's camera) or the `kybernow://address:port?name=…&fp=sha256:…` link shown in Admin, on the Link devices page. Admin's certificate fingerprint is verified: automatically if the link or the announcement gives it, otherwise by eye (the first four groups). Then the account, verified with Admin and kept in the Keychain. Kyber's certificate itself is required at each session as Admin gives it (`kyber_fingerprint` from `prepare`), never remembered: reinstalling the computer, which changes it, does not block the app. With a Station older than 0.2.8, it is remembered on first contact (`~/Library/Application Support/kyber/known_hosts`), to be deleted if the computer was reinstalled. A `kybernow://` link opened elsewhere opens the add flow directly.
- **Computer settings** (“Settings for {name}”; in French, elided before a vowel or an h: « Réglages d'Atelier »): the profile at the top, Game, Desktop or Max (changing a setting makes it “Custom”); the six sections on the left, their cards on the right:
  - **Picture**: virtual display, resolution (match this display, if needed “4K at most”, window size, custom), refresh rate (this display's or 60 Hz), video format (HEVC, H.264, AV1), sharp text (4:4:4), frame display (synced or immediate), keep the last image, bitrate from 5 to 4,294 Mbit/s with warnings;
  - **Sound**: Auto, stereo, 5.1, 7.1, on the computer;
  - **Controls**: mouse (Auto, desktop mouse, game mouse), ⌘ sent as Ctrl, shared clipboard, controller seen by the computer (Xbox 360 or as is);
  - **Network**: picture transport (fast or reliable), reconnect automatically, wake on LAN (“Ready” and the MAC address learnt, or “No address yet: connect to this computer once.”);
  - **Statistics**: the “Last session” card (median in large, frame ruler from 0 to 20 ms with the 160, 120 and 60 Hz marks and a band from the median to the 95th percentile, “95% of frames under …”, duration and day, the session's resolution, refresh rate, format and bitrate) and “This computer” (system, graphics card, Admin's address, fingerprint; Station reinstalled: the fingerprint presented, to compare with Admin's before trusting it);
  - **Station**: its version (“Up to date” or “Version {v} available”), “Restart the Station”, “Update the Station” when the published version installs from a device (otherwise: it installs with KyberNow-Station-Setup.exe), “Open Admin” (in the browser, which asks for Admin's password), the account (“This device's own access” when Admin says so, or for a computer added here by code; a new password if it is refused), “Remove this computer…” with a confirmation. Restarting and updating go through Admin (the core's `kn_console_station_restart` and `_update`, [docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md)); a device in session asks for “Restart anyway”; the app then follows the Station by polling Admin every 2 s until it is back (3 min at most, 10 min for an update) and says “The Station has restarted.” or “KyberNow Station {v} is installed.”; otherwise “The Station is taking a while to come back…” or, back on the old version, “The update did not complete…”. A Station 0.4.9 or older does not allow it: the section says so.
  Each section has a simple summary under its name (“Match this display · 60 Hz”, “Fast”, “Yesterday”, “Up to date”). Following the Station, the texts shared by the Apple apps and the last session live in `LinkUI.swift` and `Model.swift`, also compiled by the Apple TV, iPhone and iPad.
- **Wake on LAN**: Admin gives the computer's network cards (MAC address, IPv4, broadcast) with each info; the app keeps them with the computer (an empty list keeps the previous one) and publishes them to iCloud with it. “Wake the computer” sends the packet through the core (subnet broadcast, 255.255.255.255 and direct), says “Waking the computer…” and polls the computer every 2 s, for 2 min at most, until it is online. It must also be allowed on the computer (Admin › System).
- **App settings** (KyberNow Link › Settings…, ⌘,; “App settings” on the home screen and in the menu bar menu): iCloud, startup (“Open at login”), updates, language, and “This Mac” (display, macOS scaling, hardware decoding, version, connected controller).
- **Menu bar** (`MenuBar.swift`, [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md), “Icon next to the clock”): the KyberNow Link mark as a template image (the “you” half filled, the “computer” half outlined; both filled during a session), a 14-point square on 18, placed on whole points to stay sharp at 16 and 32 pixels; tooltip “KyberNow Link”, in a session “KyberNow Link · in session on {name}”. A click opens the menu. In a session: “In session on {name}”, the live figures (the session bar's latency, frames per second, bitrate; before the picture, the connection step), “Full screen” (ticked in full screen), “Mouse” ▸ Auto, Desktop mouse, Game mouse (the computer's mode ticked; once picked, it applies at once and stays for that computer, as from the bar), “Statistics”, “Disconnect”. Then each computer, in the home screen's order (“{name} — Online”, “Offline”, “Searching…”; the session's one ticked), preceded by an 8-point dot (green online, red offline, amber while waking and while the Station restarts or updates, grey while searching: brand/v2's `--kn-ok`, `--kn-bad`, `--kn-warn`, `--kn-faint`, dark or light version following the menu's appearance, drawn each time it is shown rather than as a template image; `StatusDot`), with its submenu: “Connect ({profile})” (inactive during a session on another computer; “Disconnect” on the session's one), when offline “Wake the computer” (“Waking the computer…” while waking) or “Try again” (“Connect” inactive while the Station restarts or updates), “Profile” ▸ Game, Desktop, Max (“Custom” ticked and inactive when the settings differ from them; in a session, applied right away), “Settings…”, and when online “Open Admin”, “Restart the Station”, “Update the Station ({v})” (during the action: “The Station is restarting…”, “Updating the Station…”, inactive). Last, “Open KyberNow Link”, “Add a computer…”, “App settings…”, “Check for updates…”, “Quit KyberNow Link”. During a session, “Add a computer…” and a computer's “Settings…” are inactive: the window belongs to the session. `NSStatusItem` and `NSMenu` rather than `MenuBarExtra`: the menu is built each time it opens (the computers are polled at that moment) and updated in place every second while it stays open. Its commands are those of the home screen, the settings and the session (`AppModel.perform`, `AppLife.swift`); restarting or updating the Station asks for the same confirmation when a device is in a session (an app alert when that computer's Station section is not on screen), follows the Station the same way and ends with a notification.
- **Window, Dock and quitting** (`AppLife.swift`): closing the window (red button, ⌘W) hides it, the app carries on in the menu bar; during a session it means disconnecting, and the window hides once the session has ended (after leaving full screen). The first time, a notification says so: “KyberNow Link is still here”. The Dock icon is only there while a window is open (home or session, app settings, Sparkle's window; minimised in the Dock, it counts): activation policy `.regular` or `.accessory`, the Dock icon set before the window comes to the front. Reopening the app (Finder, Launchpad, Spotlight) or “Open KyberNow Link” shows the home screen. A single home window (no “New Window”): it also carries the session. “Quit KyberNow Link”, ⌘Q and the Dock quit; a session in progress ends first (Admin gives the computer back its displays and sound), 6 s at most.
- **Open at login** (App settings › Startup): the app itself as a login item (`SMAppService.mainApp`, `LoginItem`). Ticked on the first launch of the version that brings it, from an Applications folder (not a development build), once only (`openAtLoginDefault`); after that the setting reflects System Settings (read again each time the app comes back to the front) and the app never turns it back on by itself. Launched that way (open event marked as a login item, read in `applicationDidFinishLaunching`), the app starts with no window and no Dock icon.
- **Notifications** (`Notifications.swift`, `UNUserNotificationCenter`), only when the app is not in the foreground; permission is asked for at the first one. Station update ready (once per version and per computer, `stationUpdatesAnnounced`), with “Update” (no button when the version installs with KyberNow-Station-Setup.exe); “{name} is online” after “Wake the computer”, with “Connect”, or “{name} is still not responding” after 2 min; session stopped without “Disconnect”: by Admin or another device (“Take back control”), or by an error, with its reason (“Resume”); end of a restart or an update started from the menu or a notification (the Station section's closing texts). A click opens the app on what it announces: the home screen on the computer, or its Station section.
- **Last session**: at the end of each session of at least 3 s (`Host.recordSession`), the app keeps for the computer the median and 95th percentile of the latency, the duration, the date, and the requested resolution, refresh rate, format and bitrate; a session cut off by an error after the picture counts too. Computers saved by an earlier version read back as they are (new fields optional).
- **Bitrate**: a profile's bitrate is set for 4K at 60 Hz and follows the session's number of pixels per second (power 0.75, between 0.4 and 3 times). For example, the Max profile at 300 Mbit/s starts at about 460 Mbit/s in 5K at 60 Hz. A bitrate set by hand stays as it is. The settings show the real bitrate.
- **Pointer**: the Mac's pointer takes the shape of the computer's, at the size of a Mac pointer. The Windows cell (32 pixels at 100%, 48 at 150%, 64 at 200%) is brought down to 32 points, and the hotspot follows the same scale. A hide, or a fully transparent shape (video players), never makes it disappear: it keeps the last shape.
- **Nothing stays pressed**: when the window loses the keyboard (⌘Tab, ⌘Space, Spotlight, another app in front), the app releases on the computer what it holds there (keys, modifiers, mouse buttons) and forgets its own state. No kynput `flush_keys` here: it would also release the gamepads, which, read by SDL, keep talking to the computer without the window and would not send their state again (it is still sent at the end of the session). When the window gets the keyboard back, it sends again the modifiers really held at that moment (`NSEvent.modifierFlags`, ⌘ still down after a ⌘Tab); a released modifier is released on the computer whichever side (left or right) held it. Caps Lock: sent as press + release, like a keystroke, never held.
- **Refused packets** (the core's `PacketQueue`, `apps/core/src/input/packet_queue.rs`): kynput's send queue holds 64 packets and drops without notice, while the protocol is made of deltas (a dropped release = a key stuck until the end of the session). The app counts the refusals (log `entrées : … refusé`, then the total at the end of the session). Packets that cannot catch up on their own (keys, clicks, buttons, gamepad plugs and unplugs, an axis back to rest, text, `flush_keys`) are retried every 3 ms from a small fallback queue, in order, for up to 200 ms; the others (positions, moves, wheel, moving axes) are sent once, and not at all while the fallback queue is not empty (they would overtake what is waiting: an axis back to rest, then its new value). Gamepads read by SDL do not go through it (kynput sends them itself): the router hook (`gamepad_gate.rs`) only discards axes that have not moved (by 1/512 of the travel), which quiets a chatty Bluetooth gamepad.
- **Apple ISO keyboard** (the core's `keymap.rs`, `KeymapMac`): on a European Apple keyboard (one more key next to left ⇧), the Mac reports the key below Escape (`@#` on an AZERTY, `§±` elsewhere) as `kVK_ISO_Section`, HID usage 0x64, and the one next to ⇧ (`<>`) as `kVK_ANSI_Grave`, usage 0x35: the opposite of what the computer expects (without the swap, `@` gives `<`: checked on an AZERTY MacBook; `hid-apple` quirk, SDL does the same swap). Both usages are swapped when the keyboard type of the event (`kCGKeyboardEventKeyboardType`, then `KBGetLayoutType`) is ISO; an ANSI keyboard plugged in at the same time keeps its keys.
- **Mouse: Auto, desktop mouse or game mouse** (settings, session bar, ⌃⌥⌘M to switch from one to the other). Below, “game mode” is the game mouse.
  - In game mode, the computer's pointer, when it is visible (menus, inventories), is drawn by the app at the position the computer announces (server with `kyber-patches/0006`, 60 positions per second). The shape is only rebuilt if it changes.
  - In game mode, the Mac's pointer is frozen and hidden, and only raw movements are sent (GCMouse, without macOS acceleration; AppKit movements without GCMouse). The mouse becomes absolute again, pointer put back in the same place, when the bar opens or when the window loses focus.
  - Auto (the default) switches to game mode when a game is in full screen on the computer and hides its pointer. Admin says so (`GET /api/stream/foreground`, queried every second): foreground window covering the whole screen, or DirectX exclusive full screen. Browsers, video players, slideshows and video calls in full screen do not count. A pointer shown again for less than 0.6 s (menus, Windows flicker) does not make it leave game mode.
  - The pointer being hidden is not enough on its own to trigger game mode: on a computer with no mouse plugged in, Windows hides it and shows it again every half-second, even on the desktop.
- **Introduction to Admin**: at the start of a session, the app gives Admin a stable identifier (drawn at first launch), the Mac's name, its system, its version and the profile; then its median latency with each heartbeat, and that of the whole session at the end. Admin builds its known devices and its event feed from this. If Admin asks for the session to end (“End the session”, in Admin or the Station's tray icon), the app learns it at the next heartbeat and disconnects as with “Disconnect”.
- **Shared clipboard**: text, both ways, up to 60 KB (the core's `clipboard.rs`, through `InputCore`; kynput protocol, C functions of `kyber-patches/0002`). What the computer announces is requested immediately, so that ⌘V works straight away; what the Mac copies is announced (checked twice a second) and sent on request. Admin can forbid it in one direction or the other.
- **iCloud for the Apple TV, iPhone and iPad** (`CloudPublisher`): each time a computer is added, changed or removed, the app publishes the list to the iCloud key-value store (key `hosts`: name, address, Admin port, account, stream port, fingerprint, and `from` = this Mac's name) and copies the password to the iCloud Keychain. The Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps on the same account add them without typing anything. Computers published by another Mac stay in the list. “Sync with iCloud” setting in KyberNow Link › Settings… (⌘,), on by default; turning it off removes this Mac's computers from the list and from the iCloud Keychain (the devices keep those they already added). It needs the iCloud entitlements, hence a provisioning profile, in place for Debug and Developer ID builds alike (see “Signing and iCloud”).
- **Updates**: Sparkle, daily check and the KyberNow Link › Check for updates… menu (also in the menu bar menu); the website's cumulative feed, `https://www.kybernow.app/updates/appcast.xml` (every release, EdDSA-signed archives, see [docs/releases.en.md](../../docs/releases.en.md)). Betas are only offered if “Get beta versions” is ticked in KyberNow Link › Settings… (`receiveBetaUpdates` in `UserDefaults`; the Sparkle delegate in `KyberNowApp.swift` then asks for the `beta` channel); a stable release newer than an installed beta is offered normally.
- **Bundle name**: `KyberNow Link.app` (`PRODUCT_NAME`; the Swift module stays `KyberNow`). Sparkle updates the bundle in place without renaming it: an app installed as `KyberNow.app` (0.4.2 and earlier) renames itself at launch, before Sparkle starts, then relaunches (`BundleRename` in `KyberNowApp.swift`). Only in a writable Applications folder and if the name is free; otherwise it keeps its name.
- **Choosing the display**: with the virtual display, the app waits at most 6 s, only once, for Kyber to announce only that display, then takes the display at the requested size placed at (0, 0). Outputs that Windows lists as 0 × 0 (off) are left out; if no usable display remains, the session says so instead of staying on “First frames”.
- **Gamepads**: those SDL handles (Xbox, DualSense, DualShock…). Per-computer setting “Gamepad seen by the computer”: **Xbox 360** (default), they all become Xbox 360 gamepads on the computer, the ones every game recognises; or **Faithful**, a Sony gamepad stays a DualShock 4 (touchpad, light bar for the games that use them), the others stay Xbox 360. The choice goes with the session (`gamepad_force_xbox360`) and overrides Admin's “all gamepads as Xbox 360” option, which only remains a default for other devices.
- **Gamepad combination** (L1 + R1 + Start + Select held for 250 ms; a brief press of the four buttons stays with the game, like on the Apple TV): the bar opens and the gamepad drives it (d-pad, A, B); same combination or bar closed, it goes back to the computer. The session controller is what takes the gamepad, not the layer that reads the packets (the core's `gamepad_gate.rs` hook, which only reports the combination): outside a session (connecting, reconnecting, ending), the combination does nothing and the gamepad stays with the computer. When the bar opens, everything is reset on the computer: held buttons released (trigger included) and the six axes at rest, for every gamepad the packet layer has seen; those releases are the only gamepad packets that leave while the bar holds the gamepad. When the picture restarts (profile change from the bar), the bar keeps the gamepad.
- **Reconnection**: during a Kyber reconnection, the app waits: it is the app that restarts the image once Kyber is back. If Kyber restarted on the computer, the virtual display may have gone with it (it always keeps the same number, but Admin removes it and turns the physical displays back on). So the app asks Admin for it again (`/api/stream/prepare`, no effect if it is still there), waits at most 3 s until Kyber announces only that display, then restarts the image on it. If the computer's image pipeline fails again within 15 s, it makes this request once more instead of cutting the session. The heartbeat also says whether the virtual display is in place (`active`): if it disappeared during the session, the app asks for it again (at most once every 30 s). When the app restarts the image itself (no keyframe within 1.5 s), Kyber closes the data connection and reconnects without restarting the image: the app then restarts it itself, instead of staying on a frozen image.
- **Image continuity** (see [docs/archives/audit-2026-10-20.md](../../docs/archives/audit-2026-10-20.md) §15):
  - *Computer mode changed* (a game switching to 1080p, a Windows setting): with the txproto engine, the image goes on at the new size (encoder recreated, decoder rebuilt on the new SPS/PPS, rendering recentred). The input coordinate space follows a change in the size of the decoded frames (the core, from the size `SessionEngine` reports with each renderer measurement, once per second; the first frame does not count, it may be scaled) or in the size Kyber's list gives to the same display (compared with the previous list): the latest change wins, and the list always corrects a scaled image (with libavconv, the image keeps its starting size and only the list tells the real one). The bar, the bitrate and the followed display take the new size, and a capsule says “The computer switched to 1920 × 1080”. The app tells Admin what to do with a mode change (`mode_policy` in `prepare`): Game lets it be (`follow`), Desktop has it put back (`restore`), the other profiles leave it to Admin's setting (`default`, said explicitly to undo a previous profile's choice). If “Restore” changed nothing after 6 s, the button comes back; it is never sent after the session has ended. In the Desktop profile, the bar offers “Restore 3840 × 2160” (a `prepare` at the starting mode, `restore` policy).
  - *Image pipeline error on the computer* (`pipelineError`, for example libavconv's encoder not surviving the mode change) outside the 15 s following a reconnection: the app restarts the image (stop, then start on the best display of the updated list) one second later, three times at most (a planned restart is cancelled by a reconnection, which restarts the image itself), before ending the session; the count starts over after 30 s without a restart. The capsule says “The picture was restarted”. A restart also restarts the inputs on Kyber's side (1 to 2 s). An image refused at start (`streamingStartFailed`) ends the session with its reason: Kyber disconnects by itself after this failure, and a new attempt would announce the image with no connection (black screen).
  - *Shown display gone* (HDMI unplugged, virtual display recreated): the image restarts on the replacement display, chosen in this order: the one picked in the bar, the only display left, **the same display back under another number** (same place and same size; Kyber knows the displays' names, but kyclient does not pass them to the apps), the one at the mode obtained from Admin placed at (0, 0), a display at the obtained mode, the display placed at (0, 0), the first one. A number missing from the latest list is never reused: when the image starts, a display that disappeared in the meantime is replaced by that same choice.
  - *This Mac's screen changed* (external display plugged in or unplugged, resolution or refresh rate changed in System Settings, window moved to another screen): the app listens to `NSApplication.didChangeScreenParametersNotification` and to the window's screen change, waits 0.8 s for the screens to settle, then, with “Match this display” and the virtual display, compares the mode of the screen showing the session (real pixels and refresh rate, `DisplayInfo.mode`) with the one requested from Admin. If it differs, a capsule offers “The screen changed: fit the picture to 2560 × 1440?” with **Fit** and **Later**; it fades after 15 s and the bar keeps “Fit to 2560 × 1440” as long as the modes differ (back to the requested mode, the offer goes away). Nothing changes without that click. **Fit** stops the image, redoes `prepare` at the new mode (Admin recreates the virtual display), waits up to 6 s for Kyber to announce it, then restarts the image as after a reconnection; the capsule says “Picture fitted to 2560 × 1440”. If Admin refuses, the old mode stays the one to request again and the capsule says so. Full screen on an unplugged display: if macOS leaves the window at the old screen's size or place, the app leaves full screen, puts the window on the main screen and goes full screen there again; a window left off every screen is re-centred.
  - *Inputs after a restart*: Kyber restarts its input service with the image (`stop_streaming` + `start_streaming`), and the new one holds nothing. On `INPUT_SERVICE_CONNECTED`, the view reasserts the mouse mode (the new chain starts absolute: in game mode, it goes back to relative as soon as it is created) and sends the modifiers still held on the Mac (⌘, ⇧, ⌥, ⌃, on the right side). Other keys and mouse buttons are not sent again: a game would see a new press.
  - *Heartbeat*: it carries the current profile's policy (`mode_policy`, the profile may have changed since `prepare`) and Admin answers with the virtual display's current mode (`mode`), the number of active displays (`displays`) and the policy in force. With the `restore` policy, Admin puts the mode back itself; if the heartbeat still reports another mode (older Station, change refused), the app asks for the starting mode again (`prepare`), at most once every 30 s. With `follow`, nothing.
- **Panels over the image**: the statistics panel, which stays open, has an opaque background. Frosted glass (`ultraThinMaterial`) would be recomputed by macOS at each frame of the stream (160 per second in 4K) and makes the frame rate drop. The bar, opened briefly, keeps its glass. Starting an image always stops the previous one, input chain included: two chains would fight over the gamepad (SDL only serves one), and the old one, unplugged, would reject everything.
- **Transition and session**: the session plays in the home window. The home screen is photographed and cut in two along the 45° seam; a neon tube lights up in it during preparation (capsule: preparing, connecting, first frames, Cancel), then the two halves move apart onto the computer's image and the window goes full screen (`Passage.swift`, Core Animation). For the duration of the session, a window delegate hides the menu bar and the Dock in full screen and passes the rest on to SwiftUI's. The connection capsule shows its three steps (“Preparing the display”, “Connecting”, “First frames”) and “Cancel”; a failure says “The connection failed” and the reason. If the picture does not come despite the session's own repairs, the core restarts the Station once and connects again: the capsule says “The Station is restarting…” instead of the steps, then resumes them. From the first frame, the picture alone: no arrival capsule, no bar that shows itself, no figures. Bar from the pointer, in glass: it opens when the pointer stays 0.25 s against the top edge of the picture (2 points at most), in a centred zone as wide as the bar (measured by SwiftUI, 1,020 points before the first measurement), and only if the app is in the foreground, the session window is key, the pointer is on the picture (the key window also receives the movements of a pointer on another display: `StreamView` checks the position), the mouse is in desktop mode and no mouse button is held (a computer window dragged against the top edge); passing quickly, leaving the picture (`mouseExited`), another display, the app losing the foreground or the game mouse cancel the wait (a `SessionController` timer, since a motionless pointer sends nothing). It closes 0.4 s after the pointer has left it (its measured rectangle, 12 points of margin, and the space between it and the top edge), never while the controller drives it or while one of its menus is open. The measured rectangles (bar, statistics close button, “adapt” capsule) are in the session view's frame (SwiftUI's global space), that of `ChromeHostingView`'s hit test and of the pointer position: a space named on the composition starts below the safe area (32 points windowed, hidden title bar) and would shift everything. Inside: lit symbol, live statistics (latency and its curve; a click opens the “Statistics” panel), resolution and format, profile, sound, mouse, clipboard, the computer's display (without a virtual display), full screen, “Disconnect”. Capsules only appear on a session event (picture relaunched, mode changed, this Mac's display changed, first switch to the game mouse). Clean end, checking that the computer got its displays and its sound back; the halves close on the last image, then the home screen comes back.
- **v2 guidelines** (`Theme.swift`): ink and frost, Spectre signal `#9A8CFF` that only lights up as neon in active moments (standby on hover, flash on click, lit during a connection or a live measurement, ignition on appearance), turned off with “Reduce transparency”; variable Archivo (width 125 for titles) and Martian Mono; Phosphor icons in `Assets.xcassets/Icons` (`scripts/phosphor-icons.mjs`); app icon in Icon Composer format (`KyberNow/AppIcon.icon`: the two halves of the symbol in “Liquid Glass”, the seam's neon on top, ink background), produced from the `brand/v2` geometry by `scripts/app-icon.py` and editable in Icon Composer.

## Image and sound

Kyber normally draws the image and plays the sound with VLC. KyberNow Link decodes them itself (H.264, HEVC, AV1, Opus) for latency and sync: `kyber-patches/0001` adds to libkyclient an output of the packets to the app (see [docs/kyber-changes.en.md](../../docs/kyber-changes.en.md)).

**End-to-end latency and the path of a frame** (the Rust core, `apps/core/src/latency.rs`, through `SessionEngine`, Mac, Apple TV, iPhone and iPad)
- Kyber's metrics turned on: the server timestamps each frame at each step (capture, start and end of encoding, sending), and Kyber's pings give the clock offset (median of the last 15).
- The renderer reports the moment each frame is received, decoded and shown, brought onto Kyber's `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` clock.
- Latency = display − (capture − offset), median of each second, in the bar. The session's median goes on the home screen.
- Steps, medians of each second: capture → encoding, encoding, sending, network (sending → complete frame received), decoding, display (decoded → on screen, sync included).
- Late measurements: the server may timestamp a frame after the Mac has shown it (mostly without vertical sync). The instants of a frame shown without a measurement (received, decoded, shown) are kept for one second and the latency is computed when the measurement arrives, instead of being dropped. The statistics panel shows the share of shown frames that have a measured latency (“Measurements: 97% of frames”) and the number of rejected frames (measurement never came, or absurd value): a median computed on few frames would be optimistic. The counters start from zero at each stream start (`streamingStarted`, also after a reconnection: the `pts` start again from zero).
- Losses: a frame sent by the computer that is neither shown nor replaced by a more recent one within the second is lost (missing packets, or discarded while waiting for a keyframe). A frame the display drops in favour of a more recent one (160 fps stream on a 120 Hz display) counts as replaced. Added to these are gaps in the stream, keyframes requested again, and packets lost according to Kyber (`NETWORK_LOCAL` / `NETWORK_REMOTE` totals, every few seconds, with the network round trip).

**Image** (`VideoDecoder`, `VideoRenderer`, `VideoPipeline`)
- VideoToolbox, hardware decoder required (software as a last resort), real-time mode, one frame per call, no queue. Asynchronous decoding: Kyber's network thread (the only one, which also reads QUIC and delivers the sound) gets control back as soon as the frame is handed to VideoToolbox, instead of waiting 1 to 3 ms per frame (20 to 60 ms at peaks); the decoded frame comes out on a VideoToolbox thread and goes to the renderer. A frame older than the last one delivered is ignored (`pts` monotonicity guard).
- Lost decoding session (−12785 after sleep, −12911 after a GPU change): recreated right away (50 ms) from the output handler, then a keyframe is requested, instead of waiting for the stream restart (2 to 3 s). Recovery (`onRecovered`) is only reported once the keyframe has actually been decoded.
- Output in the Apple silicon lossless compressed formats (`&8v0`, `&8v4`), read without copying by Metal (`CVMetalTextureCache`). YCbCr → RGB conversion in the shader, according to the matrix announced by the stream (709, 601, 2020) and its range.
- Display: always the most recent frame. Each frame leaves for the display as soon as it is decoded; Core Animation shows the most recent one at the refresh and discards the others (counted as replaced). The “one frame in flight” guard (frame N+1 waits until the display has shown N: a whole refresh of latency, 8 ms at 120 Hz) is only there for comparison, with `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1`. On a variable refresh rate display (ProMotion, Adaptive-Sync), the display follows the arrival of frames. Vertical sync can be turned off: lowest latency, possible tearing.
- Recovery after a loss: when packets are lost beyond what error correction can repair, VideoToolbox refuses the frames that rely on the lost reference. With the encoder's progressive refresh, no keyframe arrives by itself. The app then keeps the last image and stops sending unusable frames to the decoder. It requests a keyframe from the computer (`kyclient_client_request_keyframe`, server with patch 0005); the core repeats the request every 300 ms (`kn_session_keyframe_lost`) and the app starts again from the keyframe. With no answer within 1.5 s (computer not up to date), it restarts the stream, which always begins with a keyframe. Inside Kyber the request runs off the main loop, one at a time and given up after one second: a computer that stops answering delays neither the stop nor the reconnection.
- Decoder lifetime: one decoder and one sound player per stream (the core numbers the streams), kept until Kyber's last call for that stream (`KN_PACKET_CLOSED`, patch `kyber-patches/0001`). `SessionEngine`, the callbacks' `userdata`, and the core's session stay alive 30 s after the end: Kyber can still deliver a packet after the stop, and releasing them earlier would crash the app on disconnection.
- Initial keyframe lost: a heavy stream (4K at a high refresh rate in 4:4:4) can lose its whole first keyframe, with no gap reported by Kyber. The following frames arrive without a reference: the decoder then requests a keyframe as after a loss, instead of waiting silently.
- Fallback: if no frame is shown 4 s after the stream starts, or if keyframe requests go unanswered before the first frame, the app lightens the stream for this session only (bitrate brought down to 250 Mb/s, then 4:2:0), says so at the top of the screen and notes it in the log (`pas d'image : repli …`, `core` category). Changing profile in the bar starts again from the chosen setting.
- Compositing: the Metal layer is the session surface's own layer (`MetalSurface`, a layer-hosting view), opaque, full frame, with no sublayer or transform; successive renderers (one per stream) draw into the same layer. During the stream, the SwiftUI layer (bar, hints, statistics panel) is removed from compositing as soon as it has nothing to show, and the passage (home split in two) 1.4 s after the reveal: in full screen nothing is composited over the image and macOS can present it directly, bypassing the compositor (one refresh less). The bar's marker line (white capsule at 35 %, 220 × 3 points at screen scale, centred at the top of the view) is not in the SwiftUI layer: `VideoRenderer` draws it in the image's Metal pass (`topMarker` option, off by default and never turned on by Apple TV, iPhone and iPad; alpha blending, pipeline compiled separately, a failure is logged and the session goes on without the line), so it shows with direct presentation. `SessionController` turns it on when the gesture is possible (stream running, bar closed, app in the foreground, `appActive` followed through the activation notifications, desktop mouse) and off otherwise; the displayed image is then redrawn in place, without waiting for a new image. The end-of-session photograph does not have the line. The bar and panels stay in the window rather than in a child window: a window on top would hold the clicks and movements the statistics panel lets through to the computer and those that close the bar, and the result is the same while they are hidden. To check in a session with `MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1` (see below): bar hidden, the HUD should report direct presentation; bar open or the computer's pointer drawn in game mode, it becomes composited again.
- Measuring the display: `KN_PRESENT_STATS=1 "/Applications/KyberNow Link.app/Contents/MacOS/KyberNow Link"` writes a `présentation : …` line to the log (category `video`) every second with the frames rendered, those the display confirms it showed, those Core Animation discarded in favour of a newer one (`presentedTime == 0`), and the commit → screen delay (median and 95th percentile), plus the state of vertical sync and of the guard. `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1` puts back the “one frame in flight” guard (frame N+1 waits until the display has shown N) for comparison. What to read: without the guard, the commit → screen delay should fall towards half a refresh interval (≈ 4 ms at 120 Hz) and discarded frames appear as soon as the stream exceeds the display's refresh rate, which is expected; with the guard, the delay is a whole interval. `MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1` (same command) shows the Metal HUD over the image: Metal does not say publicly whether presentation is direct (the image goes to the display without the compositor) or composited, only the HUD shows it.
- Per-stream and per-frame costs: the Metal pipeline (shader) is compiled once per process, off the main thread at launch, together with the decoder probe (`DecoderCapabilities.prepare()`); compiled for each stream, it would cost 30 to 150 ms on the main thread. Parameter sets are keyed by (type, identifier) read from the header: two alternating PPS do not rebuild the session at each frame. The colour matrix is read once per second or per format change, not at each frame (`CVBufferCopyAttachment`). The Metal texture cache is flushed every second.
- Occluded window (another space, minimised in the Dock, covered, `NSWindow.occlusionState`): no rendering (the decoder keeps going, references stay good; `nextDrawable` would wait up to 1 s per frame) and bitrate brought down to 20 Mb/s; on return, rendering and the profile's bitrate come back (log `fenêtre occultée …` / `visible …`).
- AV1 (M3 chips and later): the sequence header is read bit by bit to build the `av1C` description VideoToolbox expects. Samples are stripped of temporal delimiters, and each OBU carries its size (`AV1.swift`). Measured on an M4 Max at 2560 × 1440: 1 ms decoding. 4:4:4 is not offered in AV1: Apple's decoders only read the Main profile (4:2:0), a 4:4:4 stream (High profile) would not open and every frame would be lost until the fallback. The app has no fallback player: AV1 is only offered to Macs that decode it in hardware.

**Sound** (the Rust core's sound engine, `apps/core/src/audio`; the Core Audio output in `AudioPlayerCore`)
- libopus (multistream: stereo, 5.1, 7.1), loss concealment by Opus. Sound travels unreliably with error correction (`UNRELIABLE_FEC`, Kyber's default): a packet lost beyond the correction is concealed by Opus instead of being retransmitted. In reliable mode, retransmissions build up delay on Wi-Fi and concealment is never used. The computer must announce its packet size (server patch 0008).
- Core Audio output (`DefaultOutput` on the Mac, `RemoteIO` on iOS/tvOS), 5 ms requests to the sound card, up to 4,096 frames per request accepted (Bluetooth, AirPlay: otherwise the unit would refuse and go silent).
- Buffer of about twenty milliseconds, against a fixed 50 ms with VLC: its margin grows by 5 ms after an empty buffer, goes down by one millisecond every two seconds without a gap. An empty buffer is filled with an Opus concealment frame (a plausible splice) rather than silence, and playback resumes as soon as the margin is there (the first start waits for the margin plus one packet). The drift between the computer's and the Mac's clocks is caught up smoothly: one sample skipped or doubled out of 256 (0.4 %, one out of 128 beyond 6 ms of excess) until the buffer's lowest level is back at the margin, instead of a hard cut; only a lead of more than 10 ms (start, output left stopped) is cut at once.
- Output followed during the session (`kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultOutputDevice`): AirPods connected or an HDMI display chosen, the unit is recreated on the new output, its latency and effective buffer size read again (log `son : sortie changée …`), the sound-image offset stays right. Fewer channels at the output than in the stream (5.1 or 7.1 to headphones): downmix, L + 0.7 C + 0.7 surround L (and rear L), same on the right, LFE dropped (an index mapping would send the centre into the right ear). 7.1 layout (Vorbis order: L, C, R, side L, side R, rear L, rear R, LFE): sides `LeftSurround`/`RightSurround`, rears `RearSurroundLeft`/`RearSurroundRight`, as in Core Audio's MPEG 7.1 C layout. To check with a channel identification clip on a 7.1 amplifier.
- Sound and image each leave as soon as they are ready. The bar shows the offset (sound = buffer + output latency, image = reception → screen), imperceptible below 45 ms. Bluetooth headphones add 150 to 250 ms on the sound side.

**Machine capabilities** (`DecoderCapabilities`, `DisplayInfo`)
- At launch, off the main thread: H.264, HEVC and AV1 hardware decoders, plus a real decode of a 64 × 64 HEVC 4:4:4 frame, requiring hardware (20 to 80 ms), and the Metal pipeline compiled.
- The profiles are brought down to them: without hardware 4:4:4, 4:2:0 with a third more bitrate; without HEVC, H.264 with half as much again. The settings grey out what the machine cannot decode.
- Display: the panel's real pixels, taken from its display modes (native mode), up to 7680 × 4320, and 4096 wide at most in H.264 (NVENC limit). In “more space” mode, macOS draws larger than the panel then scales down: a 4K display set to 3008 × 1692 points draws 6016 × 3384, and those extra pixels are not requested from the computer. “4K at most” option, maximum refresh rate, variable refresh rate or not.

Live measurements (session bar): end-to-end latency and its curve, resolution and format. A click on them (or ⌃⌥⌘S) opens the **detailed measurements** at the bottom right (`SessionStats.swift`): the path of a frame as a cascade on a common scale, with the duration of one frame as a dotted line; latency (losses marked under the curve) and bitrate over 30 s; losses over 30 s and since the start; network round trip, image, sound and the sound-image offset. The panel stays open and lets clicks and movements through to the computer, except its close button. In Debug, `KN_STATS_PREVIEW=1` shows it filled with plausible measurements, without a session.

Flow of a session: it lives in the Rust core (`apps/core/src/session.rs`, see [docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md)), driven by `SessionEngine` through the C API of `apps/apple`: preparation by Admin (virtual display at this Mac's size, sound with the right number of channels) → connection to Kyber → wait for the display list to settle → image → heartbeat every few seconds → end (the computer gets its displays and its audio output back; report in the `ended` state). The core relaunches the image (lost keyframe, error on the computer, display gone), asks for the virtual display again after a reconnection, lightens the stream when no image comes within 4 s (bitrate, then 4:2:0), follows the size of the computer's display and offers “Put back”. `SessionController` keeps only the interface: window, transition, bar, gamepad, capsules, measurements. Swift gives the core the view, one decoder and renderer per stream (`VideoPipeline`), the sound output (`AudioPlayerCore`, which pulls samples from the core's sound engine), the input capture (into the core's input chain, `InputCore`) and tells it about every frame shown (latency on Kyber's clock).


### Inputs

On Mac, inputs go through the Rust core's input module (`apps/core/src/input`, see [apps/core/README.en.md](../core/README.en.md)), through the C API of `apps/apple` (`kn_input_*`, `kn_pointer_*`, `kn_keymap_*`): kynput chain and retry backlog, keyboard (scancodes, “⌘ sent as Ctrl”, ISO swap, lock keys), release all and held modifiers, the computer's pointer and the Auto / Desktop / Game rules, clipboard, and the SDL gamepads (combination held 250 ms and capture by the bar, through a kynput router hook: `gamepad_gate.rs`). `InputCore.swift` keeps the names `StreamView` and `SessionEngine` use (`InputPipeline`, `PointerLayer`, `Keymap`, `MouseModeRules`). The Mac provides the AppKit capture, the pointer drawing, the kVK → HID table and the ISO layout (`KeymapMac`), and `NSPasteboard` through callbacks. Gamepad capture: presses and moving axes stay with the app, releases and axes at rest always go through to the computer.

## Building

Prerequisites: Xcode 27, XcodeGen, Rust 1.89 + `cargo-c`, the Kyber 0.29 client built in `third_party/kyber-desktop` (see [docs/build-notes.en.md](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)). The app targets macOS 14 and later, on Apple silicon (arm64).

```bash
apps/macos/scripts/vendor-kyber.sh      # Kyber SDK (headers, libraries, VLC plugins) → Vendor/
apps/macos/scripts/build-kyclient.sh    # libkyclient and libkynput with the KyberNow patches → Vendor/
apps/apple/scripts/build-apple.sh       # the Rust core (libkybernow, CKyberNow header) → Vendor/
cd apps/macos && xcodegen generate
xcodebuild -project KyberNow.xcodeproj -scheme KyberNow -configuration Debug -derivedDataPath build build
open "build/Build/Products/Debug/KyberNow Link.app"
```

Run `build-kyclient.sh` again after each `vendor-kyber.sh`: without it, the app picks up libraries without the patches. Run `build-apple.sh` again after each change to `apps/core` or `apps/apple` (it refuses to run while the app is open). When `Vendor/` changes, quit the app before rebuilding: VLC would crash while loading modules rewritten under its feet (see [build-notes](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)). A rebuild without a change to `Vendor/` does not touch the app's libraries. Signing (`project.yml`, team P6DB682S4K): in Debug, the “Apple Development: info@leslignesclaires.fr” certificate, the same from one build to the next, which keeps the Keychain from asking again for access to the password; in Release, “Developer ID Application”, hardened runtime and timestamp, for notarisation (`scripts/release.sh` at the repository's root).

### Signing and iCloud

Publishing for the Apple TV, iPhone and iPad requires two entitlements, `com.apple.developer.ubiquity-kvstore-identifier` (`$(TeamIdentifierPrefix)fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, the key-value store shared with their apps) and `keychain-access-groups` (`$(AppIdentifierPrefix)fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, data protection keychain). They live in `KyberNow/KyberNow.entitlements`, along with the camera. On macOS these entitlements need an embedded provisioning profile, otherwise the app is killed at launch: in Debug, the “macOS App Development” profile `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` (this Mac, info@leslignesclaires.fr certificate); in Release, the “Developer ID” profile `P6DB682S4K.fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` (all Macs, valid until 2044). Both must be installed on the Mac that builds, otherwise Xcode refuses to sign. The app also checks the entitlements at runtime (`CloudPublisher.entitled`): signed without them, it publishes nothing and says so.

Setup (profiles created on 2026-10-20), to redo for a new build Mac or an expired profile, on [developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/account/resources):

1. App ID `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`: the iCloud capability is already there (created for the Apple TV, key-value store included); nothing more to tick, the Keychain group `P6DB682S4K.fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` comes from the identifier itself.
2. Devices: register this Mac (Provisioning UDID in System Information › Hardware; or let Xcode do it from Settings › Accounts).
3. Profiles: “macOS App Development” for `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, “Apple Development: info@leslignesclaires.fr” certificate, this Mac; “Developer ID” (Mac) for the same identifier, Developer ID Application certificate. For Xcode to find them, copy each downloaded file to `~/Library/Developer/Xcode/UserData/Provisioning Profiles/<UUID>.provisionprofile` (double-clicking is not enough on macOS).
4. Their names go in `project.yml` (`PROVISIONING_PROFILE_SPECIFIER`, per configuration), then `xcodegen generate`.

Xcode puts the profile in the app (`Contents/embedded.provisionprofile`) and adds the App ID and team to its entitlements. `scripts/sign-release.sh` re-signs the app with the entitlements Xcode gave it (App ID and team included), not with the `.entitlements` file: the Developer ID release keeps the profile and its entitlements through notarisation. The local Keychain item (service `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`) does not change: the signature stays the same, and so does access to the password.

## Apple TV, iPhone and iPad

The Apple TV ([apps/tvos](../tvos/README.en.md)) compiles the same KyberKit, core included ([docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md)): its own files (`apps/tvos/KyberKitTV`) are only the UIKit session view, the Metal surface and the GameController capture (`GamepadBridge`, `KeyboardMouse`). So do the iPhone and iPad ([apps/ios](../ios/README.en.md)), with their touch session view, capture of touch, keyboard, trackpad and gamepads, the clipboard through `UIPasteboard` (the bridge's `pasteboard_*` callbacks), and the core built for iOS (`apps/apple/scripts/build-apple-mobile.sh ios`).

There is neither VRR nor turning off sync on Apple TV.
