# KyberNow Link for macOS

English · [Français](README.md)

KyberNow Link, KyberNow's streaming client: you get your computer (KyberNow Station: Kyber 0.29 server + KyberNow Admin) in full screen on the Mac, with image, sound, keyboard, mouse and gamepads. v2 identity ([guidelines](../../brand/v2/README.en.md)); mock-ups: [Claude Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/YGA7unwqztvuA26CMtdbv8) (home, first contact, transition, session). The video and audio code and the capability detection do not use AppKit: the Apple TV and iPhone/iPad apps ([apps/tvos](../tvos/README.en.md), [apps/ios](../ios/README.en.md)) reuse them as they are, along with the model (`Model.swift`) and Bonjour discovery (`Discovery.swift`). The few differences are behind `#if os(...)`; after a change to KyberKit, build the Apple TV and iPhone/iPad apps too.

## What the app does

- **Home**: the image of the computer shown full frame (very slow zoom, desaturated when offline), its name in large, its system, its graphics card and its address, the virtual display waiting for it, the main button and the profile; on the right, the latency of the last session (median, 95th percentile, duration) on the frame ruler; at the bottom, the other computers with their image (one click shows them), those announced on the network (Bonjour, they light up as they appear) and the add button. Online or not: Admin is polled every 8 s.
- **A computer's image** (`HostImagery`), in this order: the last image of its last session, photographed at the end (`SessionEngine.snapshot`, 1600 pixels wide, JPEG in `~/Library/Application Support/KyberNow/Images`), can be turned off per computer (“Keep the last image”, which deletes it); otherwise an up-to-date preview requested from Admin (`GET /api/stream/preview`, at most once a minute, arriving with a 45° sweep); otherwise its **signature**, two glows whose hue and position are drawn from Admin's fingerprint, with the huge symbol as a watermark. The home screen says which one it shows (“Image shown”).
- **Adding a computer**: on the local network (Admin's Bonjour announcement `_kybernow._tcp`; those marked `kyber=0`, an Admin with no Kyber server behind it, are not offered), by Admin's address and port, or by QR code (the Mac's camera) or the `kybernow://adresse:port?name=…&fp=sha256:…` link shown in Admin, on the Access page. Admin's certificate fingerprint is verified: automatically if the link or the announcement gives it, otherwise by eye (the first four groups). Then the account, verified with Admin and kept in the Keychain. Kyber's certificate itself is required at each session as Admin gives it (`kyber_fingerprint` from `prepare`), never remembered: reinstalling the computer, which changes it, does not block the app. With a Station older than 0.2.8, it is remembered on first contact (`~/Library/Application Support/kyber/known_hosts`), to be deleted if the computer was reinstalled. A `kybernow://` link opened elsewhere opens the add flow directly.
- **Settings per computer**: Game, Desktop and Max profiles (changing a setting makes the profile “Custom”); the computer's display (like this display, if needed “4K at most”, window size, custom, or its displays as they are); format (H.264, HEVC, AV1), 4:4:4, vertical sync, bitrate from 5 to 4,294 Mbit/s with warnings; transport (fast or reliable), reconnection; ⌘ sent as Ctrl, games mouse, gamepad seen by the computer (Xbox 360 or faithful), shared clipboard, sound (Auto, stereo, 5.1, 7.1, none); “This Mac” card.
- **Bitrate**: a profile's bitrate is set for 4K at 60 Hz and follows the session's number of pixels per second (power 0.75, between 0.4 and 3 times). For example, the Max profile at 300 Mbit/s starts at about 460 Mbit/s in 5K at 60 Hz. A bitrate set by hand stays as it is. The settings show the real bitrate.
- **Pointer**: the Mac's pointer takes the shape of the computer's, at the size of a Mac pointer. The Windows cell (32 pixels at 100%, 48 at 150%, 64 at 200%) is brought down to 32 points, and the hotspot follows the same scale. A hide, or a fully transparent shape (video players), never makes it disappear: it keeps the last shape.
- **Nothing stays pressed**: when the window loses the keyboard (⌘Tab, ⌘Space, Spotlight, another app in front), the app releases on the computer what it holds there (keys, modifiers, mouse buttons) and forgets its own state. No kynput `flush_keys` here: it would also release the gamepads, which, read by SDL, keep talking to the computer without the window and would not send their state again (it is still sent at the end of the session). When the window gets the keyboard back, it sends again the modifiers really held at that moment (`NSEvent.modifierFlags`, ⌘ still down after a ⌘Tab); a released modifier is released on the computer whichever side (left or right) held it. Caps Lock: sent as press + release, like a keystroke, never held.
- **Refused packets** (the core's `PacketQueue`, `apps/core/src/input/packet_queue.rs`): kynput's send queue holds 64 packets and drops without notice, while the protocol is made of deltas (a dropped release = a key stuck until the end of the session). The app counts the refusals (log `entrées : … refusé`, then the total at the end of the session). Packets that cannot catch up on their own (keys, clicks, buttons, gamepad plugs and unplugs, an axis back to rest, text, `flush_keys`) are retried every 3 ms from a small fallback queue, in order, for up to 200 ms; the others (positions, moves, wheel, moving axes) are sent once, and not at all while the fallback queue is not empty (they would overtake what is waiting: an axis back to rest, then its new value). Gamepads read by SDL do not go through it (kynput sends them itself): the router hook (`gamepad_gate.rs`) only discards axes that have not moved (by 1/512 of the travel), which quiets a chatty Bluetooth gamepad.
- **Apple ISO keyboard** (the core's `keymap.rs`, `KeymapMac`): on a European Apple keyboard (one more key next to left ⇧), the Mac reports the key below Escape (`@#` on an AZERTY, `§±` elsewhere) as `kVK_ISO_Section`, HID usage 0x64, and the one next to ⇧ (`<>`) as `kVK_ANSI_Grave`, usage 0x35: the opposite of what the computer expects (`@` gave `<`, confirmed on 2026-10-20 on an AZERTY MacBook; `hid-apple` quirk, SDL does the same swap). Both usages are swapped when the keyboard type of the event (`kCGKeyboardEventKeyboardType`, then `KBGetLayoutType`) is ISO; an ANSI keyboard plugged in at the same time keeps its keys.
- **Mouse: Auto, Desktop or Game** (settings, session bar, ⌃⌥⌘M to switch between desktop and game).
  - In game mode, the computer's pointer, when it is visible (menus, inventories), is drawn by the app at the position the computer announces (server with `kyber-patches/0006`, 60 positions per second). The shape is only rebuilt if it changes.
  - In game mode, the Mac's pointer is frozen and hidden, and only raw movements are sent (GCMouse, without macOS acceleration; AppKit movements without GCMouse). The mouse becomes absolute again, pointer put back in the same place, when the bar opens or when the window loses focus.
  - Auto (the default) switches to game mode when a game is in full screen on the computer and hides its pointer. Admin says so (`GET /api/stream/foreground`, queried every second): foreground window covering the whole screen, or DirectX exclusive full screen. Browsers, video players, slideshows and video calls in full screen do not count. A pointer shown again for less than 0.6 s (menus, Windows flicker) does not make it leave game mode.
  - The pointer being hidden is not enough on its own to trigger game mode: on a computer with no mouse plugged in, Windows hides it and shows it again every half-second, even on the desktop.
- **Introduction to Admin**: at the start of a session, the app gives Admin a stable identifier (drawn at first launch), the Mac's name, its system, its version and the profile; then its median latency with each heartbeat, and that of the whole session at the end. Admin builds its known devices and its event feed from this. If Admin asks for the session to end (“End the session”, in Admin or the Station's tray icon), the app learns it at the next heartbeat and disconnects as with Quit.
- **Shared clipboard**: text, both ways, up to 60 KB (the core's `clipboard.rs`, through `InputCore`; kynput protocol, C functions of `kyber-patches/0002`). What the computer announces is requested immediately, so that ⌘V works straight away; what the Mac copies is announced (checked twice a second) and sent on request. Admin can forbid it in one direction or the other.
- **iCloud for the Apple TV** (`CloudPublisher`): each time a computer is added, changed or removed, the app publishes the list to the iCloud key-value store (key `hosts`: name, address, Admin port, account, stream port, fingerprint, and `from` = this Mac's name) and copies the password to the iCloud Keychain. The Apple TV app on the same account adds them without typing anything. Computers published by another Mac stay in the list. “Synchroniser avec iCloud” setting in KyberNow Link › Settings… (⌘,), on by default; turning it off removes this Mac's computers from the list and from the iCloud Keychain (the Apple TV keeps those it already added). It needs the iCloud entitlements, hence a provisioning profile, in place for Debug and Developer ID builds alike (see “Signing and iCloud”).
- **Updates**: Sparkle, daily check and the KyberNow Link › Check for Updates… menu; the website's cumulative feed, `https://www.kybernow.app/updates/appcast.xml` (every release, EdDSA-signed archives, see [docs/releases.en.md](../../docs/releases.en.md)). Betas are only offered if "Receive beta versions" is ticked in KyberNow Link › Settings… (`receiveBetaUpdates` in `UserDefaults`; the Sparkle delegate in `KyberNowApp.swift` then asks for the `beta` channel); a stable release newer than an installed beta is offered normally.
- **Choosing the display**: with the virtual display, the app waits at most 6 s, only once, for Kyber to announce only that display, then takes the display at the requested size placed at (0, 0). Outputs that Windows lists as 0 × 0 (off) are left out; if no usable display remains, the session says so instead of staying on "First frames".
- **Gamepads**: those SDL handles (Xbox, DualSense, DualShock…). Per-computer setting “Manette vue par l'ordinateur” (gamepad seen by the computer): **Xbox 360** (default), they all become Xbox 360 gamepads on the computer, the ones every game recognises; or **Fidèle** (faithful), a Sony gamepad stays a DualShock 4 (touchpad, light bar for the games that use them), the others stay Xbox 360. The choice goes with the session (`gamepad_force_xbox360`) and overrides Admin's “all gamepads as Xbox 360” option, which only remains a default for other devices.
- **Gamepad combination** (L1 + R1 + Start + Select held for 250 ms; a brief press of the four buttons stays with the game, like on the Apple TV): the bar opens and the gamepad drives it (d-pad, A, B); same combination or bar closed, it goes back to the computer. The session controller is what takes the gamepad, not the layer that reads the packets (the core's `gamepad_gate.rs` hook, which only reports the combination): outside a session (connecting, reconnecting, ending), the combination does nothing and the gamepad stays with the computer, instead of going silent until the next combination. When the bar opens, everything is reset on the computer: held buttons released (trigger included) and the six axes at rest, for every gamepad the packet layer has seen; those releases are the only gamepad packets that leave while the bar holds the gamepad. When the picture restarts (profile change from the bar), the bar keeps the gamepad.
- **Reconnection**: during a Kyber reconnection, the app waits: it is the app that restarts the image once Kyber is back. If Kyber restarted on the computer, the virtual display may have gone with it (it always keeps the same number, but Admin removes it and turns the physical displays back on). So the app asks Admin for it again (`/api/stream/prepare`, no effect if it is still there), waits at most 3 s until Kyber announces only that display, then restarts the image on it. If the computer still refuses the image within 15 s, it makes this request once more instead of cutting the session. The heartbeat also says whether the virtual display is in place (`active`): if it disappeared during the session, the app asks for it again (at most once every 30 s).
- **Image continuity** (see [docs/audit-2026-10-20.md](../../docs/audit-2026-10-20.md) §15):
  - *Computer mode changed* (a game switching to 1080p, a Windows setting): with the txproto engine, the image goes on at the new size (encoder recreated, decoder rebuilt on the new SPS/PPS, rendering recentred). The input coordinate space follows a change in the size of the decoded frames (the core, from the size `SessionEngine` reports with each renderer measurement, once per second; the first frame does not count, it may be scaled) or in the size Kyber's list gives to the same display (compared with the previous list): the latest change wins, and the list always corrects a scaled image (with libavconv, the image keeps its starting size and only the list tells the real one). The bar, the bitrate and the followed display take the new size, and a capsule says “The computer switched to 1920 × 1080”. The app tells Admin what to do with a mode change (`mode_policy` in `prepare`): Game lets it be (`follow`), Desktop has it put back (`restore`), the other profiles leave it to Admin's setting (`default`, said explicitly to undo a previous profile's choice). If “Put back” changed nothing after 6 s, the button comes back; it is never sent after the session has ended. In the Desktop profile, the bar offers “Put back 3840 × 2160” (a `prepare` at the starting mode, `restore` policy).
  - *Image pipeline error on the computer* (`pipelineError`, for example libavconv's encoder not surviving the mode change) or *image refused* (`streamingFailed`) outside the 15 s following a reconnection: the app restarts the image (stop, then start on the best display of the updated list) one second later, three times at most (a planned restart is cancelled by a reconnection, which restarts the image itself), before ending the session; the count starts over after 30 s without a restart. The capsule says “The image was restarted”. A restart also restarts the inputs on Kyber's side (1 to 2 s).
  - *Shown display gone* (HDMI unplugged, virtual display recreated): the image restarts on the replacement display, chosen in this order: the one picked in the bar, the only display left, **the same display back under another number** (same place and same size; Kyber knows the displays' names, but kyclient does not pass them to the apps), the one at the mode obtained from Admin placed at (0, 0), a display at the obtained mode, the display placed at (0, 0), the first one. A number missing from the latest list is never reused: when the image starts, a display that disappeared in the meantime is replaced by that same choice.
  - *This Mac's screen changed* (external display plugged in or unplugged, resolution or refresh rate changed in System Settings, window moved to another screen): the app listens to `NSApplication.didChangeScreenParametersNotification` and to the window's screen change, waits 0.8 s for the screens to settle, then, with “like this display” and the virtual display, compares the mode of the screen showing the session (real pixels and refresh rate, `DisplayInfo.mode`) with the one requested from Admin. If it differs, a capsule offers "The screen changed: fit the image to 2560 × 1440?" with **Adapt** and **Later**; it fades after 15 s and the bar keeps "Adapt to 2560 × 1440" as long as the modes differ (back to the requested mode, the offer goes away). Nothing changes without that click. **Adapt** stops the image, redoes `prepare` at the new mode (Admin recreates the virtual display), waits up to 6 s for Kyber to announce it, then restarts the image as after a reconnection; the capsule says "Image fitted to 2560 × 1440". If Admin refuses, the old mode stays the one to request again and the capsule says so. Full screen on an unplugged display: if macOS leaves the window at the old screen's size or place, the app leaves full screen, puts the window on the main screen and goes full screen there again; a window left off every screen is re-centred. (The interface texts are in French.)
  - *Inputs after a restart*: Kyber restarts its input service with the image (`stop_streaming` + `start_streaming`), and the new one holds nothing. On `INPUT_SERVICE_CONNECTED`, the chain sends the plugged gamepads again (`networkConnected`) and the view reasserts the mouse mode (the new chain starts absolute: in game mode, it goes back to relative as soon as it is created) and sends the modifiers still held on the Mac (⌘, ⇧, ⌥, ⌃, on the right side). Other keys and mouse buttons are not sent again: a game would see a new press.
  - *Heartbeat*: it carries the current profile's policy (`mode_policy`, the profile may have changed since `prepare`) and Admin answers with the virtual display's current mode (`mode`), the number of active displays (`displays`) and the policy in force. With the `restore` policy, Admin puts the mode back itself; if the heartbeat still reports another mode (older Station, change refused), the app asks for the starting mode again (`prepare`), at most once every 30 s. With `follow`, nothing.
- **Panels over the image**: the measurements panel, which stays open, has an opaque background. Frosted glass (`ultraThinMaterial`) would be recomputed by macOS at each frame of the stream (160 per second in 4K) and makes the frame rate drop. The bar, opened briefly, keeps its glass. Starting an image always stops the previous one, input chain included: two chains were fighting over the gamepad (SDL only serves one), and the old one, unplugged, rejected everything.
- **Transition and session**: the session plays in the home window. The home screen is photographed and cut in two along the 45° seam; a neon tube lights up in it during preparation (capsule: preparing, connecting, first frames, Cancel), then the two halves move apart onto the computer's image and the window goes full screen (`Passage.swift`, Core Animation). For the duration of the session, a window delegate hides the menu bar and the Dock in full screen and passes the rest on to SwiftUI's. Bar when hovering at the top of the screen, in glass: lit symbol, live latency and its curve, resolution and format, profile, sound, mouse, clipboard, the computer's display (without a virtual display), full screen, Quit. Clean end, checking that the computer got its displays and its sound back; the halves close on the last image, then the home screen comes back.
- **v2 guidelines** (`Theme.swift`): ink and frost, Spectre signal `#9A8CFF` that only lights up as neon in active moments (standby on hover, flash on click, lit during a connection or a live measurement, ignition on appearance), turned off with “Reduce transparency”; variable Archivo (width 125 for titles) and Martian Mono; Phosphor icons in `Assets.xcassets/Icons` (`scripts/phosphor-icons.mjs`); app icon in Icon Composer format (`KyberNow/AppIcon.icon`: the two halves of the symbol in “Liquid Glass”, the seam's neon on top, ink background), produced from the `brand/v2` geometry by `scripts/app-icon.py` and editable in Icon Composer.

## Image and sound

Kyber normally draws the image and plays the sound with VLC. KyberNow Link decodes them itself (H.264, HEVC, AV1, Opus) for latency and sync: `kyber-patches/0001` adds to libkyclient an output of the packets to the app (see [docs/kyber-changes.en.md](../../docs/kyber-changes.en.md)).

**End-to-end latency and the path of a frame** (the Rust core, `apps/core/src/latency.rs`, through `SessionEngine`, Mac, Apple TV, iPhone and iPad)
- Kyber's metrics turned on: the server timestamps each frame at each step (capture, start and end of encoding, sending), and Kyber's pings give the clock offset (median of the last 15).
- The renderer reports the moment each frame is received, decoded and shown, brought onto Kyber's `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` clock.
- Latency = display − (capture − offset), median of each second, in the bar. The session's median goes on the home screen.
- Steps, medians of each second: capture → encoding, encoding, sending, network (sending → complete frame received), decoding, display (decoded → on screen, sync included).
- Late measurements: the server may timestamp a frame after the Mac has shown it (mostly without vertical sync). The instants of a frame shown without a measurement (received, decoded, shown) are kept for one second and the latency is computed when the measurement arrives, instead of being dropped. The measurements panel shows the share of shown frames that have a measured latency (“Mesures : 97 % des images”) and the number of rejected frames (measurement never came, or absurd value): a median computed on few frames would be optimistic. The counters start from zero at each stream start (`streamingStarted`, also after a reconnection: the `pts` start again from zero).
- Losses: a frame sent by the computer that is neither shown nor replaced by a more recent one within the second is lost (missing packets, or discarded while waiting for a keyframe). A frame the display drops in favour of a more recent one (160 fps stream on a 120 Hz display) counts as replaced. Added to these are gaps in the stream, keyframes requested again, and packets lost according to Kyber (`NETWORK_LOCAL` / `NETWORK_REMOTE` totals, every few seconds, with the network round trip).

**Image** (`VideoDecoder`, `VideoRenderer`, `VideoPipeline`)
- VideoToolbox, hardware decoder required (software as a last resort), real-time mode, one frame per call, no queue. Asynchronous decoding: Kyber's network thread (the only one, which also reads QUIC and delivers the sound) gets control back as soon as the frame is handed to VideoToolbox, instead of waiting 1 to 3 ms per frame (20 to 60 ms at peaks); the decoded frame comes out on a VideoToolbox thread and goes to the renderer. A frame older than the last one delivered is ignored (`pts` monotonicity guard).
- Lost decoding session (−12785 after sleep, −12911 after a GPU change): recreated right away (50 ms) from the output handler, then a keyframe is requested, instead of waiting for the stream restart (2 to 3 s). Recovery (`onRecovered`) is only reported once the keyframe has actually been decoded.
- Output in the Apple silicon lossless compressed formats (`&8v0`, `&8v4`), read without copying by Metal (`CVMetalTextureCache`). YCbCr → RGB conversion in the shader, according to the matrix announced by the stream (709, 601, 2020) and its range.
- Display: always the most recent frame. Each frame leaves for the display as soon as it is decoded; Core Animation shows the most recent one at the refresh and discards the others (counted as replaced). The old “one frame in flight” guard (frame N+1 waited until the display had shown N: a whole refresh of latency, 8 ms at 120 Hz) remains available with `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1`. On a variable refresh rate display (ProMotion, Adaptive-Sync), the display follows the arrival of frames. Vertical sync can be turned off: lowest latency, possible tearing.
- Recovery after a loss: when packets are lost beyond what error correction can repair, VideoToolbox refuses the frames that rely on the lost reference. With the encoder's progressive refresh, no keyframe arrives by itself. The app then keeps the last image and stops sending unusable frames to the decoder. It requests a keyframe from the computer (`kyclient_client_request_keyframe`, server with patch 0005); the core repeats the request every 300 ms (`kn_session_keyframe_lost`) and the app starts again from the keyframe. With no answer within 1.5 s (computer not up to date), it restarts the stream, which always begins with a keyframe. Inside Kyber the request runs off the main loop, one at a time and given up after one second: a computer that stops answering delays neither the stop nor the reconnection.
- Decoder lifetime: the video pipeline and the audio player handed to Kyber as `userdata` are retained (`PacketSinkLease`) until Kyber's last call (`CLOSED`, patch `kyber-patches/0001`), or 30 s after the stop if the library does not send it. Before, they were released 2 s after the stop while Kyber could still deliver a packet (possible crash on disconnection).
- Initial keyframe lost: a heavy stream (4K at a high refresh rate in 4:4:4) can lose its whole first keyframe, with no gap reported by Kyber. The following frames arrive without a reference: the decoder then requests a keyframe as after a loss, instead of waiting silently.
- Fallback: if no frame is shown 4 s after the stream starts, or if keyframe requests go unanswered before the first frame, the app lightens the stream for this session only (bitrate brought down to 250 Mb/s, then 4:2:0), says so at the top of the screen and notes it in the log (`flux : …`, `repli …`). Changing profile in the bar starts again from the chosen setting.
- Compositing: the Metal layer is the session surface's own layer (`MetalSurface`, a layer-hosting view), opaque, full frame, with no sublayer or transform; successive renderers (one per stream) draw into the same layer. During the stream, the SwiftUI layer (bar, hints, measurements panel, entry banner) is removed from compositing as soon as it has nothing to show, and the passage (home split in two) 1.4 s after the reveal: in full screen nothing is composited over the image and macOS can present it directly, bypassing the compositor (one refresh less). The bar's capsule indicator therefore only appears with the hints. The bar and panels stay in the window rather than in a child window: a window on top would hold the clicks and movements the measurements panel lets through to the computer and those that close the bar, and the result is the same while they are hidden. To check in a session with `MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1` (see below): bar hidden, the HUD should report direct presentation; bar open or the computer's pointer drawn in game mode, it becomes composited again.
- Measuring the display: `KN_PRESENT_STATS=1 /Applications/KyberNow.app/Contents/MacOS/KyberNow` writes a `présentation : …` line to the log (category `video`) every second with the frames rendered, those the display confirms it showed, those Core Animation discarded in favour of a newer one (`presentedTime == 0`), and the commit → screen delay (median and 95th percentile), plus the state of vertical sync and of the guard. `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1` puts back the “one frame in flight” guard (frame N+1 waits until the display has shown N) for comparison. What to read: without the guard, the commit → screen delay should fall towards half a refresh interval (≈ 4 ms at 120 Hz) and discarded frames appear as soon as the stream exceeds the display's refresh rate, which is expected; with the guard, the delay is a whole interval. `MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1` (same command) shows the Metal HUD over the image: Metal does not say publicly whether presentation is direct (the image goes to the display without the compositor) or composited, only the HUD shows it.
- Small cumulative costs, removed: the Metal pipeline (shader) is compiled once per process, off the main thread at launch, together with the decoder probe (`DecoderCapabilities.prepare()`), instead of 30 to 150 ms on the main thread for each stream; parameter sets are keyed by (type, identifier) read from the header (two alternating PPS no longer rebuild the session at each frame); the colour matrix is read once per second or per format change instead of `CVBufferCopyAttachment` at each frame; the Metal texture cache is flushed every second.
- Occluded window (another space, minimised in the Dock, covered, `NSWindow.occlusionState`): no more rendering (the decoder keeps going, references stay good; `nextDrawable` waited up to 1 s per frame) and bitrate brought down to 20 Mb/s; on return, rendering and the profile's bitrate come back (log `fenêtre occultée …` / `visible …`).
- AV1 (M3 chips and later): the sequence header is read bit by bit to build the `av1C` description VideoToolbox expects. Samples are stripped of temporal delimiters, and each OBU carries its size (`AV1.swift`). Measured on an M4 Max at 2560 × 1440: 1 ms decoding. 4:4:4 is not offered in AV1: Apple's decoders only read the Main profile (4:2:0), a 4:4:4 stream (High profile) would not open and every frame was lost until the fallback. The fallback VLC player is no longer part of the session (0.3.1): AV1 is only offered to Macs that decode it in hardware.

**Sound** (the Rust core's sound engine, `apps/core/src/audio`, since milestone 3; the Core Audio output in `AudioPlayerCore`)
- libopus (multistream: stereo, 5.1, 7.1), loss concealment by Opus. Sound travels unreliably with error correction (`UNRELIABLE_FEC`, Kyber's default): a packet lost beyond the correction is concealed by Opus instead of being retransmitted, and the delay no longer builds up on Wi-Fi as it did in reliable mode (where concealment was never used). The computer must announce its packet size (server patch 0008).
- Core Audio output (`DefaultOutput` on the Mac, `RemoteIO` on iOS/tvOS), 5 ms requests to the sound card, up to 4,096 frames per request accepted (Bluetooth, AirPlay: otherwise the unit refused and went silent).
- Buffer of about twenty milliseconds, against a fixed 50 ms with VLC: its margin grows by 5 ms after an empty buffer, goes down by one millisecond every two seconds without a gap. An empty buffer is filled with an Opus concealment frame (a plausible splice) rather than silence, and playback resumes as soon as the margin is there (the first start waits for the margin plus one packet). The drift between the computer's and the Mac's clocks is caught up smoothly: one sample skipped or doubled out of 256 (0.4 %, one out of 128 beyond 6 ms of excess) until the buffer's lowest level is back at the margin, instead of a hard cut; only a lead of more than 10 ms (start, output left stopped) is cut at once.
- Output followed during the session (`kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultOutputDevice`): AirPods connected or an HDMI display chosen, the unit is recreated on the new output, its latency and effective buffer size read again (log `son : sortie changée …`), the sound-image offset stays right. Fewer channels at the output than in the stream (5.1 or 7.1 to headphones): downmix, L + 0.7 C + 0.7 surround L (and rear L), same on the right, LFE dropped, instead of an index mapping that sent the centre into the right ear. 7.1 layout (Vorbis order: L, C, R, side L, side R, rear L, rear R, LFE): sides `LeftSurround`/`RightSurround`, rears `RearSurroundLeft`/`RearSurroundRight`, as in Core Audio's MPEG 7.1 C layout (the sides were labelled `…SurroundDirect`). To check with a channel identification clip on a 7.1 amplifier.
- Sound and image each leave as soon as they are ready. The bar shows the offset (sound = buffer + output latency, image = reception → screen), imperceptible below 45 ms. Bluetooth headphones add 150 to 250 ms on the sound side.

**Machine capabilities** (`DecoderCapabilities`, `DisplayInfo`)
- At launch, off the main thread: H.264, HEVC and AV1 hardware decoders, plus a real decode of a 64 × 64 HEVC 4:4:4 frame, requiring hardware (20 to 80 ms), and the Metal pipeline compiled.
- The profiles are brought down to them: without hardware 4:4:4, 4:2:0 with a third more bitrate; without HEVC, H.264 with half as much again. The settings grey out what the machine cannot decode.
- Display: the panel's real pixels, taken from its display modes (native mode), up to 7680 × 4320, and 4096 wide at most in H.264 (NVENC limit). In “more space” mode, macOS draws larger than the panel then scales down: a 4K display set to 3008 × 1692 points draws 6016 × 3384, and those extra pixels are not requested from the computer. “4K at most” option, maximum refresh rate, variable refresh rate or not.

Live measurements (session bar): end-to-end latency and its curve, resolution and format. A click on them (or ⌃⌥⌘S) opens the **detailed measurements** at the bottom right (`SessionStats.swift`): the path of a frame as a cascade on a common scale, with the duration of one frame as a dotted line; latency (losses marked under the curve) and bitrate over 30 s; losses over 30 s and since the start; network round trip, image, sound and the sound-image offset. The panel stays open and lets clicks and movements through to the computer, except its close button. In Debug, `KN_STATS_PREVIEW=1` shows it filled with plausible measurements, without a session.

```
└── Vendor/                # SDK Kyber compilé, et le cœur Rust (libkybernow, CKyberNow) ; ignoré par git
```

In English: `project.yml` is the XcodeGen project (KyberKit and KyberNow targets). `KyberKit/` is the framework: the Rust core as seen from Swift, image, sound and inputs. `SessionEngine` (the Rust core's session, see docs/coeur-apple.en.md: states, notices, packets, measurements), `ConsoleClientCore` (Admin outside a session, through the core: info, preview, fingerprint, pairing), `CoreBridge` (the core's log and language; `CKyberNow` is `Vendor/KyberNow/include`), `KyberTypes` (the computer's display, codec, stream options; shared), `LatencyReporting` (what the renderer tells the core's latency tracker), `InputCore` (inputs through the core: input chain, pointer, mouse rules, keyboard, gamepad combination), `AudioPlayerCore` (Core Audio output pulling samples from the core's sound engine), the video classes, `AV1` (reading the AV1 sequence header), `Language` (the app's language), `StreamView` (session view: AppKit events, pointer drawing, relative mouse), `MetalSurface` (view that holds the renderer's Metal layer), `RelativeMouse` (games mouse, GCMouse), `KeymapMac` (kVK → HID table, keyboard layout, AppKit modifiers; Mac only) and `KyberLog` (logs to the unified log). `KyberNow/` is the SwiftUI + AppKit app (`Model`: computers, settings, Keychain; `Discovery`: Bonjour discovery). `kyber-patches/` holds the KyberNow patches to libkyclient and libkynput, `tests/` the offline tests (video, screen), and `Vendor/` the built Kyber SDK and the Rust core (`libkybernow`, `CKyberNow`; ignored by git).

Flow of a session: it lives in the Rust core (`apps/core/src/session.rs`, see [docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md)), driven by `SessionEngine` through the C API of `apps/apple`: preparation by Admin (virtual display at this Mac's size, sound with the right number of channels) → connection to Kyber → wait for the display list to settle → image → heartbeat every few seconds → end (the computer gets its displays and its audio output back; report in the `ended` state). The core relaunches the image (lost keyframe, error on the computer, display gone), asks for the virtual display again after a reconnection, lightens the stream when no image comes within 4 s (bitrate, then 4:2:0), follows the size of the computer's display and offers “Put back”. `SessionController` keeps only the interface: window, transition, bar, gamepad, capsules, measurements. Swift gives the core the view, one decoder and renderer per stream (`VideoPipeline`), the sound output (`AudioPlayerCore`, which pulls samples from the core's sound engine), the input capture (into the core's input chain, `InputCore`) and tells it about every frame shown (latency on Kyber's clock).


### Inputs on the core (milestone 2)

On Mac, inputs go through the Rust core's input module (`apps/core/src/input`, see [apps/core/README.en.md](../core/README.en.md)), through the C API of `apps/apple` (`kn_input_*`, `kn_pointer_*`, `kn_keymap_*`): kynput chain and retry backlog, keyboard (scancodes, “⌘ sent as Ctrl”, ISO swap, lock keys), release all and held modifiers, the computer's pointer and the Auto / Desktop / Game rules, clipboard, and the SDL gamepads (combination held 250 ms and capture by the bar, through a kynput router hook: `gamepad_gate.rs`). `InputCore.swift` keeps the names `StreamView` and `SessionEngine` use (`InputPipeline`, `PointerLayer`, `Keymap`, `MouseModeRules`). The Mac provides the AppKit capture, the pointer drawing, the kVK → HID table and the ISO layout (`KeymapMac`), and `NSPasteboard` through callbacks. Gamepad capture: presses and moving axes stay with the app, releases and axes at rest always go through to the computer (no more permissions to re-arm on every retry, as in the former `GamepadGate.swift`).

## Building

Prerequisites: Xcode 27, XcodeGen, Rust 1.89 + `cargo-c`, the Kyber 0.29 client built in `third_party/kyber-desktop` (see [docs/build-notes.en.md](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)).

```bash
apps/macos/scripts/vendor-kyber.sh      # SDK Kyber (en-têtes, bibliothèques, plugins VLC) → Vendor/
apps/macos/scripts/build-kyclient.sh    # libkyclient et libkynput avec les correctifs KyberNow → Vendor/
apps/apple/scripts/build-apple.sh       # le cœur Rust (libkybernow, en-tête CKyberNow) → Vendor/
cd apps/macos && xcodegen generate
xcodebuild -project KyberNow.xcodeproj -scheme KyberNow -configuration Debug -derivedDataPath build build
open build/Build/Products/Debug/KyberNow.app
```

(`vendor-kyber.sh` copies the Kyber SDK: headers, libraries, VLC plugins; `build-kyclient.sh` builds libkyclient and libkynput with the KyberNow patches; `build-apple.sh` builds the Rust core, `libkybernow`, and its `CKyberNow` header.)

Run `build-kyclient.sh` again after each `vendor-kyber.sh`: without it, the app picks up libraries without the patches. Run `build-apple.sh` again after each change to `apps/core` or `apps/apple` (it refuses to run while the app is open). When `Vendor/` changes, quit the app before rebuilding: VLC would crash while loading modules rewritten under its feet (see [build-notes](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)). A rebuild without a change to `Vendor/` no longer touches the app's libraries. Signing: “Apple Development: info@leslignesclaires.fr” certificate, team P6DB682S4K (`project.yml`). It stays the same from one build to the next, which keeps the Keychain from asking again for access to the password; plan a Developer ID for distribution.

### Signing and iCloud

Publishing for the Apple TV requires two entitlements, `com.apple.developer.ubiquity-kvstore-identifier` (`$(TeamIdentifierPrefix)fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, the Apple TV app's key-value store) and `keychain-access-groups` (`$(AppIdentifierPrefix)fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, data protection keychain). They live in `KyberNow/KyberNow.entitlements`, along with the camera. On macOS these entitlements need an embedded provisioning profile, otherwise the app is killed at launch: in Debug, the “macOS App Development” profile `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` (this Mac, info@leslignesclaires.fr certificate); in Release, the “Developer ID” profile `P6DB682S4K.fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` (all Macs, valid until 2044). Both must be installed on the Mac that builds, otherwise Xcode refuses to sign. The app also checks the entitlements at runtime (`CloudPublisher.entitled`): signed without them, it publishes nothing and says so.

Setup (done on 2026-10-20), to redo for a new build Mac or an expired profile, on [developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/account/resources):

1. App ID `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`: the iCloud capability is already there (created for the Apple TV, key-value store included); nothing more to tick, the Keychain group `P6DB682S4K.fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow` comes from the identifier itself.
2. Devices: register this Mac (Provisioning UDID in System Information › Hardware; or let Xcode do it from Settings › Accounts).
3. Profiles: “macOS App Development” for `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`, “Apple Development: info@leslignesclaires.fr” certificate, this Mac; “Developer ID” (Mac) for the same identifier, Developer ID Application certificate. For Xcode to find them, copy each downloaded file to `~/Library/Developer/Xcode/UserData/Provisioning Profiles/<UUID>.provisionprofile` (double-clicking is not enough on macOS).
4. Their names go in `project.yml` (`PROVISIONING_PROFILE_SPECIFIER`, per configuration), then `xcodegen generate`.

Xcode puts the profile in the app (`Contents/embedded.provisionprofile`) and adds the App ID and team to its entitlements. `scripts/sign-release.sh` re-signs the app with the entitlements Xcode gave it (no longer with the `.entitlements` file): the Developer ID release keeps the profile and its entitlements through notarisation. The local Keychain item (service `fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`) does not change: the signature stays the same, and so does access to the password.

## Languages

The app is in French and English ([docs/i18n.en.md](../../docs/i18n.en.md)): French if macOS is in French, English for any other language (`CFBundleDevelopmentRegion` set to `en`). KyberNow Link › Settings… › Language: Auto, Français or English, for KyberNow Link only (the app's `AppleLanguages` preference), applied on relaunch.

## Towards iOS and Apple TV

The Apple TV (`apps/tvos`) compiles the same KyberKit, core included, since milestone 4: its own files (`apps/tvos/KyberKitTV`) are only the UIKit session view, the Metal surface and the GameController capture (`GamepadBridge`, `KeyboardMouse`). iOS and iPadOS do the same (milestone 5, [docs/coeur-apple.en.md](../../docs/coeur-apple.en.md); session validated on an iPhone on 22/10/2026): a touch session view, capture of touch, keyboard, trackpad and gamepads, the clipboard through `UIPasteboard` (the bridge's `pasteboard_*` callbacks), and the core built for iOS (`apps/apple/scripts/build-apple-mobile.sh ios`).

There is neither VRR nor turning off sync on Apple TV.
