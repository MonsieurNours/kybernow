# KyberNow Link for Linux, the Steam Deck, SteamOS and the Raspberry Pi

English · [Français](README.md)

KyberNow Link on Linux: a **native** app on the Rust core of the other Link apps (`apps/core`), bringing up the computer (KyberNow Station: Kyber 0.29 server + KyberNow Admin) full screen, with keyboard and mouse or a controller. One app, three interfaces from the mock-ups ([Claude Design](https://claude.ai/artifact/MgBJNBJ4WEMFvVB6Ujfxhc); Raspberry Pi: https://claude.ai/artifact/6crVeg3QyrA5vs8TRhWRft):

- **Desktop** (GNOME, KDE…): borderless window with its own title bar, mouse and keyboard, segmented settings, session bar at the top;
- **Handheld** (Steam Deck and consoles under gamescope, 1280 × 800): everything by focus and controller, button legend, animated launch;
- **Living room** (SteamOS on a TV, Steam Machine): the same at ten feet, with the controllers line on the home screen.

Under gamescope the app picks the console interface (handheld up to 1280 × 800, living room above), elsewhere the desktop one; App settings › Interface, `--ui desktop|handheld|tv` or `KYBERNOW_UI` force it. Installation: [INSTALLATION.en.md](INSTALLATION.en.md). The decision is in [PROJECT.en.md](../../PROJECT.en.md) ("Linux, Steam Deck and SteamOS client").

**Status**: built (x86_64 and aarch64) and tried in a session on desktop Linux (Ubuntu 24.04 under GNOME, in a virtual machine, CPU decoding); not yet tried on the Steam Deck. **Raspberry Pi**: the .deb package and KyberNow Link OS are built in an ARM64 virtual machine, the image boots under QEMU emulation; nothing has run on a board yet (see "Not yet tried").

## What the app does

- **Launch** (console): the iPhone and iPad sequence (unlit mark, flicker, name and signature, then the home screen takes the mark over); A or B skip it; `KYBERNOW_REDUCE_MOTION=1` tones it down (the mark lights up without flickering, then a fade).
- **Home** (`src/ui/home.rs`), following the screens shared by the Link apps ([docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md)): the chosen computer full frame (the last image of the last session, else Admin's preview, else its signature), its status ("Online", "Offline", "Searching…"), its name, its system, the display the session will ask for ("1280 × 800 display at 90 Hz, sized for this screen", "3 displays, sized for this device's screens" on desktop, "1920 × 1080 display at 60 Hz" when custom, "The computer's own displays, as they are" without a virtual display), "Connect" (offline: "Wake the computer" when the app knows one of the computer's network cards, then "Waking the computer…" until it answers, polled every 2 s for 2 min at most; otherwise "Try again"), the profiles and their sentence ("Custom" when the settings move away from them: format, bitrate, 4:4:4 in Game, reliable transport outside Desktop; picking a profile goes back to it), "Settings" (gear button, Y with a controller); in the living room and on KyberNow Link OS, "2 controllers". Nothing technical: the figures are in Settings › Statistics. At the bottom the computers (name and status), those found on the network ("On your network") and adding. With a controller, ‹ › on the main button switch computer.
- **Adding** (`src/ui/pair.rs`): six-digit code shown to every Station found (mDNS `_kybernow._tcp`) and accepted in Admin › Link devices; or Admin's address and port (keyboard, Steam keyboard or on-screen keypad), then the first four groups of the fingerprint to compare.
- **Computer settings** (`src/ui/settings.rs`, `src/ui/station.rs`): on desktop, from the Raspberry mock-up: on the left the profile (a row of buttons) and the sections; on the right the chosen section, one field per setting (label, segments with their "graphics card", "Pi decoder", "processor" tags, unavailable values struck through, help); breadcrumb in the title bar (logo › computer › Settings, the computer goes back home). With a controller, "Settings for {name}" (in French, `i18n::settings_for` elides "de" before a vowel or an h), the sections on the left, the ‹ › values on the right; help wraps rather than being cut. The profile first (Game, Desktop, Max, Custom), then the six shared sections:
  - **Picture**: resolution (match this display, 4K, 1440p, 1080p, 800p, 720p), displays (Auto, This display, All displays; on desktop), refresh rate, virtual display, video format (those the graphics card decodes), sharp text (4:4:4, optional, HEVC), bitrate, frame display (Auto, synced, immediate, variable refresh), lighter on battery;
  - **Sound**: Auto, stereo, 5.1, 7.1, on the computer;
  - **Controls**: controller seen by the computer (Xbox 360, As is), L1 + R1 + Start + Select, mouse (Auto, desktop mouse, game mouse), shared clipboard;
  - **Network**: picture transport (Fast, Reliable), reconnect automatically, Wake on LAN ("Ready" and the card learnt from Admin, or "No address yet: connect to this computer once."); as an advanced setting, the MAC address by hand (keyboard or hexadecimal keypad), for a Station that does not give its cards;
  - **Statistics**: the Mac app's "Last session" card (median in large type; a ruler from 0 to 20 ms, more if the measurement goes beyond, 160, 120 and 60 Hz marks, a band from the median to the 95th percentile; "95% of frames under …"; duration and day; resolution, refresh rate, format, bitrate, and the number of displays of a multi-display session), then "This computer": system, graphics card, Admin's address and port, four groups of the fingerprint;
  - **Station**: version and state ("Up to date", "Version 0.5.1 available", the one that installs with the installer, Station too old, restart or update in progress, done); Admin: "Open Admin" on desktop (`xdg-open`), a QR code of `https://address:port/` on the Deck, in the living room and on KyberNow Link OS (`qrcodegen` crate, modules aligned on pixels); "Restart the Station", "Update the Station" when a version installs from a device; the account ("This device's own access": adding by code gives the device its own account, and Admin 0.5.0 confirms it); "Remove this computer", after confirmation.
- **App settings**: interface, language, keep the last image (for every computer), offered versions, checking for a version, controller test; the "This device" card says what the app found (system, graphics card, decoders, screen, sound; on the Pi, memory, decoders, HDMI output, network, temperature).
- **Station** (`src/device/jobs.rs`, core: `Console::station_restart`, `station_update`): a device in a session refuses first ("{device} is connected", then "Restart anyway" sends the request again with `force`); too soon, a version that needs the installer, Station 0.4.9 or older: the contract's sentence. Once Admin accepts, the app polls the Station every 2 s (`GET /api/stream/info`) until it is back (and on the new version for an update); beyond three minutes (ten for an update): "The Station is taking a while to come back. Open Admin to find out more."; an update that came back on the old version: "The update did not complete. Open Admin to find out more."
- **Wake on LAN** (`src/device/jobs.rs`, core: `kybernow_core::wake`): on each answer from Admin, the app keeps the computer's network cards (MAC address, IPv4 address, broadcast address; an empty list, from a 0.4.9 Station, does not erase them) in `kybernow.json`; "Wake the computer" sends the magic packet to each of them, and to the MAC address entered by hand (with the computer's address for the direct send).
- **Session** (`src/ui/session.rs`, `src/device/`): connecting shows its three steps ("Preparing the display", "Connecting", "First frames") over the computer's background, with the neon in the seam, and Cancel; then the picture alone, with nothing over it when it arrives (on desktop, only the short line that marks the bar); a failure before the first frame says "The connection failed" and the reason. When the picture does not come, the core restarts the Station once (last resort): "The Station is restarting…" takes the place of the "Connecting" step, then the steps start again. **Desktop**: a short line (220 points, white at 35 %), centred at the top of the picture, marks the bar (latency and graph, resolution, frames per second, decoder, profile, keys, mouse, full screen, Disconnect). It opens when the pointer stays a quarter of a second against the top edge (2 points at most), in the zone centred on its width; a quick pass does not open it, nor does a pointer that leaves the zone or the window, a window without the keyboard, a held mouse button (a computer window dragged upwards) or the game mouse (pointer captured: the line disappears). The delay is measured on each turn of the loop (`session_tick`, a still pointer sends no more events), the zone and the rectangle come from the bar's layout (`BarLayout`). It closes 0.4 s after the pointer has left it (its rectangle and 8 points around), never while one of its menus is open. The line alone is neither redrawn nor re-uploaded every second: a 4-point strip is laid over the picture; **console**: controller menu (stream health, tiles: Resume, profiles, sound, controller as mouse, Steam keyboard, keys, statistics, lighter or controllers, Disconnect); statistics over the picture, on demand; capsules for what changes later (game mouse, controller plugged in, reconnecting); end on error. At the end of a session of at least 3 s, the app keeps for the computer the median, the 95th percentile, the duration, the date, the resolution, the refresh rate, the format, the bitrate and the number of displays (`LastSession`; files written by 0.4.x still load, without a separate refresh rate or format).

## Picture, variable refresh and decoding

- **Decoding** (`src/native/decoder.c`, `src/device/video.rs`): FFmpeg 7.1.1 reduced to decoding, statically linked; **VA-API** when the GPU supports the format (probed at launch through libva: H.264, HEVC, AV1), otherwise the CPU (H.264 and HEVC, slice threads). FFmpeg is built without NVDEC: an NVIDIA card only decodes through a VA-API driver. VA-API frames are copied to NV12 and shown by SDL (one copy per frame: negligible at 1280 × 800). Only the most recent frame is shown.
- **Profiles** brought down to what this machine decodes: HEVC (GPU, else CPU), H.264; AV1 on request when the GPU decodes it; **optional 4:4:4** (Settings › Picture › 4:4:4 colour, HEVC only, off by default): the GPU decodes it when VA-API supports HEVC 4:4:4 (recent Intel), otherwise the CPU; as SDL has no YUV 4:4:4 texture, `decoder.c` converts each frame to BGRA on the decoder thread (fixed point, BT.709 or BT.601, up to four threads). Measured in the VM (Apple M, CPU): 3.4 ms per 1440p frame, decoding and conversion included, against 1.9 ms in 4:2:0. Offline test: `tests/video/run.sh` (colours within ±3, formats, timing).
- **Variable refresh rate (VRR)**: the app reads the `vrr_capable` property of the connected displays through DRM (`src/native/display.c`, libdrm, read-only). Display Auto: if the display announces it, each frame is shown as soon as it is decoded (without waiting for vertical sync) and the compositor (gamescope, KWin, Mutter) paces the display on the stream; otherwise synchronised. Synchronised, Immediate and Variable refresh can be forced in the settings. The Steam Deck (LCD and OLED) has no VRR; a display or TV on the dock may have it.
- **Session mode**: the screen's ("like this screen") or the chosen height, at the screen's or the chosen refresh rate; on battery with "Lighter", 60 fps and 20 Mbit/s at most.
- **TV game mode (ALLM)**: handled by the compositor (gamescope), not by the app.

## Raspberry Pi

The same app, on Raspberry Pi OS (Debian 13 "trixie", arm64), as a `.deb` package, or alone on the card with KyberNow Link OS (below); the decision is in [PROJECT.en.md](../../PROJECT.en.md) ("Raspberry Pi client"), mock-ups: https://claude.ai/artifact/6crVeg3QyrA5vs8TRhWRft.

- **Detection** (`src/device/system.rs`, `pi()`): the device tree (`/proc/device-tree/model` → "Raspberry Pi 5", "Compute Module 5", "Raspberry Pi 3 B+"; `compatible` → generation 5: BCM2712, 4: BCM2711, 3: BCM2837), memory (`/proc/meminfo`, rounded to the GB), V4L2 decoders (`/sys/class/video4linux/*/name`: `rpivid` for HEVC on Pi 4 and 5, `bcm2835-codec` for H.264 on Pi 3 and 4), temperature (thermal zone 0, re-read every 5 s), the OS ("Raspberry Pi OS (Trixie)", from `/etc/rpi-issue`). The connected display's modes are read through DRM (`display.c`, `kn_modes_probe`): highest refresh at 1080p and the largest mode.
- **What the output allows** (`Pi` in `app.rs`): 4K at 60 Hz at most on Pi 4 and 5 (HDMI 2.0), 1080p before; 1080p at 120 Hz when the display announces it (not yet verified on a board); no VRR. Screen, refresh and format choices beyond that are greyed (struck through); tags say who decodes; 4:4:4 goes to the CPU (1080p at most); the help explains ("4K in HEVC only, decoded by the Pi. Above 1080p, H.264 goes through the CPU and stutters").
- **App settings**: the "This device" card gives the model, memory and system, the Pi's decoders, the HDMI output, the network and the temperature; the version carries the architecture ("0.4.8 · aarch64"). Captures: `--preview` produces `rpi-01-accueil.png`, `rpi-07b-reglages-image.png`, `rpi-07f-reglages-statistiques.png`… (simulated machine).
- **Decoding** (`decoder.c`, `KN_HW_V4L2` mode): HEVC through the `hevc_v4l2request` hwaccel of Raspberry Pi's FFmpeg ([jc-kynesim/rpi-ffmpeg](https://github.com/jc-kynesim/rpi-ffmpeg), branch `dev/7.1.1/rpi_import_1`, built by `build.sh --rpi` with `--enable-v4l2-request --enable-sand --enable-libudev`), DRM device (the first `/dev/dri/cardN` that opens), `DRM_PRIME` frames in the SAND layout copied to linear NV12 by `av_hwframe_transfer_data` (one copy per frame, on KyberNow Link OS too); H.264 through the `h264_v4l2m2m` decoder (M2M, in every Linux FFmpeg: `--enable-v4l2-m2m`), frames in memory. On refusal or repeated errors, the CPU decodes. The official FFmpeg (Flatpak) has no V4L2 request hwaccel: the aarch64 Flatpak on a Pi decodes HEVC on the CPU.
- **Package** (`scripts/deb.sh`, on an arm64 Linux: the VM, or a Pi): `build.sh --rpi --install`, launcher, AppStream file, icons, licences, dependencies read from the binary (`dpkg-shlibdeps`), `dpkg-deb` → `dist/KyberNow-Link-RaspberryPi-arm64.deb` (published with every release; `release-flatpaks.sh` builds it in the VM after the Flatpaks) and `kybernow-link_<version>_arm64.deb`. **apt repository**: `scripts/apt-repo.sh` assembles a repository from the .deb files in `dist` (`pool/` and `dists/stable/`, through `dpkg-scanpackages` and `apt-ftparchive`), signed when `KYBERNOW_APT_GPG_KEY` names a GPG key (public key exported as `kybernow.gpg`), to be served as is by a static site; `release.sh` does not call it and no repository is published. The .deb app (outside Flatpak, on arm64) looks for its update in `KyberNow-Link-RaspberryPi-arm64.deb` of the published release and announces it.
- **KyberNow Link OS**, the image to flash: [os/README.en.md](os/README.en.md). Raspberry Pi OS Lite through pi-gen, Link alone full screen on KMS/DRM, Plymouth with the unlit mark then the brand's ignition with sound, first boot (controller, language, network, use, computer), device settings, standby and HDMI-CEC, whole-OS A/B update (tryboot). On the app side: `src/device/os.rs`, `src/device/chime.rs`, `src/ui/setup.rs`, `src/ui/device.rs`; OS mode when `KYBERNOW_OS=1` or `/etc/kybernow-os/release` exists.
- **Not yet verified on a board** (no Raspberry Pi available; everything is built in the Ubuntu ARM64 VM): the HEVC decoder (request API, SAND layout, per-frame latency), H.264 M2M, the 1080p 120 Hz output, multichannel HDMI sound, Bluetooth controllers, the package on Raspberry Pi OS Trixie (dependencies computed on Ubuntu 24.04, names Debian 13 has too: `libsdl2-2.0-0`, `libopus0`, `libva2`, `libva-drm2`, `libdrm2`, `libudev1`).

## Sound

Opus decoded by the core's engine (adaptive buffer, loss concealment, clock drift), pulled by SDL's callback (PipeWire or PulseAudio; ALSA on KyberNow Link OS; 256 frames per callback), in stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 depending on the output (Auto) or the setting, in SDL's channel order. Mutable from the session menu; with several computers, one output per session, muted while it is neither active nor kept ("Keep sound"), with a 20 ms fade. Sound can stay on the computer.

- **Several displays** (desktop interface; the Steam Deck, living room and KyberNow Link OS stay on one display; contract: [docs/multi-ecrans.en.md](../../docs/multi-ecrans.en.md)): the app reads the device's displays through SDL (`SDL_GetNumVideoDisplays`, `SDL_GetDisplayBounds`, current mode: frame, native resolution, refresh rate (30 Hz at least: some drivers report 0, as before multi-display); display 0 is the main one; displays with the same frame, mirrored, count as one: `src/displays.rs`, `src/device/screens.rs`) and gives them to the core (`Session::start_with_screens`), which decides how many displays to take from the "Displays" setting, the profile, the resolution and what the Station can create (Admin's `max_screens`, read at every poll). The home screen's line ("3 displays, sized for this device's screens") and the settings' "Displays" row (Auto, This display, All displays, under Resolution) come from the same calculation; it is inactive, on "This display", with a resolution picked by hand (4K, 1440p…: a custom resolution, a single display) or without a virtual display (setting off, or a Station without a usable virtual display: `Screens::virtual_display_available`, from Admin's `virtual_display.available`). Its help: Auto's when it is active, the custom resolution's with a resolution picked, none without a virtual display (as on the Mac, [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md)). **"Custom"** (settings that differ from the profile: format, bitrate, transport, 4:4:4; in Auto, a single display) is decided by the core (`screens::screens_profile`, with what this machine decodes: `Screens::capabilities`) on the stream settings the session sends; the Station always receives the base profile (label, `follow` / `restore` mode policy, bitrate that follows the resolution), as before multi-display. On battery, "Lighter on battery" brings the bitrate down to 20 Mbit/s: that is a difference, a single display in Auto (as before multi-display). **Windows**: one full-screen window per display taken; the app's window moves to the main display, the others open as soon as it connects from the core's plan (the same calculation as the home screen and the session), with the same title (the connection's background, with no text or photo; "The computer could not create this display." for a display not created; each window switches to its picture at its first frame); one decoder (VA-API or processor), one picture queue, one renderer and one interface drawing per window; the extra windows are presented without waiting for the vertical sync (several presents in a row would each wait for one), the app's window follows "Frame display". After that the core is the only source of the plan: the extra windows open and close at what it announces (`Platform::on_screens`, `Event::Screens`), never from the app's own plan alone; with a Station that handles only one display, or only the main display accepted, or a single-display profile, the extra windows close, with no message, and the app's window shows that stream, full screen on the stream's display. **Inputs**: desktop mouse, absolute position sent with the window's display (`mouse_on`) and the pointer announced by the computer drawn in the window of its display (one pointer layer per display); game mouse, relative, captured in the active window; Auto: the core's rule (`PointerLayer::summary`: hidden only if it is hidden on every display the pointer has already been on, a display never visited not counting). **Bar**: a single one, in the window where the gesture happens (it becomes the active window), the line at the top in all of them; no "Full screen" (button, F11, Ctrl+Alt+Shift + F); the bar says the number of displays. Closing a window means disconnecting; the end of the session closes the other windows (a failure shows on the main display, which stays full screen until "Back to home"), and the app's window goes back to what it was. **Displays that change during the session** (SDL, read every second and on display events, a change that holds for 0.6 s): `Session::set_screens`, with the plan from before and after calculated by the core: if it does not change (a display plugged in that the session does not take, a refresh rate changed, a laptop put on a dock in Game), neither the windows, the pictures nor the pointer are touched; if it changes, the core sets the session up again (the connection starts again at its first step, the pictures are forgotten), the extra windows whose display moved close at once and the others open at its announcement. The profile picked in the bar changes the number of displays right away (the core receives the new stream settings and sets the session up again if the number changes); "This display" is the one the window is on: with several displays the main display, where it moved at the start, and that is the one a session that goes back to a single display takes (its resolution, its refresh rate); the "Displays" setting changed during the session applies to the next one. The last session keeps the number of displays the computer took, without those it could not create, the same as the bar ("3 displays · 3840 × 2160 · 160 Hz · HEVC · 120 Mbit/s"). Screenshots and tests: `--preview` (`bureau-15-accueil-ecrans`, `bureau-15b…15f` settings, including the "Displays" row inactive without a virtual display, and statistics, `bureau-16…16e` connection, the other displays' background, display not created, line, bar); `device::tests::multi_window_lifecycle` opens three windows under X11 (`DISPLAY=:0 cargo test -p kybernow-linux -- --ignored multi_window`).
- **Several computers** (desktop interface; the Steam Deck, living room and KyberNow Link OS keep one session; contract: [docs/plusieurs-ordinateurs.en.md](../../docs/plusieurs-ordinateurs.en.md), texts in [docs/link-ui.en.md](../../docs/link-ui.en.md)): on Linux there is no icon next to the clock and no home screen during a session: **another computer is opened from the bar's "Computers" menu**, a menu like the profile one, present as soon as there is another computer: your other computers in the home screen's order ("{name} — Online", with the state's colored dot: green, red, amber while waking or during a Station update, gray while searching), what the home screen's button does ("Connect", "Wake the computer", "Try again"), "Show" for those already in a session (checked); at the bottom, "Four computers at most at the same time." ("Connect" is then inactive) and the "Next computer" shortcut. **Sessions**: `App::sessions` (the interface, in opening order; a `SessionId` is never reused) and `App::session()`, the active session, which the bar, the menu and the shortcuts target; `Lives` (`device/mod.rs`) holds what each session has of the core and SDL; session actions carry their session (`Disconnect(id)`, `SetProfile(id, …)`) and the core's callbacks are tagged (`Event::Session(id, SessionEvent)`): a session that ends at the same instant never makes an action hit the next one. **Windows**: one per computer (`Win::session`). The first session takes the app's window as before (full screen, or all displays); each next one opens a real window, decorated by the window manager (move it, resize it, close it), titled "{name} · KyberNow Link", centered at two thirds of the active window's display in 16:9 (`displays::windowed_frame`), with a single display ("This display": the mode is that of the display it opens on); its connection shows in its window and its bar offers "Full screen" (it then covers its display, the first session keeps its own). Closing a window or "Disconnect" (Ctrl+Alt+Shift + D) ends only its session; the last one brings the home screen back, the app's window hides while other sessions have theirs; a failure stays in its window ("Back" closes it while other sessions run, "Back to home" for the last). **The active session** is that of the window that takes the keyboard (`SDL_WINDOWEVENT_FOCUS_GAINED`), or the one "Show", Ctrl+Alt+Shift + O ("Next computer", in opening order, even in full screen where Alt+Tab goes to the computer) or opening a session designates (its window comes forward, unless the app no longer has the keyboard). It alone receives input: keyboard, text, mouse (hover included), wheel and controller buttons or axes; a click in another session's window makes it active and only acts on that window's elements (Cancel, Back), never on the computer; controllers are plugged into every session (the game doesn't lose them), rumble only comes back from the active session. **Sound**: one SDL output per session (the system mixes them), muted while the session is neither active nor kept by the bar's speaker button ("Keep sound", named on hover like the other buttons; sound muted from the session menu wins), with a 20 ms fade, without ever stopping decoding (`AudioSink::set_muted`, `Shared::set_muted`). **The clipboard** only goes through the device: a session that isn't active neither reads nor writes the device's (`Clipboard::set_active`), and the one that takes over immediately gets the device's text. **The single application point** is `device/focus.rs`: on every loop turn, `App::focus_states()` (for each session: active or not, sound muted or not) is compared with the list already applied; as soon as it changes (activation, "Keep sound", sound muted, session opened or ended), whatever path changed it, `focus::apply` calls **one method per session**, `Live::apply_focus` (sound, clipboard, controllers: on deactivation keys and clicks released, relative mouse off, controllers at rest; on activation the held controller state is sent again), then sets SDL's global state from the active session (relative mouse, text input, controller as mouse, screensaver off while at least one session runs). In the second step only the body of `apply_focus` changes: it will call the core's `focus` registry. A click or hover goes to the elements of the window that receives it (`Win::hits`). Admin polling continues during a session, for the computers that aren't in one, to keep the "Computers" menu's state up to date. Screenshots and tests: `--preview` (`bureau-17…17f`: the menu with an offline computer, with two sessions, the speaker on and its name, four at most, a further session's window), `app::tests` (session list, active session, "Next computer", end of the active session, "Computers" menu, actions), `displays::tests::windowed_frame_…`, `device::media::tests` (the fade); `device::tests::multi_window_lifecycle` also opens a second session's window under X11, and `device::tests::second_session_gets_its_own_window` opens two sessions on absent Stations (nothing listens on 127.0.0.1): windows, active session, sound, sessions ending in either order (`DISPLAY=:0 cargo test -p kybernow-linux -- --ignored --test-threads=1`).

## Inputs

- **Keyboard**: SDL's USB scancodes converted to Windows scancodes (the `keycode` crate table, kynput's); in full screen during the session the keyboard is grabbed (Alt+Tab, Super… go to the computer). App shortcuts: Ctrl+Alt+Shift + D, F, M, O (next computer).
- **Mouse**: absolute position mapped to the computer's screen (the window's, with several displays); **game mouse** (relative, `SDL_SetRelativeMouseMode`) when a game is full screen on the computer and hides its pointer (Auto, core rules), or forced; holding Esc for a second releases it. The computer's pointer becomes the system pointer at the picture's scale (desktop), or is drawn by the app (game mouse, console).
- **Steam Deck trackpads and touchscreen**: mouse through Steam Input; a touch at the top of the screen opens the menu.
- **Controllers**: SDL GameController, four players, rumble (and triggers), announced once per link (to every session, with several computers; buttons and axes only go to the active session); Steam Input presents the Deck's controls as an Xbox controller. Controller as mouse (left stick, RT faster, LT finer, right stick wheel, A / B / X clicks). L1 + R1 + Start + Select: menu.
- **Text**: "Keyboard" in the menu opens Steam's keyboard (`steam://open/keyboard`); characters are then sent as Unicode.
- **Clipboard**: text both ways (the core's bridge; SDL read and written from the main thread).

## Architecture

```
apps/linux/
├── src/
│   ├── main.rs          launch (--preview: screenshots, --ui: interface)
│   ├── app.rs           state, spatial and mouse navigation, settings (no SDL nor core)
│   ├── model.rs         computers, settings ($XDG_CONFIG_HOME/kybernow/kybernow.json, 0600)

│   │                    controllers, session; logo traced from the brand geometry
│   ├── device/          SDL (window, events, sound), session (core Platform), picture
│   │                    focus.rs: the single application point of the active session (several computers)

│   │                    screens.rs: SDL's displays in the core's format, the session's display plan
│   └── native/          decoder.c (FFmpeg: VA-API, the Pi's V4L2), display.c (VRR and modes through DRM)
├── flatpak/             manifest, .desktop, AppStream, icon, licences
├── os/                  KyberNow Link OS: Raspberry Pi image (pi-gen, A/B, Plymouth, services), see os/README.en.md
├── scripts/             build.sh (--rpi), sources.sh, flatpak.sh, release-flatpaks.sh, deb.sh, apt-repo.sh
└── tests/video/         offline decoder test (run.sh, check.c)
```

- **Interface**: drawn in memory in *points* (the mock-ups': 1280 × 800 for desktop and Deck, 1920 × 1080 for the living room), multiplied by the screen scale (HiDPI); the signed logo is traced from the `brand/v2` paths (like the Windows app's `Mark.svelte`), sharp at any size.
- **Desktop window**: borderless; the app's title bar drags the window, the edges resize it (`SDL_SetWindowHitTest`), double-click maximises. SDL2 uses X11 (Xwayland on GNOME and KDE, gamescope on the Deck); `KYBERNOW_VIDEO=wayland` tries Wayland. The window class is the app ID (`fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow`).
- **kynput**: client only, with the webOS patch (`apps/webos/kyber-patches/0001`, `--cfg kybernow_webos`): without the server's input injection (libinput, libevdev, X11).
- **VLC**: kyvlcplayer references libvlc, never called: `build.rs` compiles an empty function per `libvlc_*` function, as on Android, iOS and webOS.

## Building

```sh
# Screen captures, without SDL nor the core (on a Mac too):
# desktop (2560 × 1600 and a 960 × 600 window), Deck, living room, Raspberry Pi,
# KyberNow Link OS; home (online, offline, Custom, no computer), each computer
# settings section (Network without an address; Statistics with and without a
# session; Station up to date, version available, in progress, done, installer,
# old, confirmations), waking, connecting, Station restarting, failure,
# picture alone, session:
cargo run --release -p kybernow-linux --no-default-features -- --preview /tmp/kn-linux en

# On Linux (Ubuntu 24.04: build-essential pkg-config nasm clang libclang-dev
# libsdl2-dev libva-dev libdrm-dev libopus-dev, Rust 1.89 or later, cargo-c):
apps/linux/scripts/build.sh                    # → apps/target/release/kybernow

# The Flatpak (flatpak-builder, org.freedesktop.Platform 25.08 runtime,
# rust-stable and llvm20 extensions), for this architecture or the other one:
apps/linux/scripts/flatpak.sh                  # → apps/linux/dist/KyberNow-Link-Linux-<arch>.flatpak
apps/linux/scripts/flatpak.sh x86_64           # from an ARM machine: Rosetta or qemu-user-static

# From the Mac, both Flatpaks then the Pi's .deb in the "KyberNow Linux" VM (what release.sh does):
apps/linux/scripts/release-flatpaks.sh [--check] [DIR]

# Raspberry Pi, on an arm64 Linux (the VM, or a Pi; libudev-dev as well):
apps/linux/scripts/build.sh --rpi              # Raspberry Pi's FFmpeg, HEVC V4L2 request decoder
apps/linux/scripts/deb.sh                      # → apps/linux/dist/KyberNow-Link-RaspberryPi-arm64.deb
apps/linux/scripts/apt-repo.sh [DIR]           # → dist/apt (KYBERNOW_APT_GPG_KEY to sign)

# The decoder, offline (after build.sh; ffmpeg and x265 generate the streams):
apps/linux/tests/video/run.sh [--hw]
```

`build.sh` applies Kyber's client patches, builds libkynput (`cargo cinstall`, static) and FFmpeg 7.1.1 reduced to decoding (VA-API) into a prefix, then the app. `sources.sh` gathers the needed sources only (about a hundred megabytes instead of Kyber's build outputs), which `flatpak.sh` hands to flatpak-builder in two modules: the dependencies (cached while Kyber does not change), then the app. Kyber 0.29's sources go in `third_party/kyber-desktop` (see [docs/build-notes.en.md](../../docs/build-notes.en.md)).

**Build and test machine**: a Parallels VM "KyberNow Linux" on the development Mac (Ubuntu 24.04 ARM64, GNOME, Flatpak), with Rosetta registered in binfmt (`/etc/binfmt.d/rosetta.conf` and a boot service) to build the Steam Deck's x86_64 Flatpak at near-native speed, and the repository shared (`/media/psf/kyber-apps`). Its virtual GPU (virgl) has no VA-API decoding: the CPU decodes there.

## Trying out several displays

A real multi-display session needs a Linux desktop with two or more displays and a 0.5.x Station with multi-display support (`max_screens` in `GET /api/stream/info`). Procedure (Ubuntu or Fedora under GNOME or KDE, X11 or Xwayland; two or three displays, one of them mirrored once):

1. Desktop interface (App settings › Interface), a computer online, Desktop profile: the home screen says "N displays, sized for this device's screens" (N: the active displays, 4 at most, mirrors counted as one). Game profile: "… display" (a single one). Settings › Picture › Displays: Auto, This display, All displays; picking a resolution (1080p…) makes the row inactive, on "This display", with "With a custom resolution, the session takes a single display."
2. Connect in the Desktop profile: the app's window goes full screen on the main display (SDL's display 0) with the connection ("Preparing the displays"), the other displays show a dark flat colour with no text; each display switches to its picture as soon as it arrives. Log: `kybernow.log` in `~/.config/kybernow` (Flatpak: `~/.var/app/fr.leslignesclaires.kybernow/config/kybernow`), with "écran 0 (principal) …" and "écrans de la session : N pris".
3. On the computer: N virtual displays laid out like the device's (Windows display settings); the pointer moves from one display to another, a window dragged from one display to another follows; a full-screen game opens on the main display at its refresh rate; keyboard and mouse reach the computer from any window.
4. Bar: pointer against the top edge of each display for a quarter of a second: it opens in that window only (which takes the keyboard), never two; no "Full screen" button; it says "N displays · …". The line at the top shows in every window.
5. Game profile in the bar: a single display, the other windows close, the connection starts again, the picture is at the main display's resolution where the window stayed full screen (not stretched); Desktop profile: the displays come back. Changing "Displays" in the settings during the session changes nothing until the next session.
6. Plug or unplug a display during the session: the connection starts again on the main display with the new displays, without going back to the home screen. In the Game profile (a single display), plugging in a display, changing a refresh rate or putting a laptop on a dock changes nothing: the picture and the mouse carry on.
7. A Station without multi-display (0.5.2 or earlier): the home screen announces a single "… display"; with a Station that refuses displays, a single window.
8. Close a window (window manager): disconnect, all windows close, the app's window comes back as it was (size, position, windowed or full screen). End of session: the last session says "N displays · …" in Settings › Statistics.

## Trying out several computers

Two online Stations and the desktop interface (App settings › Interface). The end-to-end test of [docs/plusieurs-ordinateurs.en.md](../../docs/plusieurs-ordinateurs.en.md), for Linux:

1. A session on the first computer in full screen; bar (pointer against the top edge): "Computers" lists the second, online (green dot); a click opens its window, on top, named after it, centered at two thirds of the display in 16:9, with its connection; it becomes active (the log says "session: … window, session 2 of 2").
2. Type, click, plug in a controller: only the active window's computer reacts; Ctrl+Alt+Shift + O goes to the other window, and the controller follows without the game losing it; a click in the other window makes it active without clicking on the computer.
3. Sound: only the active session is heard; "Keep sound" (the bar's speaker, named on hover) on the other leaves it audible, without a click.
4. Copy a text on one, switch to the other, paste: the text only goes through the device, never from one computer to the other in the background.
5. "Computers" › the first one: its window comes forward ("Show"). Three other computers in a session: the menu says "Four computers at most at the same time." and "Connect" is inactive; an offline computer wakes from this menu.
6. "Full screen" in the second window's bar: it covers its display (Alt+Tab then goes to the computer; Ctrl+Alt+Shift + O switches windows).
7. Close the second window: the first session carries on. Disconnect from the first while the second runs: its window goes away, the second stays, the app's window hides; when the last one closes, the home screen returns. Quit the app: every session ends, the computers' displays come back.
