# KyberNow Server (Windows)

English · [Français](README.md)

Everything that runs on the shared computer: the Kyber 0.29 server (patched, see [docs/kyber-changes.en.md](../docs/kyber-changes.en.md)), the administration console, the notification area icon and the installer that sets it all up.

| Folder | Contents |

## The console (`kyber-admin`)

Installed by `KyberNow-Setup.exe`, as a Windows service, on a port drawn at random at installation (shown at the end, and in the notification area icon). The account is the one chosen during installation.

v2 identity (Archivo, Martian Mono, Spectre signal, “console” symbol), four pages by intent, menu on the left and tabs at the bottom on a phone. Choices apply immediately; Kyber's need a restart, offered at the top of the page. The old addresses (`#/dashboard`, `#/logs`…) lead to the page that now holds them.

| Page | Role |
|---|---|
| Overview (`#/ensemble`) | The screen preview in large; during a session, the connected device, since when, its profile, its latency and “End the session”; the state in four cards (server, virtual display, sound, update), the latest devices and today's feed |
| Picture and sound (`#/image-son`) | SudoVDA virtual display (trigger, default mode, if a game changes the resolution, 15 s test, turn the displays back on), sound (trigger, channels, Opus quality), keyboard, mouse and gamepads (clipboard, Xbox 360, repeat) |
| Access (`#/acces`) | Add a device (QR code and `kybernow://` link, certificate fingerprint), access requests with a code (Apple TV: accept, refuse), known devices (forget, remove access), several devices at the same time, Kyber accounts (Basic) |
| System (`#/systeme`) | One-click updates, server (start, stop, restart, graphics card, ports), logs by source (connections, picture and sound, commands, console; filter, follow, download, level of detail), Advanced: Kyber's raw settings (NVENC, AMF, Quick Sync or x264 encoder, chosen at installation according to the graphics card; engine, progressive refresh, minimum bitrate, port, addresses, image send queue, connection methods), `kyber_config.toml` editor, console password |

**Devices and event feed** (`activity.rs`). KyberNow apps introduce themselves in `POST /api/stream/prepare` (`device`: stable identifier, name, type, system, app version; `profile`), give their median latency with their heartbeat (`latency_ms`) and that of the whole session at the end. The console keeps the last 50 devices (`devices.json`) and the last 300 events, written as sentences (`events.json`): connections, disconnections with duration and latency, interrupted sessions (no heartbeat for 20 s: `activity::HEARTBEAT_GRACE`, the same delay for the session, the app's virtual display and the full-screen watch), accounts, restarts, updates. An app older than 0.2.1 counts as “one device” per account. The session is identified by the device (apps send `device_id` with their heartbeat and their session end, from 0.2.5 on; before that, by the account): two devices on the same account are no longer confused. **One device at a time.** If a second device prepares while a first one is in session, the console asks the first to end (it learns it at its next heartbeat, `end`, and disconnects cleanly) and makes the second wait up to 7 s; if the first no longer answers, its session is closed and the display taken over. The virtual display is then recreated at the second device's mode. A replaced device that disconnects afterwards does not touch the display or the sound of the one that took over. Console routes: `GET /api/devices`, `DELETE /api/devices/{id}`, `GET /api/events?limit=`, `POST /api/session/end`, `GET /api/preview?width=` (screen preview, as for the apps). `GET /api/status` also gives the current and the last session (`activity`) and the available update.

**Ending a session.** A KyberNow app learns it at its next heartbeat (`"end": true`, 5 s at most) and disconnects cleanly; another client (browser, command line) is cut by restarting Kyber. `POST /api/local/end` does the same from the icon, reserved for the computer itself (see “Local routes”).

**The virtual display during the session** (`display/mod.rs`, `display/watch.rs`). While a session holds the virtual display, an agent launched in the Windows session (`display-agent watch`, like the full-screen watch) reads every second the current mode of the virtual display (`EnumDisplaySettingsW`, `ENUM_CURRENT_SETTINGS`, on the display's GDI name found from its adapter and target) and the active displays (`QueryDisplayConfig`), and writes them to `display-watch\state.json`; it stops by itself after 15 s without news from the console or when `display-watch\run` carries another session's token (never two agents), and the console relaunches it if it stays silent for more than 10 s. The current mode and the number of active displays are given to the apps with the heartbeat and in `GET /api/display` (`mode`, `displays`). A mode different from the one obtained at creation (within 1 Hz: a game switching to 1080p, a Windows setting) is announced once in the event feed, then the **mode policy** decides: `follow` (“Let it be”, default) keeps it, `restore` (“Put it back”) puts the session's mode back (`ChangeDisplaySettingsExW` through the agent, `display-agent set-mode`), at most once every 5 s; against a game that imposes its resolution again after each change, it gives up after three changes in a minute and lets it be for the rest of the session (announced, `mode_policy` is then `follow`), unless “Put back” is used again. The `virtual_display.on_mode_change` setting (Picture and sound, “If a game changes the resolution”) applies to clients that say nothing; the apps send `mode_policy` according to their profile (Game: `follow`, Desktop: `restore`, the others: `default`, the setting), in `prepare` and with each heartbeat (profile changed during the session). In `restore`, a new call to `prepare` at the same mode also puts the mode back right away (the apps' “Put back” button). A physical display that turns back on during the session (cable plugged back in, Windows reapplying a layout) is turned off again after 2 s of stability, by reapplying the virtual display's isolation (at the current mode in `follow`, at the session's mode in `restore`; at most once every 10 s), and the feed says so. If Windows keeps turning it back on (five isolations in two minutes) or the isolation fails three times (attempts spaced 10, 20 then 40 s apart, one message at the first failure), the console gives up for the rest of the session and announces it once. Outside Windows, the simulated driver sees the requested mode.

**Pairing with a code (Apple TV)** (`pairing.rs`). Without typing anything on the remote: the Apple TV draws a 6-digit code, shows it and sends it to every console found over Bonjour, without an account: `POST /api/pair/request` (`{"code", "device": {id, name, kind, os, app}}` → `{"id", "expires_in"}`). The request appears in Access › Devices with the device's name and its code in large type (Accept, Refuse), in a banner on the other spaces with a badge on Access, and in the icon's panel, which opens by itself for a new request, without the code (it is compared in the console, which accepts) and lets you refuse (`POST /api/local/pair/{id}/refuse`, reserved for the computer itself; `accept` exists too, the icon no longer uses it). Only accept if the code is the one shown on the TV. The device polls `GET /api/pair/request/{id}`, without an account: `pending`, `refused`, `expired` (also for an unknown request), or `accepted` with `username` and `password`, delivered once only (the request disappears at once). Accepting (`POST /api/pair/accept/{id}`, console session) creates **a Kyber account of its own** for the device: identifier drawn from its name (“Apple-TV-du-salon-3f9a”), random 128-bit password. Both the console and Kyber accept it (Basic, like an account typed by hand); the app saves it as for a normal addition, with nothing changed on the client side. Kyber only reads its accounts at startup: the console restarts it at once (in the background) if nobody is connected, otherwise the “restart the server” banner shows. The device enters the list before its first session (“Paired just now”, “own access”); “Remove access” (`DELETE /api/devices/{id}`) deletes its account, asks its session to end if it has one, and Kyber follows at the restart (the console refuses it right away). In Accounts, these accounts read “Own access of …”, with no password change. A device paired again loses its previous account. Limits (requests without an account): exactly 6 digits, 10 requests per minute and 2 pending per address, 6 pending in all, a new request from the same device replaces the previous one; 5 min to accept, then 2 min to collect the access (otherwise the created account is deleted). Requests only live in memory. `GET /api/status` gives the pending requests with their code (`pair_requests`); `GET /api/local/state` gives them without the code (`id`, `name`, `kind`, `expires_at`) and counts them (`pair_requests_count`).
Why not a JWT of the device's own, signed by the console with Kyber's key? Kyber cannot revoke a single one (the key would have to change, cutting every token), and the app would have to go through `kyclient_auth_credentials_new_jwt` and renew it. The own account can be revoked one by one and changes nothing in the apps.

**For the apps.** The console announces itself over Bonjour (`_kybernow._tcp`, TXT `v`, `name`, `fp`, `kyber`), hence the UDP 5353 firewall rule. `kyber=1`: a Kyber server is behind it (its configuration is there and its Windows service exists); `kyber=0`: console alone (development Mac, incomplete installation), no session would ever start there and the apps don't offer it. An announcement without `kyber` (console from an earlier version) is still offered. It also serves the session API:
- `GET /api/stream/info`: name, system (`os`, for example “Windows 11 Pro 24H2”, absent if unknown), GPU, AV1, virtual display, speakers. `GET /api/status` (web console) also gives `os`. On Windows, the name comes from the registry (ProductName and DisplayVersion, “Windows 11” from the build number, as ProductName still says “Windows 10”); on the development Mac, from `sw_vers`; on Linux, from `PRETTY_NAME` in `/etc/os-release`;
- `GET /api/stream/preview?width=960`: preview of the main display as it is right now, for the background of the apps' home screen. JPEG (quality 70), width bounded between 320 and 1600 (960 by default), height to ratio, `X-Captured-At` header (RFC 3339). `503` and `{"error": "…"}` if the screen cannot be captured: nobody signed in, screen locked or a security prompt shown, agent unavailable. The last capture is served again for 10 s (5 s for a failure); during a session (KyberNow app or another client that makes the GPU encode), no capture is taken: the last one is served again, or `503`. The capture is taken by an agent launched in the Windows session (`display-agent preview`, GDI, main display scaled down to the requested width), which writes the JPEG to `preview\`; on the development Mac, by `screencapture` (screen recording permission needed);
- `POST /api/stream/prepare`: virtual display at the client's size, sound with the right number of channels; the app introduces itself there (`device`, `profile`) and says what to do if the mode changes during the session (`mode_policy`: `follow` or `restore`, optional). If another device is in session, the answer waits until it has handed over (7 s at most, see above): allow a 40 s timeout for this call. The session is only counted once the display is in place;
- `POST /api/stream/heartbeat`: heartbeat (without it, the virtual display disappears), with the current latency and `device_id`; the answer says whether the console asks for the session to end (`end`: from the console, or because another device is taking over) and whether the virtual display in place is this device's own (`active`), with the current mode of the virtual display (`mode`: `width`, `height`, `refresh`, which may have changed during the session) and the number of active displays on the computer (`displays`), `null` until the watch has read them, and the policy in force (`mode_policy`); the app may send `mode_policy` there (profile changed during the session). A new call to `prepare` by the device already in session (display requested again after Kyber restarts) continues the same session: no new connection in the event feed and no extra session for the device;
- `GET /api/stream/foreground`: app in the foreground on the computer, full screen or not, game or not (Auto mode of the apps' mouse). The console gets it from an agent that runs in the Windows session for the duration of a session (`display-agent foreground`, state written every 400 ms). The agent stops by itself after 20 s without a heartbeat;
- `POST /api/stream/end`: original displays and audio output given back, with a report; the app gives the session's median latency and `device_id` there. If the display has meanwhile gone to another device, nothing is given back (`kept_for`);
- `GET /api/pairing`: pairing link;
- `POST /api/pair/request`, `GET /api/pair/request/{id}`: pairing with a code, without an account (see above).

**Updates** (console account): `GET /api/update` (installed version, latest published version, notes), `POST /api/update/check`, `POST /api/update/install`. If the installation's PowerShell stops without having replaced the console, `GET /api/update` returns `install_error` (with the end of the log) and the installation can be started again. `GET /api/local/state` (reserved for the computer itself, for the icon) also gives `update_available`, `version`, the computer's name, its address and the current session (`activity`).

**Console fingerprint for the icon** (`tls.rs`, `publish_fingerprint`). `init` and every start write the SHA-256 fingerprint of the console's certificate to `C:\Program Files\KyberNow\kybernow.json` (`console_fingerprint`), next to `kyber\`, if the file exists and it changed: the icon only accepts that certificate (see [server/installer/README.en.md](installer/README.en.md#notification-area-icon-servertray)).

**Local routes** (`local_token.rs`). `GET /api/local/state`, `POST /api/local/end` and `POST /api/local/pair/{id}/accept|refuse` serve the person in front of the screen, through the icon. They require the loopback address (also `::ffff:127.0.0.1`) **and** a token: the console draws 32 random bytes at its start and at every Windows logon (the service receives `SERVICE_CONTROL_SESSIONCHANGE`, `WTS_SESSION_LOGON` and `WTS_CONSOLE_CONNECT`, and renews 2 s later), writes them in hexadecimal (64 characters) to `C:\ProgramData\KyberNow\local-token` (reserved to SYSTEM and Administrators), then runs `kyber-admin display-agent local-token --dir …` in the user's session; the agent copies the token into the profile of the account signed in to that session (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\KyberNow\local-token`, permissions inherited from the profile: that account and administrators), a copy retried six times 5 s apart if the session is not ready. The icon reads that file at every call and sends it in the `x-kyber-local` header; constant-time comparison, `403` without a token or with one from a previous start. Another account on the computer, or a sandboxed process without access to the profile, can therefore neither end the session nor answer a pairing request. Off Windows, the token is only written to `dev-data/local-token`.

Authentication is Basic, with the Kyber account. The apps pin the console's certificate by its fingerprint.

**Signing in to the console.** After five wrong passwords from the same address, it waits 30 s, then twice as long after each further failed attempt (10 min at most), answering `429` meanwhile; a successful sign-in resets the counter. argon2 checks (sign-in, setup, password change) never run more than two at a time: a burst from the network cannot exhaust memory.

**Stopping and restarting.** When the service stops (update, `Stop-Service`, shutdown), the console first gives its displays and audio output back to the computer, then closes its connections; the service manager grants it 30 s. If the displays or the sound could not be given back (no Windows session open, for instance), the state file is kept and the next start tries again. An unreadable `kyber-admin.toml` is set aside (`kyber-admin.toml.invalide-…`) and the console starts from default settings instead of not starting at all.

**Web client** (`/ecran/`, sources in `server/web/src/ecran/`, “Open in the browser” entry on the Overview page). Open the computer from a browser, with nothing to install: home screen on the screen preview, Desktop / Game profiles, settings kept in the browser, session bar (latency, detailed measurements, sound, Keys menu with Ctrl+Alt+Del, full screen with all keys in Chrome and Edge), and on touch devices a tab, a trackpad or direct touch, and a row of keys above the device's keyboard. How it works:
- **Account**: the console's session (same password). `GET /api/web/ticket` (session required) signs an HS256 JWT (`aud` “kyber”, `sub`: the installation account, 12 h) with the key Kyber knows, set by `init` (see “Kyber's secrets”). The response is never cached (`Cache-Control: no-store`). The installation account (`install_account` in `kyber-admin.toml`) is the one created by `init`; an installation from before 0.2.6 records it at its first start (the first account of the list), so that removing or reordering accounts never signs the web client in under a paired device's account. The `/api/stream/*` routes also accept the console's session: the web client prepares the display and sends its heartbeat like an app.
- **Heartbeat** (`heartbeat.ts`): every 5 s from a dedicated Worker built into the page (`blob:` URL, same origin and same isolation), whose timers are not slowed down in a hidden tab, plus an immediate one when the tab goes to the background; without a Worker, the page sends it itself. The end goes out with `keepalive` when the page closes (`pagehide`). The heartbeat and end bodies carry `device_id` (this browser's identifier, the one given to `prepare`); `backend.ts` accepts other fields. `end` in the heartbeat response (end requested from the console, or another device taking over) ends the session with a message. A 401 from the heartbeat or the ticket (console sign-in expired) ends the session with a clear message, and going back to the home screen asks for the password again. The Kyber token is fetched again from `/api/web/ticket` whenever the engine reconnects (`reconnecting`, `authentication_required`) and every hour, then handed to the engine with `update_credentials()`.
- **Engine**: Kyber's `kyclient_wasm`, loaded from `/ecran/kyclient_wasm.js` (with `kyclient_wasm_bg.wasm`, `audio_worklet.js`, `spinlock.js`). The console relays these files from the installed Kyber `/webclient/`: always the same version as the server. The page and these files are served with `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy` (shared memory for the sound).
- **Relay** (`kyber_proxy.rs`): the engine talks to kycontroller at the page's address, and kycontroller refuses other origins. So the console relays `/session/*`, `/kymux/*`, `/ws` (WebSocket), `/capabilities`, `/enumerate_displays`, `/stop_streaming`, `/set_bitrate`, `/force_idr` and `/websocket/*` to kycontroller on this machine. In `/kymux/start_mux`, the image port announced by the engine (the console's) is replaced by Kyber's. The relay does not expose kycontroller on the console's port: it requires the **console session** (the engine, on the same origin, sends the cookie on `fetch` and on WebSockets; `401` without; the cookie is removed before Kyber, and the engine files are behind it too), refuses an `Origin` header that is not the console itself (`403`), only relays the paths above and simple names under `/websocket/` (nothing with `..`, `%2e`, `\` or `//`), **pins kycontroller's certificate** (SHA-256 fingerprint of the `[kycontroller] tls_cert` file, otherwise `kyber\kybertest_cert.pem`, read again if it changes; with no readable certificate, the relay is closed with `503`), caps relayed WebSockets at 8 at a time (`503` beyond), gives Kyber 15 s to accept the connection and answer (`502`), and keeps up to 4 HTTP connections open to kycontroller between requests instead of one TLS handshake per request. The image itself does not go through the console: WebTransport on Kyber's port, verified by its certificate's fingerprint.
- **Image transport** (`plan.ts`, explained in Settings › Advanced): WebTransport on Kyber's port when possible; WebSocket (`/websocket/*`, relayed by the console, a little more latency) on touch devices (the page slips text and positions into the inputs connection, see below), in Safari and on iPhone and iPad (their WebTransport does not verify a certificate by its fingerprint), in Firefox (with the H.264 format in Auto, `device.ts`) and without WebTransport. Elsewhere, if the image does not come over WebTransport, the page switches to WebSocket: the 12 s delay starts at the `streaming_started` event (Kyber accepted the session and started the image), not at connection time, which preparing the virtual display can delay by 8 s; from the connection on, a 25 s delay covers an image start that would never come. A control plane that does not connect within 25 s gives “Kyber is not responding”, without changing transport. Before creating another engine (switch, restart, new session), the page waits for the previous one's `stopped` event (3 s at most): never two engines, so never doubled inputs; a new display preparation also waits until the previous session's end has gone out. Each attempt (preparation, connection) carries a number: once outdated (end, Cancel, new attempt), it stops at its next step without creating an engine, and gives back the display it would have prepared.
- **Changes on the computer**: the engine relays the list of displays (`display_list_updated`). If the followed display disappears or changes size, the page restarts the image without reloading or disconnecting (`stop_streaming`, then on `streaming_stopped` a fresh canvas and `start_streaming`; Kyber also restarts the inputs, 1 to 2 s; with no answer within 5 s, the whole session starts over) and says so: “The computer's display changed”. An error in the image pipeline (`streamer_pipeline_event`, video) restarts the same way one second later, three times at most (the count goes back to zero after 30 s of image), then the session stops with a message; a sound error is only reported. `streaming_stopped` is never an end decided by the computer: Kyber only emits it at the client's request. The display targeted by positions (`displayId`) is learned again after each restart. The gamepads seen by the engine (`gamepad_plugged`, `gamepad_unplugged`) show in a chip in the bar.
- **Sound**: the browser may keep the sound suspended until a gesture has happened (Safari, iPhone, page opened without a click). After the first frame, the page asks `audio_try_resume()` every 200 ms (the engine creates its player at the first sound packet, sometimes a few seconds after the image); if it stays suspended, a “Tap for sound” chip calls `audio_resume()` within the gesture. Any click or key also retries.
- **Mouse**: the engine captures the pointer as soon as the computer hides its cursor, which Windows does constantly with no mouse plugged in. The page only allows capture in Game mode, or in Auto when the console sees a full-screen game (`GET /api/stream/foreground`). `Ctrl + Alt + Shift` gives the mouse back and shows the bar; a tab at the top of the image shows it too. The engine draws the computer's pointer as the canvas cursor and sets `cursor: none` when the computer says it is hidden (always, with no mouse plugged in): outside capture, the page puts an arrow back. Outside capture too, the engine sends a relative move then the absolute position on every movement, both applied by the computer; the relative one (accelerated by Windows, lost outside the window) shifted the pointers: the page zeroes it, only the position counts, like the Mac app in desktop mode. Except when the computer's pointer is hidden (Windows hides it on the slightest keystroke): the engine then switches to relative mouse and only sends the move; since Windows only shows its pointer again on an absolute position, the page then sends the position itself on the inputs WebSocket connection (kynput packet `[2][display u32][x i16][y i16]`, display taken from the engine's positions or from `/enumerate_displays`); over WebTransport, where a whole message cannot be slipped into the stream, the move goes through as is.
- **Touch**: the touch layer turns on as soon as there is a touch screen (`any-pointer: coarse`), also on an iPad with a Magic Keyboard: fingers touch directly, the mouse or trackpad stays the mouse (their clicks and wheel go through the guard) and the pointer takes the computer's one there. With the device keyboard open, the row of keys has a fixed height and sits right above the keyboard according to `visualViewport`; the image takes the rest, instead of half of the remaining space. During a session, closing the tab or quitting the browser (Ctrl+W, ⌘W, ⌘Q) asks for confirmation (`beforeunload`).
- **Device keyboard** (touch, `TypedText` in `touch.ts`): the hidden field that opens the keyboard sends text as kynput Unicode packets (`[10][code point u32]`, typed by Windows with `KEYEVENTF_UNICODE`) on the inputs WebSocket connection, which the page spots as soon as it is created: correct on a computer with an AZERTY layout, with capitals, accents and emoji. Text is read on `beforeinput` and `compositionend` (iOS, Android, dictation), never during a composition, and the field is only emptied outside of one. Enter, Backspace, arrows, Escape and Tab stay keys (by their `code`), as does text typed with sticky Ctrl, Alt or ⊞ (a shortcut, as QWERTY key codes). A real keyboard (iPad with a keyboard, Bluetooth keyboard) gives its keystrokes a `code`: the engine sends them, and the page does not send again the text they produce in the field. On touch devices, when the computer's pointer is hidden, the page also sends the finger's position on this connection (failing that, a relative move).
- **Clicks**: the engine listens to mouse buttons on the whole window, without looking at the target. A guard installed before it (`guard.ts`, in the capture phase on `window`) only lets through presses on the image (the canvas or the touch layer): a click in the session bar or on a touch button no longer clicks on the computer. A button pressed on the image is released on the computer even if it is let go elsewhere. Touch buttons cancel `pointerdown`, which suppresses the browser's compatibility mouse events.
- **Wheel**: the engine ignores a wheel that counts in lines or pages (`deltaMode` other than pixel), which Firefox does. The guard converts it to pixels (40 px per line: the three lines of a Firefox notch make one notch on the computer, since the engine divides by 120; the window height per page) and sends it back to the canvas, like the wheel received by the touch layer, which covers the canvas. Two-finger scrolling goes out in whole notches (120 px).
- **Held keys and buttons**: the same guard tracks pressed keys (by `code`, which is what the engine reads) and buttons pressed on the image. When the window loses focus (`blur`, for example Alt+Tab or ⌘Tab), the tab is hidden or the captured mouse is given back, it sends a release for each one and forgets the touch sticky modifiers. If ⊞ or Alt were held, a Ctrl press goes first, so that Windows opens neither the Start menu nor the app's menu bar.

**Patched engine** (patches 0013 to 0015, see [docs/kyber-changes.en.md](../docs/kyber-changes.en.md)): with the overlay, `kyclient_wasm` is rebuilt by `server/kyber-patches/build.sh` and laid into Kyber's `webclient\`, so the console relays it as it is. It reads by itself the text typed into a field marked `data-kynput-text` (Unicode packets, over WebTransport too), keeps sending absolute positions as long as the pointer is not locked (even when the computer hides its cursor), releases everything on `blur`, hidden tab and lost pointer lock, ignores gamepads without the "standard" mapping, and no longer ends the session on a decoding error if a keyframe comes within a second. With this engine, the page can stop forcing WebSocket on touch devices: mark its hidden field `data-kynput-text`, then remove `TypedText`'s Unicode sending and the position it injects (`inputs.ts`, `pointer.ts`). Not done yet: the page stays compatible with the original engine, and the engine leaves alone a `beforeinput` the page already handled (`preventDefault`).

Kyber's original `/webclient/` is still served by kycontroller, for troubleshooting.

Display, sound and pointer changes are made in the user's session by `kyber-admin.exe display-agent …`, launched by the service.

The `C:\ProgramData\KyberNow` folder is reserved to SYSTEM and Administrators: the console reapplies its permissions at every start (`src/update/acl.rs`, see [server/installer/README.en.md](installer/README.en.md#permissions-of-cprogramdatakybernow)).

**Kyber's secrets** (`kyber_config.rs`). Program Files is readable by every account, and `kyber\kyber_config.toml` holds the (unsalted) SHA-256 hashes of the Kyber accounts' passwords. Since 0.2.6, `kyber_config.toml`, kycontroller's certificate and key (`[kycontroller] tls_cert` and `tls_key`, otherwise `kybertest_cert.pem` and `kybertest_key.pem` next to it, produced by `cert-gen.exe`) and `kyber\avant-maj\` if it exists are reserved to SYSTEM and Administrators (Kyber runs as SYSTEM): applied by the installer, by `kyber-admin init`, at every console start and at every write of `kyber_config.toml` (the new file would otherwise inherit Program Files' permissions). An update copies `kyber\` again (`update.ps1`, `Copy-Item`): the files get Program Files' permissions back until the new console starts, a few seconds later. The HS256 key of the web client's tokens is no longer in clear in `kyber_config.toml`: Kyber accepts `key = { file = … }` for HS256 too (`kycontroller/src/auth/jwt.rs`, `ConfigKey::File`, content read then trimmed), so `init` writes it to `C:\ProgramData\KyberNow\kyber-jwt.key` and `kyber_config.toml` only carries the path; a previous installation (`key = { plain = … }`) is converted at the console's first start, same key (Kyber reads it from the file at its next restart).

## Developing on the Mac

```bash
cd server/web && npm run build                       # ou npm run dev (proxy /api vers la console)
cd ../admin && cargo run -- --http --listen 127.0.0.1:9443 \
    --data-dir dev-data --kyber-dir dev-data/kyber    # copier un kyber_config.toml dans dev-data/kyber
```

(`npm run dev` proxies `/api` to the console; copy a `kyber_config.toml` into `dev-data/kyber`.)

The local routes are tested with the token in `dev-data/local-token`: `curl -H "x-kyber-local: $(cat dev-data/local-token)" http://127.0.0.1:9443/api/local/state` (without the header: `403`). Outside Windows, the Kyber service, the virtual display, the sound and the GPU are simulated or absent. The console then announces itself over Bonjour with `kyber=0` (the apps don't show it); `--announce-kyber` announces it with `kyber=1` to test adding and pairing from the apps (over HTTPS, without `--http`, and on `0.0.0.0` so that the Apple TV reaches it).

`cargo test` (in `server/admin`) also runs the virtual display state machine on the simulated driver (`display/mod.rs`, end of the file): display kept by the app against the automatic trigger, heartbeat from the owner, mode changed during the session with each policy (`follow`: announced once and kept; `restore`: put back, at most every 5 s, and right away by a `prepare` at the same mode; default policy from the setting), physical display turned back on (turned off after 2 s of stability, at most every 10 s, at the mode that fits the policy). The watch moves one round per call to `watch_tick`, at the instant given by the test.

Web client against the Kyber on the test computer: run the console over HTTPS (the engine always calls `https://`) with `KYBERNOW_KYBER_ADDR=192.168.1.100:30241` (Kyber's address and port on the test computer) and `KYBERNOW_KYBER_FINGERPRINT=sha256:…` (fingerprint of kycontroller's certificate on that computer, `openssl x509 -in kybertest_cert.pem -noout -fingerprint -sha256`; without it, the relay finds no certificate and answers `503`), then `npm run dev` in `server/web` (relay to `https://127.0.0.1:9448`) or open `https://localhost:<port>/ecran/`. The image can only arrive if the browser reaches Kyber at the page's address: on the Mac, the home screen and the settings can be checked; the session is tested on the computer itself.

## Updating a computer that is already installed

In one click: the console's **Updates** page (or “Install version X…” in the icon's menu). The console downloads the latest release published on GitHub, checks its checksum and launches `update.ps1`. Details, manual update and publishing a release: [docs/releases.en.md](../docs/releases.en.md).

`server/installer/update.ps1` replaces the console, the icon and the Kyber overlay without reinstalling or changing the ports. It prepares `kyber.new`, `admin.new` and `tray.new`, switches by renaming (the previous version stays in `kyber.prev`, `admin.prev`, `tray.prev`), then checks that the console answers within 60 s; otherwise it puts the previous version back. It also installs Steam's speakers if they are missing and adds the Bonjour firewall rule. `server/installer/package-update.sh` builds its package (`dist/kybernow-update.zip`).
