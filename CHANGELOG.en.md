# KyberNow releases

English · [Français](CHANGELOG.md)

English translation of `CHANGELOG.md`. The release notes published on GitHub, and shown by the computer's console and the Mac app before installing, come from the French file; add each new section to both files.

## 0.4.5

- **KyberNow Link comes to Android and Android TV.** A single app for the phone, the tablet, the TV (Android TV, Google TV, NVIDIA Shield) and the handheld console, from Android 11: home with the computer's image, adding over the network, by code, address or QR code, full-screen session by touch, remote, keyboard and mouse or gamepad (four players, gamepad or remote as a mouse), session menu on the TV, clipboard, a "Resume on a computer" row on the Android TV home screen. Tried on a Shield TV in 4K at 60 Hz.
- The APK (`KyberNow-Link-Android.apk`, signed) is published with every release, and the app updates itself, verified by the signed manifest (stable or beta channel). An app installed from Android Studio must be uninstalled once before installing the published APK.
- **KyberNow Link comes to LG TVs (webOS).** A native app, on the same core as the other Link apps: picture through the TV's decoder (4K at 120 Hz in HEVC on 2022 OLEDs), 30, 60 or 120 frames per second, bitrate capped according to the network (LG's Ethernet port stops at 100 Mbit/s), four controllers with their rumble, Magic Remote or controller as mouse, session menu (Back button or L1 + R1 + Start + Select), adding by code or address. Installs in developer mode (`apps/webos/INSTALLATION.en.md`); not yet tried on a TV.

## 0.4.4

- **Admin: the Access space becomes “Link devices”, and accounts get their own “Users” entry.** Each device is a card that opens on its settings, readable in one line when closed and explicit when open: the app at session start and its two options, access, sessions with “End the session”, forget the device or remove its access; no more “…” menu. The menu icon is the Link symbol. Under Users: one person per username and the devices that use it, the own access of a device accepted with a code in read-only, “Add a person”. The old addresses (`#/acces`, `#/acces/comptes`) lead to the right space; the apps and the docs point to the new names.
- **Admin: an app opens on the computer when a device connects.** Under Link devices, each device's card offers “At session start”: Steam Big Picture for the Apple TV, Playnite, a spreadsheet for the remote desktop… Nothing to type: the picker shows the detected launchers (Steam Big Picture, Playnite, Xbox, GOG Galaxy, Epic Games, EA app, Ubisoft Connect, Battle.net), the computer's whole Start menu with its icons, and a browser limited to Program Files and the Desktop. The app opens as soon as the virtual display is ready, with the user's account; already open, it comes back to the front; with the Windows session locked, it waits for the unlock (up to 2 min). Options: close it at the end of the session (Big Picture can do it by itself), do not open it in the Desktop profile. A device taking over with the same app keeps it open. The event feed says what opens and closes.
- **Apple TV app: the Metal layer is alone on screen with the menu closed.** The menu's veil, its material and the passage no longer stay above the image once the session is open, and the rounded-corner clipping is only set while the menu is open: tvOS can send the image straight to the screen instead of compositing it, one refresh less (up to 16.7 ms at 60 Hz). Display latency is now measured on the Apple TV (presentation time reported by the screen) instead of estimated; frames without a measurement are counted.
- Proposals of 22 October 2026 (`docs/propositions-2026-10-22.md`, in French): HDR in Kyber (costed, postponed), Apple TV latency, app at session start.

## 0.4.0

- **KyberNow becomes three products, each with its own name and logo.** **KyberNow Station** is what you install on the Windows computer that streams the image (formerly KyberNow Server); **KyberNow Admin** is its administration in the browser (formerly the console); **KyberNow Link** is the apps that display the image on Mac, Apple TV, iPhone, iPad and Windows, and in the browser. The symbol keeps its three states (the upper half for the Station, the seam for Admin, the lower half for Link); the logos now carry the product name as a signature under “KyberNow”. The texts of the apps, Admin, the installers, the website and the documentation follow, in French and English.
- Installers: `KyberNow-Station-Setup.exe` (the Station) and `KyberNow-Link-Setup.exe` (the Windows app). Their old names, `KyberNow-Server-Setup.exe` (and `KyberNow-Setup.exe`) and `KyberNow-Client-Setup.exe`, are still published, identical, for a few releases: existing links and updates from earlier versions keep working.
- On the computer, the update renames what Windows shows, without moving anything: “KyberNow Station” in installed apps and the Start menu, “KyberNow Admin” for the administration shortcut and the `kyber-admin` service, “KyberNow Station - streaming server” for the `kyber` service. Folders, settings, accounts and authorised devices do not change.
- On Mac, the app is called KyberNow Link (Dock, Finder, menus); on iPhone, iPad and Apple TV, the home screen keeps “KyberNow”. The Windows app is called KyberNow Link in installed apps and the Start menu; the old shortcut is replaced. Settings, saved computers and passwords are kept.
- Website: a section introduces the KyberNow family, and each download carries its product's logo.

## 0.3.1

- **Mac app: the session moves onto the Rust core.** The course of a session (preparation by the console, connection to Kyber, display choice, image relaunches, reconnections, fallback when no image comes, heartbeat, verified end, end-to-end latency) is now the one of the Rust client core (`apps/core`), the same as the Windows app, through a C bridge (`apps/apple`, `libkybernow`). The app keeps in Swift only decoding (VideoToolbox), rendering (Metal), event capture, the sound output and the interface. Same settings, same capsules, same measurements as in 0.3.0. First milestone of moving the Apple apps onto the core ([docs/coeur-apple.en.md](docs/coeur-apple.en.md)), followed by inputs, sound, the Apple TV and iPhone and iPad (below). The fallback VLC player (AV1 on a Mac without an AV1 decoder) is no longer part of the session: the app already offered AV1 only to Macs that decode it.
- Client core (Mac, Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps): it now knows what only the Mac app knew: following the size of the computer's display and recalibrating the inputs, telling the console what to do with a mode change and offering “Put back 3840 × 2160” in the Desktop profile, adapting the virtual display to the device's screen, showing another of the computer's displays without a virtual display, capping the bitrate (occluded window), returning the end-of-session report (displays and sound restored), and watching over the image: keyframe requested again after a loss, bitrate brought down to 250 Mb/s then 4:2:0 when no image comes within 4 s. The Windows app does not use these yet.
- **Mac and Windows apps: inputs and sound on the Rust core.** Keyboard, mouse, gamepads, the computer's pointer, release all and clipboard go through the core's input module (`apps/core/src/input`), sound through its engine (Opus, adaptive buffer, drift correction, loss concealment, downmix): Core Audio and WASAPI only pull samples. On Mac, the gamepad captured by the bar always lets releases and axes at rest through to the computer. On Windows, a fresh input chain at each image start (as on Mac), the Mac's mouse rules (pointer shown again tolerated 0.6 s, new check at 0.7 s), and the sound buffer and its gaps when hovering the latency in the bar; the clipboard stays kynput's own.
- First tries on hardware (22/10/2026). Mac: session preparation stayed stuck (a core lock taken twice); fixed, the Mac session works. iPhone: the screen stayed black, then the session was cut (“the computer sends no image”): the Metal rendering, which starts as soon as the first frame is decoded, drew into a layer not yet placed in the window, and iOS discards the frames of such a layer forever. The session view (Apple TV, iPhone and iPad) now creates its Metal layer once in the window and attaches the rendering at that point, as on the Mac; if the display still discarded everything for two seconds, the layer is remade. The core no longer counts “no image” while the app freezes the image on purpose (background, hidden window). On iPhone and iPad, the clipboard is only read when the computer asks for it (reading it made iOS ask for permission to paste at the start of every session). GPU errors and the decoded image format go to the log. Not yet tried on hardware: the Apple TV app on the core (installed on an Apple TV, not launched yet) and the Windows client since its inputs and sound moved onto the core.
- **Apple TV app: on the Rust core, like the Mac.** Session, console, latency, sound and inputs (gamepads, remote, Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, pointer held by the Apple TV, L1 + R1 + Start + Select combination) go through the core; the app keeps only the view, Metal rendering, the sound output and GameController capture. Same menus, same settings, same 150 Mb/s fallback as in 0.3.0. The Swift versions of these modules are gone from both apps.
- **New app for iPhone and iPad** (`apps/ios`, iOS 18; not distributed yet: internal TestFlight to come, installed from a Mac with Xcode meanwhile): your computer by touch, on the Rust core like the Mac and the Apple TV. Home with each computer's image, adding through iCloud, the network, a six-digit code, an address or the console's QR code with the camera; animated launch; transition along the seam; session always in landscape on iPhone, with the tab and the bar, trackpad or direct touch, on-screen keyboard with a row of keys (Esc, Tab, sticky Ctrl, Alt and ⊞, arrows, F1 to F12), Keys menu, Bluetooth gamepads, the iPad's keyboard and trackpad; detailed measurements; per-computer settings. External display over USB-C or AirPlay, on iPhone as on iPad (the image moves there at its definition, the device becomes the trackpad); Stage Manager on iPad. First successful session on an iPhone 15 Pro Max on 22/10/2026; iPad (not in the provisioning profile yet), external display and Stage Manager still to be tried.

## 0.2.9

- **New Windows app** (`KyberNow-Client-Setup.exe`): your computer on a Windows PC, designed for a laptop or a more modest computer. Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11, x64 and ARM64 (Snapdragon) in the same installer, which installs the processor's version; installation for the user only, no administrator rights. Image decoded by the graphics card (H.264, HEVC, AV1 depending on what it supports, checked at launch), shown by Direct3D 11 as soon as it is decoded (about ten milliseconds end to end measured on a home network); WASAPI sound; game mouse; gamepads; clipboard. Home screen with each computer's image (the last image of your session, otherwise a preview of its display), adding through the network, the six-digit code accepted in the console, the console's `kybernow://` link or an address; Game, Desktop and Max profiles; per-computer settings (including a custom virtual display). In session: a bar at the top of the screen (live latency, profile, Keys menu with Ctrl+Alt+Del), Windows shortcuts (⊞, Alt+Tab) sent to the computer in full screen, app shortcuts on Ctrl+Alt+Shift + D, F, M. Windows integration: your computers when right-clicking the taskbar icon, notifications, a single open app at a time. App updates verified by each release's signed manifest, betas optional. Not yet signed with Authenticode: Windows warns at first launch (“More info” › “Run anyway”).
- Computer installer: it is now called `KyberNow-Server-Setup.exe`, so it is not confused with the Windows app's; a copy under the old name, `KyberNow-Setup.exe`, is still published for a few releases for existing links.
- Uninstalling the computer: the `C:\Program Files\KyberNow` folder was not deleted at the end (the command's quotes were passed wrongly to `cmd`); it now is.
- Website: the Windows app in the downloads and the guide.
- Console, pairing by code: a device accepted during another device's session received its account, but Kyber refused it ("User … is not authorized") until the server was restarted by hand, because Kyber only reads its accounts at start-up. The console now keeps these pending accesses and restarts Kyber by itself as soon as it serves nobody (end of session, no more heartbeat or encoding), with an event in the feed. If the device connects before that and nobody else is in a session, Kyber restarts while the session is prepared and the device gets in on the first try; if another device is in a session, it is neither cut off nor taken over, and the app shows "the server will restart as soon as …'s session ends". The console and the tray panel show "Server restart pending to give access to …" (also for a removed access). Affects the Apple TV and the Windows client.

## 0.2.8

- Mac and Apple TV apps, console: after reinstalling the computer, the apps refused to connect ("connection impossible") because Kyber's certificate had changed and they had kept the old one from the first contact. The console now gives Kyber's certificate fingerprint when the session is prepared (`kyber_fingerprint`), and the apps require it instead of keeping the first certificate; with an older console, the previous behaviour remains.

## 0.2.7

- Installer: the installation window opened on "Microsoft Edge can't read and write to its data directory" (0.2.6): WebView2's data folder goes back to `%TEMP%`, the interface and its DLL stay under Program Files.
- Console, web client: the relay to kycontroller refused (403 "origin refused") any request carrying an `Origin` that arrived over HTTP/2, which browsers negotiate with the console: HTTP/2 has no `Host` header, and the origin is now compared with the request's authority. The web client's login (`/session/login`) and the stream start went through it.
- Console, virtual display: when Windows does not activate the freshly created virtual display by itself, the console activates it (extended desktop, then a supplied layout with that single display if Windows knows it without turning it on), once a second for 8 s instead of a single attempt at the start; and the message "the virtual display does not appear in the display configuration" now says what was seen and what Windows answered.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: the input code (keyboard, gamepads, pointer, release everything, send queue, clipboard) is now written once in KyberKit, without AppKit or UIKit; the Apple TV app compiles it as it is, and both apps' READMEs say what each platform provides and what an iOS app will have to write. Apart from the gamepad combination on the Mac (below), both apps behave as in 0.2.6.
- Mac app: offline test of the shared input module (`apps/macos/tests/inputs/run.sh`): keyboard table (keys, ISO or not, Caps Lock), gamepad combination (brief, held, fully released), packet retry backlog and axis filtering.
- Mac app, clipboard: the computer's packets are no longer handled under the lock of kynput's input router (the answer re-entered it on the same thread) but on the app's input queue; the pasteboard itself goes behind a small interface (`PlatformPasteboard`: NSPasteboard on the Mac, UIPasteboard to come on iOS, none on the Apple TV). Same exchanges as before.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, inputs: the retry backlog for packets refused by kynput (`PacketQueue`) and the gamepad axis filtering (unchanged axes dropped, batched per 4 ms, returns to rest retried) are now written once in the shared input chain; the Apple TV app no longer keeps a copy. Same thresholds, same delays.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: a single “release everything” policy (`ReleaseAll`): held keys, modifiers and clicks are released when the Mac window loses the keyboard, when the Apple TV app goes to the background and at the end of the session, and the modifiers actually held are sent again on return. Same gestures as before, written once.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, mouse: the state of the computer's pointer (position, shape, hot spot, visibility) and the Auto / Desktop / Game rules with the full-screen watch are a single shared piece of code (`PointerLayer`); on the Apple TV it is held on the gamepads' queue: in desktop mode, the position of the pointer moved with the remote, a gamepad or a mouse no longer goes through the app's main thread (one hop less per movement, clicks and wheel in the same order). Same rules, same delays as before.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, gamepads: the L1 + R1 + Start + Select combination and the held buttons of each gamepad are tracked by a single shared piece of code (`PadCombo`). **Mac app**: the combination now only opens the bar after 250 ms held, like on the Apple TV; the four buttons go to the game (they are released there when the bar opens), and a brief press of the four stays with the game. Before, the bar opened at the fourth button, which did not reach the computer.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, keyboard: the key table (HID usages → scancodes), the swap of the `@#` and `<>` keys of Apple ISO keyboards and the handling of lock keys are now written once (`Keymap`, shared); the Mac brings its kVK table and the layout reported by the system, the Apple TV its “Apple ISO keyboard” setting. Nothing changes for the user.
- Mac app, updates: the app reads the website's cumulative feed (`www.kybernow.app/updates/appcast.xml`, every release, stable and beta) instead of the latest stable release's feed; new "Receive beta versions" setting in KyberNow › Settings…, a newer stable release always being offered. Earlier apps receive this version through the old feed, still published with each release. `scripts/release.sh` updates and commits the website's feed on each release; `scripts/rebuild-appcast.sh` rebuilds it from the GitHub releases.
- Mac app: a display plugged in, unplugged or changed in resolution during a session is noticed. With “like this display”, a capsule offers to fit the image to the new mode ("The screen changed: fit the image to 2560 × 1440?"), also in the bar; a click recreates the computer's virtual display and restarts the image, nothing changes without it. In full screen on an unplugged display, the session goes back to full screen on the remaining screen.
- Web client: with the patched engine (console 0.2.6 and later), touch devices other than Safari and iOS use WebTransport instead of WebSocket; the engine itself types the device keyboard's text (field marked `data-kynput-text`) and sends the finger's position. With an older engine, and always in Safari and on iPhone and iPad, nothing changes.

## 0.2.6

- Console, updates: the applicator also stops the icon relaunched by the new console before putting the old version back, suspends Kyber's automatic restart during the switch, reaches the console at the address of its listen setting, refuses Windows' reserved file names, and a rollback that does not succeed is shown instead of hidden.
- Console, updates: automatic tests of the signed manifest (including a manifest produced by the release script), of the package extraction and of the switch on a fake installation folder (success, rollback, forged package, going back to the previous version).
- Console, updates: installing (or going back) while a device is in session first asks for confirmation, since its session will be cut.
- Console, updates: “Go back to the previous version” button on the Updates page, as long as the previous version is kept on the computer; same switch and same health check as an update, Kyber's settings kept.
- Console, updates: installing no longer goes through PowerShell. The console launches a copy of itself (`kyber-admin update apply`) that verifies the signed manifest and the package again, extracts it under Program Files (never into a folder another account could change), refuses any escaping path and any link, switches the folders, checks that the new console answers with the right version and puts the old one back otherwise. `update.ps1` remains for manual updates, which only check the package's integrity.
- Console, updates: a package is only installed if its release manifest is signed by KyberNow's key, announces the same number as GitHub and a channel the computer follows, and gives the package's exact size and checksum; otherwise the Updates page says why. New “Channel” setting (Stable, or Beta, which also gets prereleases).
- Computer updates: each release publishes a signed manifest (`kybernow-release.json`, minisign) giving its number, its channel and the checksum of each file; `scripts/sign-manifest.sh` writes and signs it with the release key, kept in the Mac's Keychain.
- Console: a single delay without a heartbeat (20 s) to consider an app's session interrupted, give its virtual display back and stop the full-screen watch (20 s or 30 s depending on the place, before); the full-screen watch is only noted as running if its agent started; the `kyber-admin.log` log is rotated at 5 MB, five files kept.
- Icon: it no longer ignores the console's certificate and only accepts the one whose fingerprint is recorded in `kybernow.json` (`console_fingerprint`, written by `kyber-admin init` and by the console at every start); an account that stops the service and serves something else on the console's port is no longer followed. The panel only creates its WebView2 view at the first click and releases it after 2 minutes without being opened (60 to 100 MB per user permanently, before); closed, nothing is pushed to it.
- Console, web client: the relay to kycontroller (`/session/*`, `/ws`, `/websocket/*`, `/kymux/*`, engine files…) requires the console session instead of being open to anyone reaching its port, refuses another origin, only relays known paths (nothing climbing up), verifies Kyber's certificate by its fingerprint instead of accepting any, caps relayed WebSockets at 8, gives Kyber 15 s to answer and reuses its HTTP connections. On another computer (`KYBERNOW_KYBER_ADDR`), `KYBERNOW_KYBER_FINGERPRINT` gives the fingerprint.
- Server and icon: the local routes (`/api/local/*`: state for the icon, end of session, answer to a pairing request) require, besides the local address (also `::ffff:127.0.0.1`), a token drawn by the console at its start and at every Windows logon, copied into the user's profile by an agent and sent by the icon (`x-kyber-local`): another account on the computer can no longer end the session or accept a device. The state given to the icon no longer carries the pairing codes: the panel points to the console to compare the code, and can refuse.
- Server: Kyber's secrets under Program Files (`kyber_config.toml` with the password hashes, kycontroller's certificate and key, `avant-maj`) are reserved to SYSTEM and Administrators, at installation, at every console start and at every write; the web client's token key leaves `kyber_config.toml` for `C:\ProgramData\KyberNow\kyber-jwt.key` (Kyber reads it with `key = { file = … }`); the web client signs in under the installation account, recorded in `kyber-admin.toml`, no longer under the first account of the list; its token is never cached by the browser.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, image: 4:4:4 is no longer offered in AV1 (Apple's decoders only read 4:2:0: the stream would not open); the Metal shader is no longer compiled at each stream and the decoder probe no longer blocks the launch; two alternating parameter sets no longer rebuild the decoder at each frame; colour matrix and texture cache lightened; on the Mac, an occluded or minimised window stops rendering and lowers the requested bitrate, then resumes them.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: the end-to-end latency no longer drops frames whose server measurements arrive after display (optimistic median without vertical sync); the measurements panel shows the share of measured frames and the rejected measurements; the counters start from zero after a reconnection.
- Mac and Apple TV apps, sound: sound travels unreliably with error correction (a lost packet is concealed by Opus instead of being retransmitted: no more delay building up on Wi-Fi); an empty buffer is filled with a concealment frame rather than silence, playback resumes as soon as the margin is there and the margin goes down five times faster; clock drift is caught up by one sample skipped or doubled out of 256 instead of a hard cut (clicks); AirPods or an HDMI display chosen during the session: the output follows, its latency is read again; 5.1 or 7.1 to headphones: downmix instead of the centre in the right ear; Bluetooth and AirPlay no longer silence the output (4,096 frames per request); 7.1 side and rear channels labelled as Core Audio expects.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: image decoding is asynchronous. Kyber's network thread no longer waits for the decoder (1 to 3 ms per frame, 20 to 60 ms at peaks, during which neither packets nor sound were read). A decoding session lost after sleep or a GPU change is recreated right away, without waiting for the stream restart (2 to 3 s).
- Mac app: in full screen, the image can go directly to the display without macOS's compositor (one refresh less): the Metal layer is the session surface's layer, and the bar, hints, measurements panel and passage leave compositing when they have nothing to show. To check with `MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1`.
- Mac app: the image leaves for the display as soon as it is decoded. The next frame no longer waits until the display has shown the previous one (a whole refresh of latency, 8 ms at 120 Hz, 17 ms at 60 Hz); Core Animation shows the most recent one at the refresh. The old behaviour remains available with `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1`.
- Mac app: image presentation measurements. Launched with `KN_PRESENT_STATS=1`, the app logs every second the frames rendered, shown and discarded by the display, and the commit → screen delay (median, 95th percentile); `KN_INFLIGHT_GUARD=1` puts back the “one frame in flight” guard for comparison. The offline display test compares both.
- Console: against a game that imposes its resolution again or Windows that keeps turning a display back on, the console gives up after a few attempts instead of looping (and making the image restart endlessly); switching to Max or Custom during the session hands the decision back to the console's setting.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: in the Desktop profile, if the computer stays at another resolution despite the console, the app asks for it again by itself (at most every 30 s). Changing profile during the session also changes what the console does.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: after an image restart, the mouse stays in game mode if it was (on the Mac, it stopped responding) and modifier keys still held are sent to the computer again; on the Apple TV, the pointer comes back to its place.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: when the shown display disappears then comes back (cable plugged back in), the image finds it again instead of switching to the first display around, and never asks for a display that no longer exists.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: an image error on the computer no longer ends the session right away: the image is restarted up to three times, and a capsule says so.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: when the computer changes resolution mid-session (a game switching to 1080p), the mouse lands in the right place again; a capsule says so, and in the Desktop profile the app offers to put the device's resolution back.
- Console: automated tests for a mode change and for a physical display turning back on mid-session, for each policy.
- Console: “If a game changes the resolution” setting (Picture and sound): let it be, or put the device's resolution back. The apps choose according to their profile: Game lets the game choose, Desktop puts the display back to the device's size.
- Console: during a session, the virtual display's mode and the active displays are read every second. The apps receive the current mode with their heartbeat; a mode change (a game switching to 1080p) is noted in the event feed; a computer display that turns back on mid-session (cable plugged back in, Windows reapplying its old layout) is turned off again after 2 s.
- Server: the Kyber overlay shipped with KyberNow is rebuilt with these patches: capture and encoding (kyavserver, txproto), controller, input server (`kynput.dll`, `kynputserver.exe`) and the web client's engine.
- Kyber: eleven patches prepared to be proposed to the Kyber team (audio timestamps, refused 5.1 sound, removed display freezing the image, pointer, keyframe on request), in `server/kyber-patches/upstream/`. Nothing has been sent yet.
- Web client: Kyber's engine is rebuilt with KyberNow patches and shipped with the server. It types by itself the text of a field meant for it (accents and composition included, also without WebSocket), keeps the mouse position when Windows hides its pointer without it being locked, releases everything when the tab goes to the background or the pointer lock is lost, ignores gamepads whose layout it does not know, and no longer ends the session on a decoding error if a full frame comes within a second.
- Server: the computer's pointer no longer travels with its shape at every move, only its position; the shape is only sent when it changes or the pointer shows again. Up to 1 MB/s less on each device's connection while the mouse moves, and the rate becomes configurable: `[kyavserver] cursor_move_rate`, 60 per second by default, up to 120. Kyber's input server (`kynput.dll`, `kynputserver.exe`) is now part of the overlay.
- Server: the image send queue becomes a setting, `[kycontroller] datagram_send_queue` (console: System › Advanced › Image send queue). "Deep" (8 MB, the default) keeps the current behaviour; "Short" (3 MB) drops late frames when the network saturates instead of sending them up to two seconds late. To be compared on a busy Wi-Fi.
- Console: a second device that connects takes over with notice. The first one is told at its next heartbeat and disconnects cleanly, then the virtual display is recreated at the second one's size; before, the two devices kept stealing the display from each other and the first one's disconnection cut the second one's display and sound. The session is now recognised by the device rather than by the account.
- Console: five wrong passwords from the same address make it wait 30 s, then twice as long at each attempt; password checks now run at most two at a time.
- Console: when the service stops (update, shutdown), the displays and the sound are given back to the computer right away; an unreadable setting no longer prevents the console from starting; a display or sound that could not be given back is retried at the next start; a display agent that does not answer is stopped; two simultaneous changes to `kyber_config.toml` no longer overwrite each other.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: no more possible crash on disconnection or profile change. The decoders were released after a fixed delay while Kyber could still deliver them a packet; Kyber now signals its last call. The keyframe request no longer holds Kyber's loop when the computer does not answer (Quit no longer freezes), and its retry also fires while a menu is open.
- libavconv capture engine: the pointer follows the mouse again on displays above 60 Hz (it stayed frozen while the mouse moved), and keeps its shape when Kyber's input service restarts.
- Keyframes requested by the apps after a loss: at most one per session every 150 ms, so a network losing many packets does not weigh down the image of every shared screen.
- libavconv capture engine: `audio_layout = "stereo"` setting (`[kyavserver]` section) to send stereo sound even when Windows outputs 5.1 or 7.1; otherwise Kyber's web client, which only plays stereo, stays silent. The sound also announces its packet size, without which a Kyber client with unreliable sound stopped.
- Computer updates without the risk of leaving everything stopped: the new version is prepared next to the old one and then put in place in one go; if the console does not answer within a minute, the old version comes back by itself and the Updates page says why. The manual update also checks the package's checksum, and “Installed apps” shows the right version.
- Shared computer: KyberNow's data folder (`C:\ProgramData\KyberNow`) is now accessible to administrators only. Another account on the computer can no longer read the password hash there or slip a file into an update.
- Installer: the randomly drawn ports also avoid Steam's ranges and read Windows' IPv6 exclusions.
- Installer: on a laptop without a “High performance” power mode, the installation no longer stops at 96%: the mode is recreated, or left as it is with a warning. The option is unticked if the computer already uses it.
- Installer: Alt+F4 no longer closes the window in the middle of an installation, and KyberNow appears in “Installed apps” as soon as the files are copied: an interrupted installation can be uninstalled normally.
- Installer: run again on a computer where KyberNow is already installed, it offers to keep the settings (accounts, paired devices, ports, certificate, console settings) and replaces only the programs and drivers. “Start from scratch” is still possible, with a warning: devices will have to be paired again.
- More complete uninstall: the virtual display driver also leaves Windows' driver store, the power mode and network category used before KyberNow are put back, and the settings can be kept for later.
- Installer: the interface is extracted under Program Files instead of the temporary folder; the end of the installation checks that the server and the console answer; an official Kyber installation whose uninstall command has arguments is removed properly; standard accounts can no longer pause the services.
- Apple TV app: every picture restart (profile change, fallback) left Bluetooth keyboard and mouse observers behind; they are now removed.
- Mac and Apple TV apps: per-computer setting “Manette vue par l'ordinateur” (gamepad seen by the computer): Xbox 360 (default, recognised by every game) or faithful (a Sony gamepad stays a DualShock 4). It overrides the console's “all gamepads as Xbox 360” option, which only remains a default for other clients.

On the Apple TV, a stick only sends the axis that moved, in 4 ms slices: a DualSense over Bluetooth sent up to 1,500 packets per second.

- Mac and Apple TV apps: when the bar or the menu opens, the gamepad is reset on the computer (buttons released, sticks and triggers at rest). A held trigger no longer stays pressed in the game while the menu is open; on the Apple TV, the gamepad as mouse also releases its clicks and keys, and a stick still held when the menu closes picks up right away.

- Mac app: after ⌘Tab, ⌘Space or a trip to another app, the ⌘ key (sent as Ctrl) no longer stays pressed on the computer. Everything is released when the window loses the keyboard, and the modifiers really held are sent again when it gets it back.
- Web client: a click in the session bar (Quit, Sound, Keys…) or on a touch button no longer also clicks on the computer, under the bar.
- Web client on phones and tablets: text typed on the device's keyboard arrives correctly on a computer with an AZERTY layout, with capitals, accents and emoji, and a keyboard attached to the iPad no longer doubles letters. - Web client: the page no longer wrongly switches from WebTransport to WebSocket while the display is being prepared, and closes the old connection before opening the new one (no more doubled inputs). “Kyber is not responding” and “the image does not arrive” are no longer confused.
